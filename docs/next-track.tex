\begin{abstract}
This document is the living synthesis of the \emph{next-track} sequence
recommendation campaigns; the markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/}
(filenames containing \texttt{nexttrack}) are the primary record. The task is
next-item \emph{retrieval}: given a listening \emph{session} (an ordered
sequence of tracks), rank the catalog and recover the next distinct track the
user actually played, scored on a single leak-free chronological
\emph{next-distinct} split (\num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items;
\num{5723} train / \num{1431} test; cold-item rate \num{0.55}). The headline
metric is Recall@10, reported beside mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and the
graded-relevance diagnostics artist@10 / genre@10 / artist\_mrr. Twelve campaigns
are consolidated. A session GRU alone only \emph{ties} the first-order Markov
co-listening bar (R@10 \num{0.1069}) on Recall@10 and loses on MRR, and neither
loss-tuning nor any network topology (depth, width, residual, direction) beats
it. The breakthrough is \emph{rank fusion}: an R+M z-blend (GRU $\times$ Markov)
beats the bar by $\sim$$+61\%$ Recall@10 (\num{0.1726}, three-seed confirmed),
because the two legs rank \emph{different} correct tracks; a learned XGB stacker
underperforms the fixed z-blend and adding redundant neural legs does not help.
The one genuinely additive new signal is \emph{content}: an item-representation
sweep found latent \emph{dimension} (not the compressor) to be the lever, with a
peak at PCA-192, and on that rich space a three-leg R+M+C z-blend
(GRU $\times$ Markov $\times$ content-kNN) is the current best-on-record row at
Recall@10 \num{0.1859}, MRR \num{0.1025}. A subsequent literature-fit campaign
then superseded that crown: a supervised \emph{learned content projection}
(\Rp+M+\Cp), which fits a linear metric map of the PCA-192 item space toward
next-track adjacency by InfoNCE and retrieves the GRU and content legs in it,
reaches Recall@10 \num{0.2117}, MRR \num{0.1220} (paired $\Delta$ over R+M+C
$+\num{0.0259}$, CI above zero), is \emph{seed-robust} across three seeds and
\emph{cold-reaching}, and hardens on a second disjoint 0.64-cold split (paired
$\Delta$ $+\num{0.0426}$, CI above zero) --- resolving the two thin caveats
(determinism-only confirm, $+\num{0.0007}$ margin) that had bounded the R+M+C
crown. Unlike every earlier ranking predictor it is registered, promoted and
\emph{servable} (\texttt{blend-gru-markov-content-proj}, best-models rank 1),
closing the productization gap; an item-vector representation exploration and a
continuous ``sounds-alike'' \emph{music@10} metric corroborate the champion
without dethroning it. A final measurement campaign adds a display-only
\emph{session-holisticness} metric suite (artist\_adj@10 / mood\_coh@10 /
ild@10) that fingerprints the champion as the \emph{most} artist-eager model
measured (artist\_adj@10 \num{0.610}) --- with the eagerness sourced from its
Markov bigram leg, not the RNN, and the GRU and LSTM statistically tied --- and
finds an eval-time MMR re-rank ($\lambda\approx\num{0.9}$) a near-free
holisticness lever while a training-time anti-eager regularizer is weak; nothing
is promoted and the primary metric stays Recall@10. Two final architecture
campaigns then close the remaining architecture axes, and both are nulls: a
jointly-trained \emph{dual-tower} GRU over paired causal views of the prefix
fails to beat a parameter-matched single tower --- the fourth independent
learned-combiner null, and the one that answers the ``train the fusion
end-to-end'' rebuttal --- and a learned per-step \emph{pre-encoder} in front of
the recurrence is not merely useless but actively \emph{harmful} in a single
tower ($-\num{0.0140}$ Recall@10, CI below zero), a loss traced by a
counterfactual interpretability read to half-wave rectification of a
zero-centred latent deleting exactly its negative half
(\texttt{frac\_exact\_zero} \num{0.5327} $=$ \texttt{frac\_negative\_coords}
\num{0.5327}). A measured power analysis finally reframes all five architecture
nulls: at $n=\num{1431}$ the paired-bootstrap CI half-width is \num{0.0124} on
Recall@10 while a whole architecture family spans only \num{0.028}, so large
harms were detected reliably while moderate wins were never detectable at all ---
and \emph{holisticness@10} exists in three mutually non-comparable code
generations, which is why this synthesis keeps its board denominated in
Recall@10. A final measurement campaign then changes the
\emph{surface} rather than the model: the shipped product serves an
\emph{autoregressive walk}, while holisticness@10 scores a one-shot top-10 whose
\texttt{mood\_coh} anchors to a \emph{growing} prefix centroid, so journey drift
was invisible by construction (and the composite is $\num{5.5}\times$ more
sensitive to its relevance factor than to its eagerness factor). Four
walk-level metrics calibrated against the user's own listening (\num{73632} real
consecutive transitions, median step cosine \num{0.261}, \SI{27.0}{\percent} of
them negative) show the shipped engine walking $\num{2.5}\times$ tighter than
the listener it imitates (median step \num{0.661}), and three deterministic
retrieval-time controls --- a seed anchor, a stride term, and a hard per-artist
cap that \emph{cannot be outscored} (two disjoint splits:
$\Delta$artist\_conc $-\num{0.527}$ with $\Delta$music@10 $+\num{0.026}$, both
CI clear of zero) --- make a real-listening stride and a held vibe
simultaneously reachable. On \num{40} identical held-out sessions under paired
bootstrap the \emph{dual-tower} family that three campaigns had refuted on
recall is the winner on the stated objective (\texttt{latent/cummean}: stride
error $-\num{0.2545}$, genres $+\num{1.50}$, drift $+\num{0.0351}$, all CI
clear of zero, vibe \emph{held}), while pushing the single GRU or the champion
to the same stride \emph{costs} vibe --- but \texttt{ground} straddles zero for
every arm, so only journey \emph{shape} is established, and the primary metric
is unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
This document is the living synthesis of the next-track sequence-recommendation
campaigns; the markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/} are the primary
record. Each section transcribes (never recomputes) the quantitative results of
a campaign, preserves its run and dataset identifiers verbatim for
traceability, and adds the rationale and interpretation the terse reports omit.
This instance was cloned from a sibling taste-fit / rotation lab and retargeted
to a sequence ranking goal; the rotation campaigns are a separate line of work
and are \emph{not} covered here.

\paragraph{Task and split.} The lab predicts, within a listening
\emph{session}, which track the user plays \emph{next} --- a next-item
retrieval task. The model consumes the ordered session prefix and scores every
catalog item; candidates are ranked and the held-out next \emph{distinct} track
is the target (a leave-last-out evaluation that excludes prefix items, matching
the Markov parity convention). All work runs on one sequence artifact,
\dsid{seq-20260713-014226} (\num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items), split
\emph{chronologically by session start} into \num{5723} train / \num{1431} test
at cut 2024-08-24. The defining property of this split is its \textbf{cold-item
rate of \num{0.55}}: more than half of test-session target tracks never appear
in any training session. This is the leak-free forward reality, and it caps
absolute recall by construction --- every result is read against the incumbent
baseline bar, never an absolute number.

\paragraph{Metrics.} The headline metric is \textbf{Recall@10} (R@10, the
fraction of test sessions whose held-out next track appears in the top ten
retrieved), reported beside \textbf{mean reciprocal rank} (MRR, the average
inverse rank of the true next track) and Recall@20. Since 2026-07-14 the
evaluation also emits first-class \emph{graded-relevance} metrics over the same
prefix-excluded ranking (Section~\ref{sec:graded}): \textbf{artist@$k$} (a
top-$k$ candidate shares the true next track's artist), \textbf{genre@$k$}
(likewise on primary genre), and \textbf{artist\_mrr} (MRR of the first
same-artist candidate). All confidence intervals are bootstrap 95\% CIs over
\num{2000} resamples of the \num{1431} test sessions, and the decisive
statistic is the \emph{paired} per-session $\Delta$ against a named reference.
A board \emph{win} requires beating the reference on \emph{both} R@10 and MRR
beyond non-overlapping marginal CIs (the strict bar), then a reproduction
confirm; the paired-$\Delta$ CI-entirely-above-zero test is the more powerful
alternative statistic (it crowned both champions) and can disagree with the
strict bar. The graded metrics are the honest scorecard: exact recall is modest
by construction, but the champion lands the right \emph{artist} in the top ten
$\sim$32\% of the time and the right \emph{genre} $\sim$40\% --- far more useful
than the $\sim$17\% exact number the cold-item rate imposes. Since 2026-07-20 the
leaderboard also carries \textbf{music@10}, a continuous ``sounds-alike'' graded
metric (Section~\ref{sec:music}): the mean best cosine, in a balanced intrinsic
musical-distance space, between the true next track and the top-ten retrieved
candidates --- a smooth superset of Recall@10 that credits landing something that
\emph{sounds like} the truth on an exact miss. It is display-only; the primary
metric stays Recall@10.

\paragraph{The standing champion.} The current best-on-record \emph{row} ---
and, since 2026-07-18, a \emph{registered, promoted and servable} model --- is a
supervised \emph{learned content projection}, \emph{\Rp+M+\Cp}: a session GRU
$\times$ a first-order Markov co-occurrence model $\times$ a content-kNN leg,
where the GRU (\Rp) and the content leg (\Cp) retrieve in an InfoNCE metric map
of the PCA-192 item space fit toward next-track adjacency, at R@10 \num{0.2117},
MRR \num{0.1220} (Sections~\ref{sec:projection}--\ref{sec:projection-scan}). It
supersedes the three-leg \emph{R+M+C} z-blend on the same PCA-192 space (R@10
\num{0.1859}; Section~\ref{sec:phase2}), which had itself dethroned the two-leg
\emph{R+M} z-blend on the 64-dim song-autoencoder space (AE-64, R@10
\num{0.1726}; Section~\ref{sec:blend}). The projection is \emph{seed-robust} and
\emph{two-split hardened}, and it is deployable via
\texttt{POST /api/models/blend-gru-markov-content-proj/predict} --- closing the
productization gap the earlier off-server fusion drivers left open. The prior
champions remain useful references (R+M is still the leanest two-leg blend), but
they are no longer the deployable anchor.

\section{Background: the sequence task, the baselines, and the metrics}
\label{sec:background}
This section gives the minimum theory for a reader who knows machine learning
but not this repo. It is keyed to the project's living facts file and grows only
when a new predictor family or metric enters the reports.

\paragraph{The models and baselines in play.}
\begin{description}
  \item[Session GRU / LSTM (\texttt{seq-nexttrack}).] A recurrent neural network
  that reads the ordered session prefix one item at a time (each item
  represented by its item-latent vector) and emits a predicted next-item latent;
  candidates are ranked by cosine similarity to that prediction. The GRU (gated
  recurrent unit) and LSTM (long short-term memory) are the two recurrent cells
  tried. The recurrence captures \emph{session context} --- the trajectory of
  the listening session --- rather than a single previous item.
  \item[Training loss: InfoNCE versus cosine.] The sequence model can be trained
  to regress the next-item latent by \emph{cosine} loss (pull the prediction
  toward the true next latent) or by \textbf{InfoNCE}, a contrastive objective
  that pulls the prediction toward the true next item and pushes it away from a
  batch of negatives. InfoNCE is the single most important lever in the whole
  arc: it is $\approx 2.3\times$ cosine on Recall@10, because ranking retrieval
  rewards a representation that \emph{separates} the true next item from
  distractors, which a contrastive loss optimizes directly and a pointwise
  regression does not.
  \item[First-order Markov co-occurrence (the bar, \texttt{B2}).] A train-only
  transition model: a \texttt{track\_bigram} counting ``what track follows what''
  with artist/genre back-off for unseen pairs (the transition term the sibling
  app already ships). It directly \emph{memorizes} adjacency, which is exactly
  what exact next-track ranking rewards, and it is the reference bar every arm is
  measured against. A second-order Markov (trigram) back-off was also tried and
  is essentially neutral --- sessions are too short/sparse for a trigram to fire,
  so it falls through to the bigram.
  \item[item2vec.] A skip-gram (SGNS) co-listening embedding trained on the
  \emph{train} sessions only (a leak-safe analogue of word2vec, where ``words''
  are tracks and ``sentences'' are sessions); candidates are ranked by cosine to
  the mean-pooled prefix. On \num{5723} train sessions it is too sparse to
  localize and is the weakest leg on record; the full-history co-listening
  vectors are deliberately \emph{not} used because they leak across the split.
  \item[content-kNN.] A training-free retrieval leg: cosine of each candidate's
  item latent to the prefix latents (max-aggregated). It is weak standalone on
  exact recall but captures \emph{vibe} similarity the counts miss (its genre@10
  already edges the Markov bar), and it is the one genuinely additive third leg
  for the fusion blend.
  \item[Rank-fusion z-blend versus a learned stacker.] Two ways to combine legs.
  The \textbf{z-blend} normalizes each leg's candidate scores (z-score) and takes
  a weighted sum ($\alpha\cdot z(\text{leg})+\dots$) --- a fixed, parameter-light
  fuser. The \textbf{XGB stacker} is a gradient-boosted meta-learner over
  per-candidate features (leg scores $+$ popularity $+$ recency $+$
  artist/genre-match), trained pointwise with sampled negatives. Across two
  campaigns the fixed z-blend \emph{beats} the learned stacker decisively,
  because the stacker's pointwise binary training does not optimize the ranking
  objective as well as directly fusing normalized scores.
  \item[Learned content projection (supervised metric map).] A linear map $W$ of
  the item-latent space fit by \textbf{InfoNCE} over the train consecutive pairs
  $(u\!\to\!v)$ (self-loops dropped, Markov convention), so cosine in $W$-space
  reflects ``likely to follow.'' It is leak-free (train pairs $+$ intrinsic
  content latents only, never the split). Both the content-kNN leg (\Cp) and the
  GRU (\Rp) then retrieve in the projected space; Markov is unchanged. It is the
  one \emph{supervised} alternative to the unsupervised, variance-ordered PCA
  geometry, and --- because content is the only channel with cold reach --- it
  lifts \emph{both} legs that share the space, making it the current champion
  (Section~\ref{sec:projection}).
  \item[Dual-tower recurrent fusion (\texttt{seq-dualgru}).] Two
  \emph{independent} recurrent towers (separate weights) read the prefix in
  parallel over configurable strictly-causal \emph{views} of it ---
  \texttt{latent} (the raw item vector), \texttt{delta} (the step-to-step
  difference) or \texttt{cummean} (the running session centroid) --- and their
  concatenated per-step hidden states are read out by a fusion head
  (\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$ for a pure linear read, ${}=1$ for a 256-wide
  MLP). It reuses the \texttt{seq-nexttrack} objective, batching, early stopping
  and evaluation verbatim, so it isolates architecture as the only variable. The
  motivating idea is that two towers should help only if they see genuinely
  different information; Section~\ref{sec:dualtower} tests that and refutes it.
  \item[Per-step pre-encoder.] A nonlinear re-embedding placed \emph{in front of}
  the recurrence --- $[\,\text{Linear},\ \text{ReLU},\ \text{Dropout}\,]$
  applied at each timestep, exposed as
  \texttt{pre\_hidden}\,/\,\texttt{pre\_layers}. It is an unusually sharp test
  object: a GRU's own input transform is \emph{already} linear, so a linear
  pre-encoder at width $\ge$ the input dimension is exactly as expressive as no
  pre-encoder at all, and the \emph{rectifier} is the only change --- which is
  what makes Section~\ref{sec:preencoder}'s null mechanistically attributable
  rather than merely negative.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Item-latent spaces: song-AE versus PCA, and the wrong-collection
trap.} The sequence model predicts \emph{into}, and retrieves \emph{over}, a
per-item latent space, so the choice of that space is a first-class axis. Three
families appear:
\begin{description}
  \item[AE-64 (\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_song\_ae}).] A 64-dim nonlinear
  \emph{song-autoencoder} latent, the original champion item space. An
  autoencoder compresses each item by reconstructing it through a bottleneck;
  its equal-weight-per-block reconstruction loss spreads capacity evenly across
  content, acoustic and catalog blocks.
  \item[PCA-$k$ (\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_song\_pca$k$}).] A \emph{linear} principal
  components compression at dim $k \in \{32,64,128,192,256\}$, which orders
  dimensions by explained variance and decorrelates them. As
  Sections~\ref{sec:compression}--\ref{sec:dim} show, this variance-ordered,
  decorrelated geometry is \emph{easier} for the GRU to regress into than the
  AE's entangled equal-weight latents, so PCA beats the AE at every dim $\ge 64$.
  \item[The wrong-collection trap.] Both of the above are fit \emph{only} from
  the leak-safe \dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content} source (intrinsic
  content/acoustic/catalog fields; no behavioral or target field). The default
  \dsid{spotify\_tracks} 200-dim collection is a \emph{behavioral} co-listening
  embedding that soft-leaks replay behavior and must \textbf{never} be used as an
  item space.
\end{description}

\section{The sequence sweep: a GRU-infonce ties the Markov bar}
\label{sec:sweep}
\emph{Rationale.} The instance's opening campaign asked the founding question:
does a session-context sequence model --- a GRU/LSTM over the ordered prefix,
predicting the next track's 64-dim song-AE latent and retrieving by cosine ---
beat the first-order Markov transition model the sibling app already ships,
\emph{forward}, on the leak-free session split? Four arms crossed the recurrent
cell (GRU, LSTM), capacity (\texttt{hidden} 128, 256) and, decisively, the
training loss (cosine versus InfoNCE), at 60 epochs.

\emph{Results.} No arm beat the bar. The best arm (M3, GRU \texttt{hidden}128
InfoNCE) reached a statistical \emph{tie} with the Markov bar on R@10
(\num{0.0894}, paired-$\Delta$ CI includes zero) but \emph{lost} on MRR
(\num{0.0388} versus \num{0.0694}) and Recall@20. The dominant lever was the
loss objective: InfoNCE was $\approx 2.3\times$ cosine on R@10 (\num{0.0894}
versus \num{0.0391}), while capacity (\texttt{h256}) and the LSTM cell barely
moved the cosine arms (all $\sim$0.038--0.041). The popularity and recency
floors sat near zero (R@10 \num{0.0007}).

\emph{Interpretation.} A latent-plus-single-cosine-hop is a harder route to the
\emph{exact} next track than a co-listening bigram: the bigram directly
memorizes ``what follows what,'' while the GRU must land near the right point in
a 64-d content space and then retrieve, losing on precise ranking (MRR). The
finding is consistent with a taste-drift reality --- \num{0.55} cold items,
identity/adjacency-driven behavior --- and it set the agenda: InfoNCE is the
base loss, and the natural next arm is a \emph{blend} of the GRU's candidate
scores with the Markov bigram.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-sequence-sweep.md}.}

\section{Topology scan: depth, width and direction are a spent lever}
\label{sec:topology}
\emph{Rationale.} With the loss fixed at InfoNCE, this scan isolated network
\emph{topology}: does depth (stacked layers), width (\texttt{hidden} 256), an
inter-layer residual stack or bidirectionality lift the sequence model over the
Markov bar? Because a bidirectional RNN under per-step teacher forcing would leak
future items, the bidirectional arm used a leak-free last-item objective (encode
the full prefix, predict only the held-out next item).

\emph{Results.} Refuted. No arm beat the bar on both metrics. The best arm
(width \texttt{h256}, R@10 \num{0.0957}) is a \emph{tie} on R@10 and loses on
MRR (\num{0.0411} versus \num{0.0694}). Depth is flat 1$\to$2 layers
(\num{0.0894}$\to$\num{0.0922}, in-band) and \emph{hurts} at 3 layers
(\num{0.0825}, out-of-band below the bar); the residual stack over depth-2/h256
added nothing. The bidirectional arm collapsed (\num{0.0482}), but this is an
\emph{objective} artifact, not a directionality verdict: the leak-free last-item
objective supplies $\sim$1 training target per session versus teacher forcing's
$\sim$13 next-step pairs, so it trained on $\sim$13$\times$ less signal.

\emph{Interpretation.} Architecture is not the lever for next-track on this
corpus. The transferable latent-retrieval signal caps at R@10 $\sim$0.09--0.10
across depth, width, residual and direction, below the co-listening bigram ---
consistent with the whole arc that the loss objective moved the needle and
capacity/topology do not. The one remaining unexplored lever was the candidate
framing / fusion, which the next campaign pulled decisively.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-topology-scan.md}.}

\section{The GRU$\times$Markov fusion champion, and why more ensembling fails}
\label{sec:blend}
\emph{Rationale.} The sweep and the topology scan agreed that the best single
GRU only \emph{ties} the Markov bar on R@10 while losing on MRR. This campaign
asked whether \emph{combining} models beats the bar, and how far ensembling
goes. Three legs were defined, each emitting full-vocab candidate scores:
\textbf{M} = the train-only Markov bigram (the bar), \textbf{R} = the
GRU-infonce-h256 recurrence, and \textbf{A} = an ANN bag-of-prefix MLP (a
feed-forward twin of R). They were combined by a weighted z-blend and, as a
learned alternative, an XGB stacker.

\emph{Results.} The \textbf{R+M z-blend} ($\alpha{=}0.5$) \emph{beats the bar
decisively}: R@10 \num{0.1726} $[0.154, 0.192]$, MRR \num{0.0963}, a paired
$\Delta$ over Markov of $+\num{0.0657}$ $[+0.049, +0.083]$ (CI entirely above
zero) --- $\sim$$+61\%$ R@10, $+39\%$ MRR --- on a robust plateau across
$\alpha \in [0.25, 0.75]$ and reproduced across three seeds (\num{0.1705} /
\num{0.1726} / \num{0.1726}). It was registered as the \texttt{seq-blend}
predictor and the \texttt{blend-gru-markov} definition, the first next-track
champion. Further ensembling then \emph{failed} to improve on it
(Table~\ref{tab:blend}): R+A+M (\num{0.1684}) is slightly worse, R+A ($\approx$
GRU alone, \num{0.0894}) is much worse, and both XGB stacker variants (M+R
\num{0.1020}; M+R+A \num{0.0887}) barely clear Markov alone.

\emph{underperforms} the simple z-blend, counter to the usual ``stacking beats
blending'' intuition: its pointwise binary-with-negatives training does not
optimize the ranking objective as well as directly fusing normalized scores. The
remaining headroom is bounded by the \num{0.55} cold-item rate and requires
\emph{genuinely new signal}, not more model fusion --- the hypothesis the next
campaign tests.

\section{Graded-relevance metrics and new base learners: content is the only additive signal}
\label{sec:graded}
\emph{Rationale.} Two threads in one campaign. (A) Make the evaluation credit
landing the right \emph{band/vibe} even when the exact track is wrong --- the
observation that ``the first choice is usually not the exact track, but the
artist is usually right'' --- by promoting \emph{artist@k}, \emph{genre@k} and
\emph{artist\_mrr} to first-class metrics wired end-to-end
(\texttt{seq\_common.eval\_from\_scores} $\to$ Rust \texttt{Metrics} /
\texttt{MetricsSpec} $\to$ \texttt{domain.toml} $\to$ UI). (B) Try genuinely new
leak-free base learners --- \textbf{C} = content-kNN, \textbf{M2} = a
second-order Markov trigram with back-off, and \textbf{I} = a train-only
item2vec SGNS --- scored on both the exact and the new graded metrics.

\emph{Results.} The exact-metric path was byte-identical (purely additive keys);
the champion reproduced R@10 \num{0.1726} / MRR \num{0.0963} exactly. No new base
learner beat the bar standalone (M2 \num{0.1076} $\approx$ M \num{0.1069} --- the
trigram falls through to the bigram; item2vec \num{0.0517} is the weakest leg).
Blended into the champion, \textbf{content is the only additive signal}:
\textbf{R+M+C} (adding the content leg) is the top blend on MRR
(\num{0.0963}$\to$\num{0.1014}), artist@10 (\num{0.3215}$\to$\num{0.3298}) and
artist\_mrr, and ties the champion on exact R@10 (paired $\Delta$
$+\num{0.0014}$, CI straddles zero). Item2vec \emph{drags} the blend (R+M+I
\num{0.1349}); the trigram is neutral.

\emph{Interpretation.} Content-kNN ranks \emph{different} near-correct tracks
(latent vibe similarity) than the GRU (session recurrence) and Markov
(co-occurrence), exactly the diversity the fusion campaign predicted new signal
would need. On the AE-64 space the content leg only \emph{tied} the champion on
exact recall while lifting the graded metrics, so it was carried forward as the
graded-preferred third leg, not a new exact champion. That ``tie'' becomes a
\emph{win} once the item space is richer (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}): on PCA-192
the same R+M+C construction is the strongest on record. Co-listening item2vec and
the trigram Markov added nothing on this data.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-14-nexttrack-graded-relevance-and-new-legs.md}.}

\section{The item-representation compression bake-off}
\label{sec:compression}
\emph{Rationale.} The champion's item space --- a 64-dim AE latent --- was one
arbitrary early choice, and the sequence GRU predicts \emph{into} and retrieves
\emph{over} whatever geometry that space imposes. This campaign asks which
\emph{compression} is the best space for the GRU: linear PCA versus the
nonlinear autoencoder, each at latent dim 32 / 64 / 128. Six alternative spaces
were baked from the \emph{same} leak-safe 384-dim content source
(\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content}), each fit unsupervised over all \num{23529}
items using only intrinsic content/acoustic/catalog fields, then scored under a
\emph{frozen} GRU readout (\texttt{gru-infonce-h256}), so the item latent space
is the only axis that moves. The design bet was that the AE's
equal-weight-per-block reconstruction loss would \emph{preserve} the small
acoustic/catalog blocks that PCA's text-dominated global variance would starve;
the null was that dim, not the compressor, sets the ceiling.

\emph{Results.} The parity gate passed: the AE-64 control reproduced its
on-record R@10 \num{0.0957}. All six spaces trained cleanly
(Table~\ref{tab:compression}; Figure~\ref{fig:compression}). \textbf{PCA-128} is
the single-GRU winner at R@10 \num{0.1146}, a paired $\Delta$ of $+\num{0.0189}$
$[+0.0042, +0.0335]$ over the AE-64 control --- a CI \emph{entirely above zero},
the only arm that clears the control significantly --- and it also tops MRR,
artist@10 and artist\_mrr. PCA-64 is the runner-up (\num{0.1041}, tie). The two
32-dim spaces sit far below the 64-dim control (both CIs below zero). The
training-free content-kNN probe is flat at $\sim$0.06 across all six spaces and
does \emph{not} track the GRU ranking.

caution is the blend result: a clearly stronger GRU leg bought \emph{nothing} at
the blend, because the Markov leg is representation-independent and the extra
tracks the better GRU recovers overlap what Markov already supplies.

\section{The dimension ceiling: PCA peaks at 192}
\label{sec:dim}
\emph{Rationale.} With dimension established as the lever and PCA still climbing
at 128, this leg extended the PCA sweep upward (PCA-192, PCA-256) to locate the
real optimum before Phase 2 committed a base space, and added AE-192 to test
whether PCA $\ge$ AE holds at higher dimension. The protocol was identical (same
frozen \texttt{gru-infonce-h256} readout, same parity gate); the reference is
PCA-128 (\runid{run-20260715-042100-a1d9d}).

\emph{Results.} The PCA R@10-versus-dimension curve \textbf{peaks at $\sim$192}
(R@10 \num{0.1230}) and \emph{turns over} by 256 (\num{0.1167}); it was not still
climbing. The full trend is $32\!\to\!\num{0.041}$, $64\!\to\!\num{0.104}$,
$128\!\to\!\num{0.115}$, $\mathbf{192\!\to\!\num{0.123}}$, $256\!\to\!\num{0.117}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:dim}). PCA-192 is the point-optimum, but its paired $\Delta$
versus PCA-128 is $+\num{0.0084}$ $[-0.0007, +0.0189]$ --- it misses the ``CI
entirely above zero'' bar by \num{0.0007}, a whisker --- so \textbf{PCA-128
stands as the Phase-2 base}, with PCA-192 flagged as the confirm-and-promote
candidate. PCA $\ge$ AE holds strongly and the gap is widest at 192 (PCA-192
\num{0.123} versus AE-192 \num{0.098}); the AE plateaus flat at $\sim$0.09--0.10
from dim 64 upward.

\emph{Interpretation.} Extra variance past $\sim$192 (PCA-256 captures cumulative
explained-variance ratio \num{0.997}) is low-signal / noise dimensions the GRU
cannot exploit and that slightly dilute the target geometry. The AE's flat
plateau reconfirms the refuted acoustic-preservation hypothesis at higher
dimension: its equal-weight reconstruction objective keeps spending capacity on
the tiny numeric/acoustic blocks (reconstruction $R^2 \approx 0.999$ at 192)
rather than the high-variance text/catalog structure the GRU needs. As before,
the training-free content-kNN probe stays flat and does not reproduce the GRU's
peak, so the dimension optimum is about target \emph{learnability}, not intrinsic
retrieval richness.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-15b-nexttrack-compression-dim-extension.md}.}

\section{The model-family sweep and the new champion, R+M+C on PCA-192}
\label{sec:phase2}
\emph{Rationale.} With the compression axis settled (PCA-128 base, PCA-192
candidate), this campaign swept the model \emph{family} on the chosen spaces:
does a different recurrent cell (LSTM), a non-recurrent pooled MLP (ANN), or a
\emph{learned} XGB stacker beat the fixed GRU$\times$Markov z-blend --- and does
adding the content-kNN third leg (R+M+C) on the \emph{rich} PCA space finally
beat the champion? A frozen held config ran across all arms; only the model
family and the representation varied, and the server was serialized
(\texttt{-{}-max-runs 1}) to avoid a thread-oversubscription throughput trap.

\emph{Results.} A \textbf{new best-on-record row}: the \textbf{R+M+C z-blend on
PCA-192} (GRU $\times$ train-only Markov $\times$ content-kNN, equal thirds)
reaches R@10 \num{0.1859} $[0.166, 0.207]$, MRR \num{0.1025}, a paired $\Delta$
over the prior champion of $+\num{0.0133}$ $[+0.0007, +0.0259]$ --- entirely
above zero, with no MRR regression (Table~\ref{tab:phase2};
Figure~\ref{fig:phase2}). The content leg is genuinely additive on the rich
space, beating same-space R+M by $+\num{0.0154}$ (PCA-128) / $+\num{0.0147}$
(PCA-192), both CIs above zero --- \emph{stronger} than the AE-64 R+M+C that
only tied (Section~\ref{sec:graded}), because the richer, higher-EVR space makes
the content leg pay off. The learned \textbf{XGB stacker badly underperforms} the
fixed z-blend (\num{0.080}--\num{0.087}, losing even to its own single GRU base
leg); the \textbf{LSTM is the best single model} (PCA-128 \num{0.1272}, paired
$\Delta$ over the GRU $+\num{0.0126}$, CI above zero) but far below the blends;
and the Phase-2 single base \textbf{stays PCA-128}.

\section{The learned content projection: a new champion from the literature-fit campaign}
\label{sec:projection}
\emph{Rationale.} The R+M+C champion (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}) carried two thin
caveats --- a determinism-only reproduction confirm and a paired-$\Delta$ lower
bound of only $+\num{0.0007}$ --- and its headroom was capped by the \num{0.55}
cold-item rate, which only \emph{new} signal can attack. Guided by a survey of the
established session-recommendation literature (the Jannach--Ludewig session-kNN
line, sequential rules / factorized Markov, GRU4Rec training tricks, rank fusion
and learning-to-rank, content / cold-start-aware session rec), this campaign
pre-registered three tiers of classic methods and asked which actually improve the
fit to \emph{this} problem --- a single-user, session-based, next-distinct
\emph{retrieval} task with 55\% cold targets. All arms were measured off-server on
the PCA-192 champion artifact \dsid{seq-20260715-131139}, reusing the sanctioned
driver that reproduces the champion (R+M+C \num{0.1859}) exactly; the decision
statistic is the paired per-session $\Delta$ against that champion.

\emph{Results --- the one win.} A supervised \textbf{learned content projection}
is a new best-on-record row. Fitting a linear metric map $W$ of the PCA-192
content space toward next-track adjacency (InfoNCE over train consecutive pairs,
self-loops dropped) and then retrieving \emph{both} the content leg (\Cp) and the
GRU (\Rp) in that projected space gives \textbf{\Rp+M+\Cp z-blend R@10
\num{0.2117} $[0.191, 0.233]$, MRR \num{0.1220}} --- a paired $\Delta$ over the
champion of $+\num{0.0259}$ $[+0.012, +0.041]$, entirely above zero
(Table~\ref{tab:projection}). Unlike the R+M+C crown it is \textbf{seed-robust}
(three seeds; paired $\Delta$ $+\num{0.026}$ / $+\num{0.025}$ / $+\num{0.029}$,
every CI $>0$ --- a genuine seed-varied confirm, since the projection has random
init and shuffled batches) and \textbf{cold-reaching}: the projected content leg
\Cp\ scores cold targets \num{0.152} $\approx$ warm \num{0.151}, versus Markov's
warm \num{0.226} / cold \num{0.010}, so it reaches never-seen items as well as
seen ones --- genuine content generalization, the one channel that reaches the
55\% cold majority. The symmetric single map beats the directional two-tower, and
rank-192 $=$ rank-64 (the gain is the supervision, not the capacity). As a side
effect the projected single GRU \Rp\ ($\sim$\num{0.176}) nearly matches the
\emph{old} champion blend and crushes the prior best single (LSTM \num{0.127}).

\section{Item-vector representation: is the geometry the bottleneck?}
\label{sec:representation}
\emph{Rationale.} The compression axis (Sections~\ref{sec:compression}--\ref{sec:dim})
found latent \emph{dimension} to dominate but never asked whether the
\emph{composition} of the 192-d item vector --- the equal-weighted 4-block PCA
(text / numeric / acoustic / categorical) --- is itself a handicap. An
explained-variance-ratio (EVR) preflight said it might be: the raw input variance
budget is 44\% acoustic $+$ 43\% numeric versus only 2.5\% text (text is
L2-normalized, so its per-dimension scale is tiny), so full-matrix PCA-192 spends
\textbf{87\% of its variance budget} reconstructing the numeric $+$ acoustic
blocks to $R^2 = \num{1.000}$ while the signal-carrying text block reaches only
$R^2 = \num{0.717}$; per-column standardize (whitening) flips the captured budget
to 73\% text / 21\% categorical (text $R^2$ \num{0.905})
(Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-evr-budget}). Eight alternative item-latent spaces
(whitening $\times$ acoustic-drop, text-only, text$+$categorical, and the
musical-distance metric geometry) were built parity-gated to the champion split
(vocab / split byte-identical; only \texttt{item\_latents.f32} differs) and each
evaluated under \emph{two} readouts: \textbf{(A)} the champion projection
\texttt{blend-gru-markov-content-proj} (the InfoNCE map re-fits on that space ---
the space's ceiling under the winning recipe) and \textbf{(C)} the frozen
\texttt{gru-infonce-h256} single (raw representation quality). Validation-only;
nothing promoted.

immovable --- which is precisely the axis the supervised projection owns.

handicap for a \emph{raw} model, and the learned projection is exactly the right
response to it --- this campaign independently validates \emph{why} the projection

free lunch left in re-basing the input geometry. The one caution: dropping
numeric$+$acoustic \emph{entirely} (V4 textcat) hurts the champion even though it
helps the raw GRU, so the numeric block carries something the projection uses ---
keep numeric, whiten / drop only acoustic. The most promising open direction is
composing the two fixes: fitting the projection \emph{on} a whitened / std-noaco /
metric space to see whether it reaches further or is redundant.

\section{The music@10 metric: the recall champion is not the musically-closest}
\label{sec:music}
\emph{Rationale.} Exact Recall@10 is unforgiving on a 55\%-cold single-user corpus,
and the artist@10 / genre@10 diagnostics are coarse category hits. This campaign
introduces a \emph{continuous} ``sounds-alike'' graded metric, \textbf{music@10},
and back-fills it across every next-track run. It is defined as the mean over test
cases of the best (max) cosine, in a \emph{balanced} intrinsic musical-distance
space (\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content\_metric}: a block-weighted, partial-whitened
fusion of the whitened 64-d song-AE latent, the 384-d name/tag text embedding, five
metadata numerics, and a per-genre AE prototype), between the true next track and
the top-ten retrieved candidates. A top-ten that contains the exact truth scores
\num{1.0}, so music@10 is a \emph{smooth superset} of Recall@10 --- it credits
landing something that \emph{sounds like} the truth even on an exact / artist /
genre miss. It is display-only; the primary metric stays Recall@10.

\emph{Results.} music@10 was recomputed offline and non-destructively across all
53 next-track runs (from each run's stored \texttt{predictions.json} and its
dataset \texttt{items.json}, on the shared \num{1431}-case split). It
\textbf{correlates} with Recall@10 ($r = \num{0.609}$) --- a better ranker also
lands closer misses --- but it is far from redundant, and it crowns a
\emph{different} winner (Table~\ref{tab:music-families};
Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-music-scatter}). The Recall@10 champion is a
\texttt{seq-blend} run (\num{0.223}), but the \emph{musically-closest} predictions
come from \texttt{seq-nexttrack} --- which regresses the next track's content
latent directly and retrieves by cosine --- at best music@10 \num{0.482} and the
highest family mean (\num{0.448}). When a next-track model misses the exact track,
it still tends to surface something that \emph{sounds} right; the
\texttt{seq-popularity} floor is correctly worst (music@10 \num{0.185}), confirming
the metric is not trivially saturated.

\section{Session holisticness metrics and the anti-eager levers (MMR $\lambda$ / eager $\beta$)}
\label{sec:holisticness}
\emph{Rationale.} A recurring qualitative complaint about the top blends is that
their predictions feel ``too album-eager / bigram-like'' --- they parrot the
current artist rather than curating a holistic continuation. This campaign
quantifies that symptom with a newly-added \emph{display-only session-holisticness}
metric suite, tests the user's hypothesis that \emph{the GRU beats the LSTM because
it is more eager}, and measures whether two new Phase-2 levers reduce eagerness at
an acceptable recall cost: an eval-time MMR re-rank (\texttt{mmr\_lambda}) and an
anti-eager training regularizer (\texttt{eager\_beta}). It is a measurement /
exploratory pass: nothing is promoted, the primary metric stays Recall@10, and the
holisticness objective decision is explicitly deferred to the user. Every arm runs
on the \emph{canonical} PCA-192 champion split \dsid{seq-20260715-131139}
(\num{1431} test sessions; cold-item rate \num{0.55}), and the champion reproduced
its on-record Recall@10 \num{0.2117} exactly (\num{0.21174}).

\paragraph{The new display-only holisticness metrics.} Wired end-to-end alongside
music@10 (\texttt{seq\_common.eval\_from\_scores} $\to$ Rust $\to$
\texttt{domain.toml} $\to$ UI); the primary metric is unchanged.
\begin{description}
  \item[artist\_adj@10 ($\downarrow$).] Fraction of the top-10 sharing the
  \emph{seed} track's artist --- the ``parrots the current artist'' eagerness rate.
  \textbf{Lower is better}.
  \item[album\_adj@10.] The same on (artist, album); \textbf{omitted on this
  dataset} because its \texttt{items.json} carries no album field (expected, not an
  error --- covered by a separate album-carrying dataset).
  \item[mood\_coh@10 ($\uparrow$).] Mean cosine of the top-10 to the prefix mood
  centroid in the content-metric space; higher is more mood-coherent.
  \item[ild@10 ($\uparrow$).] Intra-list diversity (mean pairwise sonic distance) of
  the top-10; higher is less duplicative.
  \item[suffix\_recall@10 / cont\_prec@10.] Multi-step continuation coverage,
  produced only by the on-demand harness \texttt{seq\_continuation\_eval.py}
  (Phase E below), not by normal runs.
\end{description}
The sanity gate passed: music@10 / mood\_coh@10 / ild@10 populate on all blend and
GRU runs, so the content-metric vectors loaded and the MMR / mood / ild levers are
live, not no-ops. Both new levers are OFF by default and verified byte-identical
when off.

\emph{Results --- the holisticness fingerprint (Phase A).} Table~\ref{tab:holisticness-fingerprint}
fingerprints the model family. \textbf{Eagerness tracks Recall@10 inside the blend
family} --- the champion (Recall@10 \num{0.2117}) is the \emph{most} artist-eager
model measured (artist\_adj@10 \num{0.610}), with R+M next (\num{0.579}) --- but the
eagerness lives in the \emph{Markov} leg, not the RNN: the standalone first-order
Markov bar is already at \num{0.543}, while the single GRU / LSTM sit far lower at
artist\_adj@10 $\approx$ \num{0.345}. So the blends buy their Recall@10 lead by being
artist-adjacent (bigram-like), which is exactly what the holisticness suite flags.
The single RNNs are conversely the \emph{mood-coherent, low-eager} models (highest
mood\_coh@10, lowest real-model artist\_adj@10). Crucially the metrics are \emph{not
trivially gamed}: the popularity floor maxes ild@10 (\num{0.898}) and minimizes
artist\_adj@10 (\num{0.003}) yet scores $\approx 0$ recall and \emph{negative}
mood\_coh@10 --- you maximize them by being random, so the meaningful low-eager /

\section{Dual-tower fusion refuted: the fourth combiner null}
\label{sec:dualtower}
\emph{Rationale.} By 2026-07-25 the record held three independent
\emph{learned-combiner} nulls --- the XGB stacker (\numrange{0.080}{0.087},
\emph{below} its own GRU base leg; Section~\ref{sec:phase2}), reciprocal-rank
fusion ($-\num{0.029}$) and even \emph{oracle} test-fit blend weights
($+\num{0.004}$, CI straddling zero; Section~\ref{sec:projection}). All three
were \textbf{post-hoc, score-level} combiners, which left one obvious rebuttal
standing: \emph{of course} a bolted-on combiner fails --- train the fusion
end-to-end so the parts co-adapt. This campaign runs exactly that rebuttal, at
the \emph{representation} level, jointly, from scratch.

The vehicle is a new predictor, \texttt{seq-dualgru}: two \emph{independent}
recurrent towers (separate weights) read the prefix in parallel over
configurable \emph{causal views}, their per-step hidden states are concatenated,
and one MLP head fuses them into the predicted next-item latent. Three views are
exposed, all strictly causal at step $t$: \texttt{latent} (the raw item vector),
\texttt{delta} (the step-to-step difference, $x_{-1}=0$), and \texttt{cummean}
(the running session centroid --- the quantity the \texttt{seq-ann} pooled-MLP
leg was built from). Crucially it reuses \texttt{seq\_nexttrack.make\_batches}
and \texttt{step\_loss} verbatim, so objective, batching, early stopping and
evaluation are byte-for-byte the family's, and it carries no bidirectional mode
(a backward pass would leak the teacher-forcing target).

The hypothesis was sharp: two towers should beat one \emph{only if they see
genuinely different views}; two towers on the same \texttt{latent} view should
reduce to a capacity / implicit-ensemble effect a wider single GRU can buy more
cheaply. The design therefore crosses the \emph{complete upper triangle} of the
$\{\texttt{latent},\texttt{delta},\texttt{cummean}\}^2$ view matrix (six cells at
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=1$) with a \emph{fusion-depth} axis (two cells at
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$, a pure linear read of the concatenated states) and
--- the axis the earlier topology scan lacked (Section~\ref{sec:topology}) --- a
\textbf{parameter-matched single-GRU capacity control}. Parameter counts were
re-measured by instantiating the real classes at \texttt{latent\_dim}${}=192$:
the control C1 (h425, \num{871017} parameters) matches the six
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=1$ dual arms (\num{871872}) to
\SI{0.098}{\percent}, and is \SI{10}{\percent} \emph{larger} than the
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$ arms (\num{789696}) --- deliberately generous to
the single tower.

\paragraph{The honest bar.} Two boards were pre-registered \emph{separately}, so
that the scientific verdict and the board event could not be traded off after the
fact. On \textbf{Recall@10} the bar for a standalone single model is the
single-model board \num{0.123}, \emph{not} the three-leg blend champion
\num{0.212} --- comparing one trained model against a fusion of three legs would
be a category error, and dual-tower-as-the-\Rp-leg is a \texttt{seq-blend} code
change explicitly out of scope. On \textbf{holisticness@10} the crown rule
carries an anti-degenerate recall floor (see below). All ten runs are on the
canonical split \dsid{seq-20260715-131139} at seed 1337, executed one at a time
on a single remote worker, and the baseline C0 reproduced its on-record
Recall@10 \num{0.12299} (\num{176} of \num{1431}) \emph{exactly}.

as a winner because there is no winner}: the single-GRU baseline holds the
batch's best Recall@10, and not one of the eight dual arms beats it by
paired-$\Delta$ CI above zero --- six lose outright and two (the
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$ pair) tie. The pre-registered refutation rule
fired.

\emph{Capacity is not the missed lever either.} The param-matched control C1
(h425, \num{871017} parameters --- $2.2\times$ C0) scores \num{0.12089} against
C0's \num{0.12299}, a paired $\Delta$ of $-\num{0.0021}$ straddling zero. This
fills a real gap: single-GRU \emph{width} had only ever been scanned on the
64-dim AE space (Section~\ref{sec:topology}), and the rich PCA-192 space was
untested. It behaves the same way, so \textbf{single-GRU width is now closed on
PCA-192 as well as AE-64}. Because C1 is a genuine \SI{0.098}{\percent}
parameter match to the \texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=1$ arms, the null cannot be
re-attributed to ``capacity would have helped'': it demonstrably would not.

\emph{View attribution --- complementarity is real, but lands on the wrong
pair.} The complete view triangle is what makes this mechanically decidable
rather than a judgment call, and the answer is genuinely surprising
(Table~\ref{tab:dualtower-views}, Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-dualtower-matrix}).
The pair the design nominated as its best hope --- \texttt{latent} $+$
\texttt{cummean} --- is \emph{not} complementary: it ties its latent/latent
parent and beats only the centroid-only one, i.e.\ the raw-latent tower carries
the cell and the centroid tower is dead weight. Same for \texttt{latent} $+$
\texttt{delta}. But \texttt{delta} $+$ \texttt{cummean} \emph{is} complementary
--- it beats \emph{both} parents with CI above zero. Read together: the
co-adaptation mechanism the hypothesis proposed genuinely \textbf{works}; it is
simply that \textbf{the raw \texttt{latent} view already subsumes everything the
other two carry}. A GRU over raw latents integrates step-to-step movement and
the running mean internally, so pairing it with an explicit computation of
either is redundant --- while two \emph{impoverished} views do have
non-overlapping information to pool, at a hopeless absolute level
(\num{0.08665}). That is a clean mechanistic explanation for the null, not
merely an absence of effect.

\emph{The MLP fusion head is actively harmful, not merely useless.} Both
\texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$ arms beat their \texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=1$ twins
by paired-$\Delta$ CI above zero ($+\num{0.0161}$ on latent/latent,
$+\num{0.0203}$ on latent/cummean) at \SI{10}{\percent} \emph{fewer} parameters
--- so removing the head makes the model measurably better in \emph{both} view
regimes. Mechanistically this fits the whole family arc: the objective is
cosine/InfoNCE retrieval \emph{in} the item-latent space, so the head's job is
to land \emph{in} that geometry, and an extra 256-wide non-linear layer between
the recurrent state and a metric read-out just adds a warp the loss must then
undo. The trade-off runs the other way on holisticness (both f0 arms have
\emph{lower} H, $\Delta$H CI below zero): the f1 arms' extra warp diffuses the
top-ten (higher ild, lower artist\_adj) at the cost of exact hits. The rule
adopted is \textbf{default \texttt{fusion\_layers}${}=0$}, later confirmed a
third time (Table~\ref{tab:fusion-depth}).

\emph{Holisticness and recall are anti-correlated, and the recall floor earned
its place.} Five arms lift holisticness@10 by paired-$\Delta$H CI above zero,
topping out at D-CC's \num{0.19715} ($+\num{0.055}$) --- which on the naked crown
metric would have ranked second on the entire board. But D-CC lands \num{95} of
\num{1431} exact hits against the baseline's \num{176}. The ordering of the batch
by H is almost the \emph{reverse} of its ordering by recall
(Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-holisticness-antiwin}), so \textbf{all five
$\Delta$H winners fail the pre-registered $\pm\num{0.015}$ recall floor} and the
crown rule never fired. Without that floor the campaign would have ``won the
crown'' with a model \SI{46}{\percent} worse at its actual job --- precisely the
degenerate \texttt{mood-session} failure mode (H \num{0.2332} at Recall@10
\num{0.0238}) the floor was written to block. On the present evidence,
\textbf{unconstrained holisticness is maximized by predicting less well}, so any
future holisticness objective on this corpus must be a \emph{constrained} one.

the leaderboard and the pins are untouched. What the campaign buys is a
\emph{closure}: the combiner null is now robust across the whole combiner design
space on this corpus --- post-hoc score-level (three ways) \emph{and}
representation-level jointly-trained with a parameter-matched control --- which
materially consolidates ``the fixed equal-thirds z-blend is the combiner of
record.'' Two genuine sub-findings survive the null (the harmful MLP fusion head,
and \texttt{delta}$+$\texttt{cummean} complementarity), and the machinery itself
is sound: 10 of 10 runs succeeded, the new predictor trains, converges and
evaluates at exact parity with the family, and D-LL-f0 even posts the batch's
best MRR (\num{0.05631} against C0's \num{0.05341}) --- the one metric on which a
dual arm leads. Per the noise-band discipline that is ``at least equal, likely
neither better nor worse,'' not a win.

\paragraph{A provenance finding that revises two earlier numbers.} Before the
batch, the live remote worker directory was found \emph{stale}: its
\texttt{seq\_common.py} had no \texttt{holisticness\_at\_k} at all,
\texttt{seq\_nexttrack.py} differed from the hub by md5, and three
\texttt{seq\_*.py} modules were missing entirely. After refreshing and verifying
the whole family md5-identical to the hub, C0 returned \num{0.12299},

$-\num{0.0028}$'' those runs had suggested is therefore \textbf{withdrawn: it was
a code difference, not a host effect}. Their recall numbers belong to a
superseded code state and are not code-comparable to later batches; they remain
valid as the $\beta$-eagerness measurements they were taken for
(Table~\ref{tab:eager-beta}), since those conclusions rest on within-batch
comparisons. The general lesson --- a divergent remote checkout produces a
\emph{wrong number}, not a crash --- is now a standing pre-flight gate.

\section{The per-step pre-encoder refuted, and the rectifier mechanism}
\label{sec:preencoder}
\emph{Rationale.} The dual-tower null closed \emph{deterministic} view
asymmetry: \texttt{delta} and \texttt{cummean} are lossy functions of what a
raw-latent tower already holds, so pairing them buys nothing. This campaign asks
the strictly stronger version --- does a \textbf{learned} per-step
re-embedding, which is \emph{not} a function of information the tower already
has, enlarge the hypothesis space usefully? The axis is a nonlinear
re-embedding in front of the recurrence,
$[\,\text{Linear}(d_{\text{in}},w),\ \text{ReLU},\ \text{Dropout}\,]\times
\texttt{pre\_layers}$, applied per timestep (strictly causal), with the GRU's
\texttt{input\_size} becoming $w$.

The hypothesis has an unusually sharp analytic edge. A GRU's own input transform
is \emph{already linear} ($W_i x$ per gate), and a \emph{linear} pre-encoder at
$w \ge d_{\text{in}}$ is exactly as expressive as a bare GRU, since
$W_i(Vx) = (W_iV)x$ with no rank constraint. \textbf{The ReLU is therefore the
only expressivity change}, which means no linear-pre control run is needed and a
null cannot be explained away as ``the pre-encoder was redundant by
construction.'' The design's decisive move is the \textbf{single-tower ablation}
S1 --- a \texttt{MLP256}$\to$\texttt{GRU256} against a parameter-matched plain
GRU --- because without it any dual-tower result is uninterpretable: pre-encoder,
or second tower?

Ten runs, same canonical split, same single remote worker, zero failures. Two
reproduction gates both passed \emph{bit-identically}: C0 returned Recall@10
\num{0.12299091544374564} (\num{176} of \num{1431}, identical to the last digit,
a seventh reproduction), confirming the \texttt{pre\_hidden} edit did not perturb
the family's default code path; and C3, the bare dual-tower arm, returned
\num{0.10272536687631029}, exactly its on-record value, validating every dual
verdict.

\emph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:preencoder} is the batch and
Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-preencoder-forest} is the verdict at a glance. Not one
of the six pre-MLP arms beats the baseline by paired-$\Delta$ CI above zero:
five lose outright, one ties. \textbf{And the decisive ablation does not merely
tie --- it loses}: S1 at \num{0.10901} against C0's \num{0.12299}, $\Delta$
$-\num{0.0140}$ with the CI entirely below zero, \num{156} hits against
\num{176}. Since a linear pre-encoder here is provably expressivity-neutral,
\textbf{the ReLU re-embedding is destroying prefix information that the GRU's own
gated linear input transform preserved}.

\emph{The mechanism, measured rather than inferred.} The analytic argument named
the rectifier as the only candidate; a follow-up interpretability read then
\emph{measured} it (Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-rectifier}). The decisive control
was a counterfactual tap the sparse-autoencoder engine cannot express --- the
model's \emph{own} affine map with the ReLU deleted. Reading next-item genre
decodability at the same step (\num{11184} probe test rows, 25 classes, paired
standard error $\le\num{0.0065}$): the raw latent scores \num{0.4033}, the
affine map \num{0.4084} (\textbf{neutral, ${\sim}0.8\sigma$}, exactly as
$W_i(Vx)=(W_iV)x$ predicts), and the rectified tap \num{0.3700}
(\textbf{${\sim}5.9\sigma$ --- the ReLU is the whole loss}). The smoking gun is
arithmetic: \texttt{frac\_exact\_zero} on the rectified tap is \num{0.5327},
matching the affine tap's \texttt{frac\_negative\_coords} \num{0.5327} to four
digits. The rectifier deletes \emph{precisely} the negative half, leaving
${\sim}120$ live units for 192 signed directions --- \emph{fewer live coordinates
than the input has dimensions}. PCA-192 latents are zero-centred and
near-symmetric by construction, and at $w=256$ (only $1.33\times$ the input
dimension) there is nowhere near enough over-completeness to re-encode the
discarded half-space, unlike the wide embeddings where ReLU MLPs normally live.
The damage survives the recurrence: within one model, \texttt{tower\_b} (fed
rectified) scores \num{0.3929} against its \emph{identical twin}
\texttt{tower\_a} (fed raw) at \num{0.4183}, ${\sim}3.9\sigma$ --- and
\texttt{tower\_a} reproduces the standalone plain GRU (\num{0.4235}), which is
exactly what ``the bare tower \emph{rescues}'' requires.

\emph{Interpretation, and one honest caveat about the controls.} This is the
fifth consecutive architecture null, and it closes the \emph{input side} as the
view null closed the input-view direction: together with the four
learned-combiner nulls, adding representation machinery \emph{around} a single
GRU over a good latent space does not help on this corpus, whereas every real
gain on record came from improving the \emph{latent space itself} (the InfoNCE
content projection, PCA-192) or from the fixed z-blend. The axis is now
quantitatively bounded --- best pre arm \num{0.12369}, a tie; dose flat to twice
the input dimension; placement irrelevant; the fusion-depth interaction
resolved. The caveat is that the \emph{parameter-matched width control was a
weaker instrument than assumed}: C1 (h297) came in at \num{0.11321}, itself
losing to C0, and the batch's width points run \num{176} $\to$ \num{162} $\to$
\num{173} $\to$ \num{179} hits at h256 / h297 / h425 / h454 --- non-monotone,
spanning \num{0.0119} in recall with no trend. ``Single-GRU width is flat on
PCA-192'' survives as a \emph{trend} claim (h454 ties h256), but per-width jitter
is ${\sim}\pm\num{0.01}$ on a single split, roughly two-thirds of the noise band.
\textbf{A width-matched control that is itself a single unreplicated run is not a
reliable capacity anchor at this effect size} --- which is why it matters that
S1's verdict rests on the C0 comparison, reproduced to the last digit. A second
caveat is un-disentangled: the pre-MLP ends in \texttt{Dropout}, so pre arms
receive GRU-\emph{input} dropout that the bare arms lack, and S1's loss could be
partly over-regularization rather than the rectifier --- separating them needs a
\texttt{pre\_dropout} parameter that does not yet exist.

\section{Reading the record: statistical power and the holisticness generations}
\label{sec:power}
Two cross-cutting facts emerged from the 2026-07-25/26 batches that change how
every number above should be read. Neither is a result about a model; both are
results about the \emph{measuring instrument}, and they belong here rather than
buried in one campaign.

\subsection{The nulls are power-limited, and that is a different claim}
\label{sec:power-stats}
Every verdict in this document rests on a paired per-session bootstrap over the
same \num{1431} test sessions. That $n$ has a measurable resolution. Averaged
over the nine paired comparisons of the pre-encoder batch the 95\% CI half-width
is \textbf{\num{0.0124}} on Recall@10 (\num{0.0128} over the dual-tower batch's
nine, so the figure is stable), and \textbf{${\sim}\num{0.0018}$} on
holisticness@10. Now compare those to the \emph{entire spread} of an architecture
family: \num{0.028} on Recall@10 and \num{0.0031} on holisticness@10
(Table~\ref{tab:power}). The ratio is the whole story --- a family's full range
is only ${\sim}2.3$ resolution widths wide on recall and ${\sim}1.9$ on
holisticness, so of eight adjacent arms roughly \textbf{two tiers} are separable
on Recall@10 and \textbf{one} on holisticness@10. Resolving a \num{0.005}
Recall@10 difference at this precision would require
$n \approx 1431\cdot(\num{0.0124}/\num{0.005})^2 \approx \num{8800}$ test
sessions, six times what the split provides
(Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-power}).

\textbf{The honest reading of the five architecture nulls follows directly, and
it is not the reading a casual glance gives.} What these campaigns established
firmly is that the tested architectures do not deliver \emph{large} gains, and
that several of them do real \emph{harm} --- the harms are the well-powered
results, because they are big (S1 $-\num{0.0140}$; the \texttt{fusion\_layers}
regression $+\num{0.0266}$; six of eight dual arms below the baseline). What the
campaigns \emph{cannot} establish is the absence of a moderate gain: a genuine
$+\num{0.005}$ improvement --- which on a \num{0.123} baseline would be a
respectable \SI{4}{\percent} relative lift --- would have been invisible by
construction. So ``dual-tower fusion is refuted'' and ``the pre-encoder is
refuted'' should be read as \emph{no large effect, and no effect of the
hypothesized magnitude}, not as ``these architectures do not work.'' The
practical consequence is a prioritization rule rather than a caveat: on this
split, further architecture arms are a poor use of runs no matter how they come
out, because the design space's whole dynamic range is barely twice the
measurement error. Either enlarge $n$ or move to axes whose effects are large ---
which is, empirically, exactly what the representation and supervision axes have
delivered (the content projection's $+\num{0.0259}$ is twice the half-width, and
the second-split hardening's $+\num{0.0426}$ is three times it).

\subsection{Three generations of holisticness@10, and why the board is denominated in recall}
\label{sec:power-generations}
The composite \emph{holisticness@10} was revised twice in four days, and stored
values are \textbf{not comparable across the cuts}:
\begin{description}
  \item[Gen.\ 1 --- 3-factor (to 2026-07-24).] The bare product
  \[\mathrm{clamp}(\text{mood\_coh},0)\times\text{ild}\times(1-\text{artist\_adj}),\]
  carrying \emph{no correctness term at all}. Values land in
  \numrange{0.063}{0.233} --- the column in Table~\ref{tab:dualtower}.
  \item[Gen.\ 2 --- 4-factor, grounded (2026-07-25).] Multiplies in a
  \texttt{music@10} grounding factor,
  \[\mathrm{clamp}\bigl((\text{music@10}-\num{0.185})/\num{0.815},\,0,\,1\bigr).\]
  Because \texttt{music@10} falls in lockstep with recall, this cancels the
  de-eagering ``wins'' the 3-factor form rewarded; values land in
  \numrange{0.044}{0.050} --- the column in Table~\ref{tab:preencoder}.

  \texttt{max(artist\_adj,} \texttt{artist\_conc)} and the grounding floor
  rises \num{0.185}$\to$\num{0.34}. Values land near \numrange{0.009}{0.025}.

\section{The walk surface: measuring what the product actually serves}
\label{sec:walk}
\emph{Rationale.} Every verdict up to this point --- including the crown board
of Table~\ref{tab:lb} and all three holisticness generations of
Section~\ref{sec:power-generations} --- is computed on a \textbf{one-shot
top-10}: the model sees one held-out prefix, ranks the catalog once, and is
scored against one target. But the artifact this lab ships,
\texttt{clients/infinite-playlist}, serves an \textbf{autoregressive walk}: it
picks a track, appends it to the session, re-scores, and repeats for as many
steps as the listener asks for. Those are different surfaces, and
\texttt{domain.toml} has said so in prose the whole time --- its
\texttt{[metrics]} block states the goal as ``mood-coherent, non-eager
\emph{sessions}, not guessing the literal next track.'' The stated intent and
the implemented metric had diverged. This section closes that gap: it defines
four walk-level metrics calibrated against the user's own listening, adds three
\emph{retrieval-time} controls, and runs a matched-sample head-to-head. It is
also the one place in this document where a documented refutation is
\emph{reversed} on a new objective, which is why it is reported at length rather
than folded into the campaign sections above.

\subsection{The surface mismatch, and why drift was invisible}
\label{sec:walk-mismatch}
Two mechanical facts explain why nothing above could have caught a drifting
journey.

\textbf{First, the anchor grows.} The \texttt{mood\_coh} facet of
holisticness@10 measures each candidate's cosine to the \emph{prefix} centroid
(\texttt{seq\_common.py:558}). On a one-shot evaluation the prefix is fixed and
that is a reasonable read of ``does this list stay on vibe.'' On a walk the
prefix \emph{is} the journey so far, so the reference point travels with the
model: a journey that leaves its starting region by a thousand individually
legal steps scores well \emph{at every step}, because each step is compared to a
centroid that has already moved most of the way there. \textbf{Drift is
invisible by construction} --- not under-weighted, but unmeasurable in this
formulation.

\textbf{Second, the composite is dominated by relevance.} Differentiating the
generation-3 composite at the champion's operating point gives
$\partial H/\partial\,\texttt{music@10} = +\num{0.206}$ against
$\partial H/\partial\,\texttt{eager} = -\num{0.038}$: holisticness@10 is
$\num{5.5}\times$ more sensitive to its relevance factor than to its eagerness
factor. So even the term that was \emph{meant} to price session quality is
mostly re-reporting graded relevance. This is the same asymmetry that
Section~\ref{sec:power-generations} observed from the outside, where the
grounding factor's ${\sim}0.3$ multiplier made every generation-2 value rank
below every generation-1 value; here it is quantified at the derivative.

\subsection{Four walk-level metrics, calibrated against the user's own sessions}
\label{sec:walk-metrics}
The new instrument (\texttt{tools/walk\_eval.py}) generates journeys from
held-out \emph{real} sessions --- a 5-track seed taken from the session, then 20
autoregressive steps, prefix items excluded, at most one track per artist unless
stated --- and scores them on four axes:

\begin{description}
  \item[1. Transition match (primary).] Do the walk's step-to-step distances
  look like this user's \emph{real} listening? This follows the playlist-lab
  finding directly: measure \emph{transitions}, not averages, and calibrate
  against the user's own sessions. Averaging a distance-to-centroid is the wrong
  shape --- it penalizes a legitimate genre move and rewards standing still.
  Reported as the 1-Wasserstein distance ($W_1$, lower better) between the
  journey's consecutive-pair cosine distribution and the real one, plus two
  legible reads: the median step cosine and the share of steps inside the real
  p10--p90 band.
  \item[2. Vibe and drift.] The \emph{seed-anchored} cosine: mean cosine of the
  generated tracks to the fixed seed-core centroid (\texttt{vibe}, higher
  better), and the end-quarter minus start-quarter difference (\texttt{drift},
  nearer zero better). The anchor is fixed at the seed and does \emph{not} grow,
  which is precisely the defect of \texttt{mood\_coh} that
  Section~\ref{sec:walk-mismatch} identifies.
  \item[3. Variety as credit.] Distinct artists and distinct genres in the
  journey, counted as a \emph{credit} rather than as the cost that
  \texttt{artist\_adj}/\texttt{artist\_conc} price. Cohesion is not one genre:
  different genres are welcome if they preserve the vibe, so a journey covering
  more of them at equal vibe is better, not worse.
  \item[4. Grounding.] Graded relevance (best sonic cosine, in the balanced
  musical-distance space of Section~\ref{sec:music}) between each generated
  track and what the user \emph{actually} played next in that session. This is
  the guard-rail: without it a pleasant unrelated drift wins, which is exactly
  the lesson the crown learned when \texttt{music\_rel} was added.
\end{description}

\paragraph{The calibration, and the single number that reframed the project.}
The reference distribution is the user's own consecutive-pair cosines over the
\num{5723} \emph{train} sessions of \dsid{seq-20260715-131139}:
\textbf{\num{73632} pairs, median \num{0.261}, p10--p90
$[-\num{0.192}, \num{0.830}]$, and \SI{27.0}{\percent} of real steps
\emph{negative}}. Real listening moves --- it changes direction more than a
quarter of the time. Against that, the engine the showcase actually shipped (a
single GRU as \texttt{gru.onnx} with a seed anchor of \num{0.4}) walks with a
median step of \textbf{\num{0.661}}, roughly $\num{2.5}\times$ tighter than the
user does (Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-walk-transitions}). The complaint that the
showcase felt ``flat despite lots of artists'' now has a measurement behind it:
many small, safe moves. The blend champion has the opposite failure --- its
median step, \num{0.317}, sits essentially on the real median, but its
\texttt{vibe} is only \num{0.187}, because its InfoNCE projection
(Section~\ref{sec:projection}) encodes ``what follows this,'' not ``what sounds
like this,'' so a mood anchor applied \emph{in} that projected space caps at
\num{0.44} while the same anchor in the raw PCA-192 space reaches \num{0.58}.

\paragraph{$W_1$ is not pairable, and that mattered.} The distributional $W_1$ is
computed over a \emph{pooled} sample of steps, so it has no per-session value and
cannot enter a paired bootstrap --- the statistic every other verdict in this
document rests on. The first two sweeps therefore compared arms measured on
\emph{different} samples (20 sessions $\times$ 20 steps for the frontier, 12
$\times$ 15 for the dual-tower probe), and a vibe gap of \num{0.588} against
\num{0.624} is well inside what that mismatch alone can produce: nothing was
established. The head-to-head (Section~\ref{sec:walk-h2h}) therefore replaces
$W_1$ with \texttt{stride\_err} $=$ $|$median step $-$ \num{0.261}$|$, a
per-session quantity that pairs, at the cost of describing only the
distribution's centre.

\subsection{Three retrieval-time controls, and two refuted ones}
\label{sec:walk-controls}
None of the three controls below touches training. All are applied at
\emph{retrieval} time, on the scores the model already produces, which is what
makes them cheap to ship and cheap to re-tune.

\begin{description}
  \item[ANCHOR ($a$).] Add $a\cdot\cos(\text{candidate},\ \text{seed centroid})$
  to every candidate's score. It holds the geist by pulling the walk back toward
  the region the listener seeded it from. For the champion the term is
  z-normalized over the admissible candidates, because its base score is itself a
  z-blend of three legs and adding a raw cosine to a z-blend is scale-mismatched.
  \item[STRIDE ($s$).] Subtract $s\cdot\cos(\text{candidate},\ \text{previous
  track})$. It \emph{enlarges} each step without letting the walk leave the
  seed's neighbourhood, because the anchor still applies --- the two terms are
  orthogonal by design, one pulling toward a fixed point and one pushing off the
  last point. It is deliberately \emph{deterministic}: sampling would also loosen
  the stride, but the showcase ships permalinks, and ``same recipe $\Rightarrow$
  same journey'' is now a promise, so a stochastic decoder was ruled out rather
  than untried.
  \item[CAP.] A \emph{hard} per-artist limit applied to the ordering
  (\texttt{seq\_common.artist\_cap\_order}), after MMR, in both
  \texttt{eval\_from\_scores} and \texttt{rank\_topk}/\texttt{predict\_ranking},
  so evaluation and serving agree by construction. Best-effort rather than
  absolute: where a prefix's admissible candidate space is exhausted the
  remaining slots are backfilled with deferred items instead of returning fewer
  than $k$. Default \texttt{None}, so every pre-existing run and model is
  byte-identical; and because it needs no sonic vectors it is the only diversity
  lever here that ports to a thin client unchanged.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why a cap and not a penalty --- the two refuted controls.} Both soft
anti-eager levers of Section~\ref{sec:holisticness} fail for one shared reason: a
soft penalty in score space loses to a steep relevance gradient.
\texttt{eager\_beta}, the training-time regularizer, is \emph{mis-specified}: it
hinges on the absolute value of $\cos(\text{prediction},\ \text{current
track})$, which a hedged InfoNCE prediction never drives above ${\sim}0.6$,
whereas eagerness is a \emph{ranking} property --- whether same-artist items
occupy the top slots --- not a property of an absolute similarity.
\texttt{mmr\_lambda}, the eval-time re-rank, was additionally \emph{eval-only}
until the serving path was fixed, so the $\lambda$ frontier had never been a
deployable lever at all; and even fixed it measures inert on the pathological
case. On the real playlist \texttt{rock chabon ahre} the model ranks \num{59} of
its top \num{60} candidates as one artist and the next artist first appears at
\textbf{rank \num{75}}, while MMR's entire diversity budget there is worth
$\num{0.3}\times\num{0.26}\approx\num{0.078}$ of normalized score --- nowhere
near enough to pay for a 70-rank relevance drop. A cap does not compete on score
at all: it \emph{cannot be outscored}, the same property \texttt{seq\_extend}'s
\texttt{artist\_cooldown} already relied on. The showcase's own soft artist
penalty turned out to be effectively \textbf{binary} in the same way --- at
\num{0} it produced artist blocks with runs up to \num{13}, and at \emph{any}
value $\ge\num{0.25}$ every 30-step walk returned \num{30} distinct artists, max
run \num{1}, concentration \num{0.000} --- so the slider could not reach the
middle at all, and a hard cap exposed as a journey shape (Explore / Balanced /
Continue / Blocks) is what reaches it.

\paragraph{The anchor $\times$ stride frontier.} With the two score-space
controls in hand the question becomes whether the \emph{conjunction} is
reachable at all: a real-listening stride \emph{and} a held vibe.
Table~\ref{tab:walk-frontier} sweeps \num{24} cells --- \num{16} on the GRU,
\num{8} on the champion, all at cap 1 --- against a target window
pre-registered before the sweep: median step inside the real band's middle
($\numrange{0.20}{0.38}$) \emph{and} \texttt{vibe} $\ge\num{0.50}$. Exactly two
cells land inside it, one per engine (Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-walk-frontier}).
The structure of the grid is the finding: the two controls are genuinely
orthogonal --- along a fixed anchor, raising the stride walks the median step
down monotonically while the vibe decays much more slowly --- so the window is
reachable only with both on, and neither shipped configuration was inside it.
The champion's anchor also has to be applied in the \emph{raw} PCA-192 space
rather than its own projected geometry, which is why the frontier reaches
\num{0.503} where the first probe capped at \num{0.44}.

\subsection{The dual-tower reversal, and what the record already knew}
\label{sec:walk-reversal}
This is worth stating plainly, because it is the most consequential
methodological result in this document: \textbf{the dual-tower family was closed
by three campaigns on recall grounds and is the winner on the stated
objective.} Section~\ref{sec:dualtower} refuted it across the complete
$\{$\texttt{latent},\texttt{delta},\texttt{cummean}$\}^2$ view matrix; the
\texttt{cummean/cummean} arm was even flagged \emph{degenerate} for topping the
generation-1 holisticness at Recall@10 \num{0.066}. Every one of those judgements
was made on one-shot next-track accuracy, and none of them is wrong on that
surface --- the reversal is not a correction of the recall verdicts but a
demonstration that they answered a different question. Section~\ref{sec:power-stats}'s
power analysis makes the two readings compatible rather than contradictory: what
the dual-tower campaign established was the absence of a \emph{large} recall
gain, which is fully consistent with the family being better at something the
recall metric does not measure.

\textbf{The corroboration was already in the record, filed as a negative.} The
interpretability note at \texttt{predictors/seq\_dualgru.py:113} records that the
\texttt{cummean} tower ``added real linear directions (CKA \num{0.458}) but
\textbf{zero} next-item concepts tower A lacked.'' Read as a next-track result
that is dead weight, and it was written down as such. Read on the walk surface it
is the useful part: a tower whose content is orthogonal to next-item prediction
is exactly a tower carrying \emph{session state} rather than adjacency. Two
structural facts complete the explanation. The family retrieves in the
\emph{raw} item-latent space with no learned projection, so a raw-space mood
anchor is native to its geometry --- unlike the champion, whose InfoNCE
projection strips precisely the sound-similarity structure an anchor needs. And
one promoted arm reads the \emph{causal mean} of the prefix, which is
structurally a running-average signal.

\textbf{One sub-hypothesis is refuted.} The natural prediction from the above ---
that the \texttt{cummean} tower holds a vibe \emph{natively} --- is false: with
both controls off its vibe is \num{0.236}, statistically indistinguishable from
\texttt{latent/latent}'s \num{0.234}. The architecture does not supply the mood
hold on its own; it only becomes the best host for one once the anchor and stride
are applied. That is a weaker and more interesting claim than the one the
structural argument suggested, and it is the one the measurement supports.

\subsection{Deployment, and what is not established}
\label{sec:walk-limits}
The tuned dual-tower arm is now the \textbf{default engine} in the showcase,
exported as \texttt{dualgru.onnx} (\num{3.16}\,MB) with ONNX-versus-torch
parity of \num{1e-7} across every prefix length $T = 1\ldots40$ --- the
\texttt{cummean} cumulative sum was the trace risk, and it is the reason the
parity check swept $T$ rather than spot-checking it. The single GRU is kept
alongside for comparison, and the tuned anchor/stride live in the deployment
manifest rather than in code, so re-tuning is a re-bake and not a code edit. The
blend champion was removed from the showcase end-to-end: served from a Worker
its walk cost ${\sim}\SI{0.94}{\second}$ of CPU per batch of 8, roughly
$100\times$ the \SI{10}{\milli\second} CPU cap of the free plan the showcase is
deployed on, and batching cannot rescue a single step that already costs
${\sim}\SI{117}{\milli\second}$.

\paragraph{What this section does \emph{not} establish.} Four limits, stated
plainly. \textbf{(1)} \texttt{ground} straddles zero for \emph{every} arm
(Table~\ref{tab:walk-paired}), so no arm is established as better at predicting
the real continuation --- the wins are about journey \emph{shape} only, and the
grounding guard-rail confirms only that no arm degraded into pleasant
irrelevance. \textbf{(2)} One split, one seed, \num{40} sessions of \num{20}
steps; the walk metrics have had no second-split hardening of the kind
Section~\ref{sec:projection-hardening} applies to the recall crown, and the
target window itself was chosen from the first sweep's spread. \textbf{(3)}
Nothing here has been \emph{listened} to. Every claim is a geometric claim about
cosines in a PCA-192 space, and ``holds a mood'' is operationalized as
``stays near a seed centroid,'' which is a hypothesis about perception, not a
measurement of it. \textbf{(4)} The project's primary metric is
\textbf{unchanged}: holisticness@10 remains \texttt{[metrics].primary} and this
document's board remains denominated in Recall@10
(Section~\ref{sec:power-generations}). Whether the walk metrics should become
primary is an open decision, not a settled one --- and until it is settled, the
honest summary is that the walk surface is now \emph{measurable}, and on it one
refuted family wins.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Fifteen campaigns (Jul 2026) on the next-track retrieval task converge on a clear
story. (1) A session sequence model, even at its best (GRU-infonce), only
\emph{ties} the first-order Markov co-listening bar on Recall@10 and loses on
MRR: predicting a next-item latent and retrieving by one cosine hop is a harder
route to the exact next track than a bigram that directly memorizes adjacency.
(2) The training \emph{loss} is the dominant single-model lever (InfoNCE
$\approx 2.3\times$ cosine); network \emph{topology} --- depth, width, residual,
direction --- is a spent lever, and no architecture beats the bar. (3) The
breakthrough is \emph{rank fusion}: an R+M z-blend of the GRU and Markov legs
beats the bar by $\sim$$+61\%$ Recall@10 (three-seed confirmed), because the two
legs rank \emph{different} correct tracks. The win is about signal
\emph{diversity}, not model count --- adding a redundant neural leg does not
help, and a learned XGB stacker \emph{underperforms} the fixed z-blend, because
its pointwise training does not capture the ranking objective. (4) An
item-representation sweep found latent \emph{dimension}, not the compressor, to
be the lever: linear PCA beats the nonlinear autoencoder at every dim $\ge 64$
(the acoustic-preservation hypothesis is refuted), the curve peaks at PCA-192 and
turns over by 256, and PCA-128 is the confirmed single-model base. (5) On the
rich PCA space the one genuinely additive new signal --- \emph{content-kNN},
which only \emph{tied} the champion on the AE-64 space --- becomes a win: a
three-leg R+M+C z-blend on PCA-192 is the current best-on-record row at Recall@10
\num{0.1859}, MRR \num{0.1025} (paired $\Delta$ over the prior champion
$+\num{0.0133}$, CI above zero, confirmed across five reproductions). (6) Two
honest caveats bound the crown: the reproduction confirm had zero spread because
\texttt{seq-nexttrack} is deterministic on this CPU (so it verified determinism,
not seed-robustness), and the paired-$\Delta$ lower bound clears zero by only
$+\num{0.0007}$ on a single split --- hardening it needs a second leak-free test
split. (7) Every ranking predictor here is train-only and off-server, so the
crown is a best-on-record \emph{row}, not a promoted, app-exportable model; the
prior champion R+M on AE-64 remains the productization anchor, and registering a
three-leg blend with a \texttt{predict} subcommand is the standing top follow-up.
(8) A subsequent literature-fit campaign then \emph{superseded} the crown with the
first genuinely-additive new signal since content: a supervised \emph{learned
content projection} (\Rp+M+\Cp), an InfoNCE metric map of the PCA-192 item space
fit toward next-track adjacency in which both the GRU and content legs retrieve, at
Recall@10 \num{0.2117}, MRR \num{0.1220} (paired $\Delta$ over R+M+C $+\num{0.0259}$,
CI$>$0). It resolves both R+M+C caveats --- it is seed-robust across three seeds and
cold-reaching (its content leg scores cold targets as well as warm) --- and it was
\emph{registered, promoted and servable} (\texttt{blend-gru-markov-content-proj},
best-models rank 1), closing the productization gap; an objective / rank / $\tau$
scan confirms InfoNCE $\gg$ BPR and holds the default, and the crown then hardened
on a second disjoint 0.64-cold split (paired $\Delta$ $+\num{0.0426}$, CI$>$0, the
margin \emph{wider} on the colder split). Every borrowed literature-fit method
(session-kNN, rank-fusion / learned combiners, GRU-side loss tricks, Markov
smoothing) closed --- the only additive gain came from new signal, not smarter
fusion. (9) An item-vector representation exploration then confirmed \emph{why} the
projection is the champion: the equal-weighted PCA-192 geometry is a real handicap
for a raw model (six of eight whitened / acoustic-dropped / metric spaces lift the
frozen GRU, paired $\Delta$ CI$>$0), but the gain is fully \emph{absorbed} by the
learned projection (no space beats the champion on readout A) --- whitening and the
projection are the same fix, and the champion has already pulled it. (10) Finally, a
continuous ``sounds-alike'' metric, \emph{music@10}, was introduced and back-filled
across all 53 runs: it correlates with Recall@10 ($r=\num{0.609}$) but crowns a
different winner (the content-regressing \texttt{seq-nexttrack} lands the
musically-closest misses, best \num{0.482}), corroborating the exact-recall findings
without overturning them; it is display-only and the primary metric stays Recall@10. (11) Finally, a
measurement campaign added a display-only \emph{session-holisticness} suite
(artist\_adj@10 / mood\_coh@10 / ild@10) and two anti-eager levers. It found the
eagerness symptom \emph{tracks} Recall@10 inside the blend family --- the champion
is the most artist-eager model (artist\_adj@10 \num{0.610}) --- but the eagerness
is sourced from the \emph{Markov} bigram leg, not the RNN (single GRU / LSTM
artist\_adj@10 $\approx$ \num{0.345}), and the GRU and LSTM are statistically
tied on both recall and eagerness (the ``GRU wins because more eager'' hypothesis
is refuted). Of the two levers the eval-time MMR re-rank is the strong knob
($\lambda{=}\num{0.9}$ near-free: recall tie, ild@10 \num{0.406}$\to$\num{0.436};
$\lambda\approx\num{0.7}$ de-eagers for $\sim$2 recall points; music@10 even
rises as the list diversifies) while the training-time $\beta$ regularizer is weak
($\beta$ \numrange{0}{0.2} moves artist\_adj@10 only $-\num{0.0074}$); nothing
was promoted, and $\lambda\approx\num{0.9}$ is flagged for a future user
holisticness decision (Section~\ref{sec:holisticness}).
(12) Two final architecture campaigns then closed the last open architecture
axes, and both are nulls --- but nulls with mechanism. A jointly-trained
\emph{dual-tower} GRU, crossing the complete
$\{$\texttt{latent},\texttt{delta},\texttt{cummean}$\}^2$ view matrix against a
\SI{0.098}{\percent} parameter-matched single tower, fails to beat that tower on
any of eight arms (Section~\ref{sec:dualtower}). This is the \emph{fourth}
independent learned-combiner null and the one that matters most, because it
answers the standing rebuttal to the earlier three (``of course a bolted-on
combiner fails --- train the fusion end-to-end''): trained end-to-end at the
representation level, it still fails. The mechanism is that the raw
\texttt{latent} view already \emph{subsumes} the others --- a GRU over raw
latents integrates movement and running mean internally, and the one genuinely
complementary pair is \texttt{delta}$+$\texttt{cummean}, which beats both its
parents yet is hopeless in absolute terms. Two by-products are durable: the MLP
fusion head is actively \emph{harmful} (removing it wins in every regime
measured, three times over, Table~\ref{tab:fusion-depth}), and single-GRU width
is now closed on PCA-192 as it already was on AE-64. A learned per-step
\emph{pre-encoder} then fails harder (Section~\ref{sec:preencoder}): the
decisive single-tower ablation \emph{loses} outright ($-\num{0.0140}$, CI below
zero, \num{156} hits against \num{176}), the dose curve is flat to twice the
input dimension, and placement is irrelevant. Because a \emph{linear}
pre-encoder is provably expressivity-neutral here, the rectifier is the only
candidate cause --- and a counterfactual interpretability read confirmed it,
measuring the affine map neutral at ${\sim}0.8\sigma$ and the ReLU costing
${\sim}5.9\sigma$, with \texttt{frac\_exact\_zero} \num{0.5327} matching
\texttt{frac\_negative\_coords} \num{0.5327} to four digits: half-wave
rectification of a zero-centred latent deletes exactly its negative half. Across
five architecture nulls the pattern is now hard to dismiss --- on this
single-user, \SI{55}{\percent}-cold corpus, adding representation machinery
\emph{around} a single GRU over a good latent space does not help, while every
real gain came from improving the latent space itself or from the fixed z-blend.
(13) Finally, two facts about the \emph{instrument} rather than any model
reframe the whole architecture arc (Section~\ref{sec:power}). The nulls are
\textbf{power-limited}: at $n=\num{1431}$ the paired-bootstrap CI half-width is
\num{0.0124} on Recall@10 and ${\sim}\num{0.0018}$ on holisticness@10, while a
whole architecture family spans only \num{0.028} and \num{0.0031} --- so of
eight adjacent arms roughly two tiers are separable on recall and one on
holisticness, and resolving a \num{0.005} difference would need
${\sim}\num{8800}$ sessions. The honest reading is therefore that \emph{large
harms were detected reliably while moderate wins were never detectable at all},
which is a different claim from ``these architectures do not work'' and yields a
prioritization rule: further architecture arms are a poor use of runs on this
split whatever they return, because the axis's whole dynamic range is barely
twice the measurement error. And \emph{holisticness@10} exists in three
mutually non-comparable code generations (3-factor; 4-factor grounded;
grounded plus artist-concentration), whose differences dwarf every model
difference and under all of which the servable champion ranks \emph{last} ---
so this synthesis keeps its board denominated in Recall@10 and tags every
holisticness column with its generation (Table~\ref{tab:hgen}).
(14) Finally, a campaign about the \emph{surface} rather than the model finds
that the board above had been scoring the wrong one
(Section~\ref{sec:walk}). Everything else in this document is a one-shot top-10,
while the shipped product serves an autoregressive \emph{walk} --- and
\texttt{holisticness@10}'s \texttt{mood\_coh} anchors to the \emph{prefix}
centroid, which on a walk grows with the journey, so a run of individually legal
steps that ends in the opposite region of the space scores well at every step:
drift was invisible by construction, and the composite is $\num{5.5}\times$ more
sensitive to relevance than to eagerness besides. Calibrated against the user's
own \num{73632} consecutive transitions (median step cosine \num{0.261},
\SI{27.0}{\percent} negative), the engine the showcase shipped walks
$\num{2.5}\times$ too tight (median step \num{0.661}) --- ``flat despite lots of
artists'' was many small safe moves --- while the blend champion matches the real
stride but cannot hold a mood, because its InfoNCE projection encodes what
\emph{follows} rather than what \emph{sounds alike}. Three deterministic
retrieval-time controls fix the reachability: a seed anchor, a stride term
(deterministic on purpose --- sampling would break the permalink guarantee), and
a \emph{hard} per-artist cap, which succeeds exactly where the two soft
anti-eager levers failed because it cannot be outscored (on the pathological
playlist \num{59} of the top \num{60} candidates are one artist and the next
artist sits at rank \num{75}, against an MMR diversity budget worth
${\approx}\num{0.078}$ of normalized score; \texttt{eager\_beta} is mis-specified
on an absolute cosine when eagerness is a \emph{ranking} property). The cap
hardens on two disjoint splits and, unexpectedly, \emph{raises} \texttt{music@10}
at every setting: it trades literal for graded relevance. And on \num{40}
identical held-out sessions under paired bootstrap the \textbf{dual-tower family,
closed by three campaigns on recall grounds, is the winner on the stated
objective} --- \texttt{latent/cummean} at anchor \num{0.8} stride \num{0.5} takes
stride error $-\num{0.2545}$, genres $+\num{1.50}$ and drift $+\num{0.0351}$
with every CI clear of zero while \emph{holding} vibe, where the single GRU
($-\num{0.0409}$) and the champion ($-\num{0.1327}$) both pay vibe for the same
stride. The corroboration was already in the record as a \emph{negative}
(\texttt{seq\_dualgru.py:113}: the \texttt{cummean} tower ``added real linear
directions (CKA \num{0.458}) but zero next-item concepts tower A lacked'') ---
dead weight for next-track prediction is exactly session state for mood-holding,
though the stronger reading is refuted: \texttt{cummean} does \emph{not} hold a
vibe natively (\num{0.236} against \num{0.234}), it only hosts one once the
controls are applied. It is deployed as the showcase's default engine; and
\texttt{ground} straddles zero for every arm, so what is established is journey
\emph{shape}, not next-track truth, on one split, one seed and \num{40}
sessions, with nothing yet listened to and the primary metric unchanged.
