\begin{abstract}
This document is the living synthesis of the \emph{next-track} sequence
recommendation campaigns; the markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/}
(filenames containing \texttt{nexttrack}) are the primary record. The task is
next-item \emph{retrieval}: given a listening \emph{session} (an ordered
sequence of tracks), rank the catalog and recover the next distinct track the
user actually played, scored on a single leak-free chronological
\emph{next-distinct} split (\num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items;
\num{5723} train / \num{1431} test; cold-item rate \num{0.55}). The headline
metric is Recall@10, reported beside mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and the
graded-relevance diagnostics artist@10 / genre@10 / artist\_mrr. Seven campaigns
are consolidated. A session GRU alone only \emph{ties} the first-order Markov
co-listening bar (R@10 \num{0.1069}) on Recall@10 and loses on MRR, and neither
loss-tuning nor any network topology (depth, width, residual, direction) beats
it. The breakthrough is \emph{rank fusion}: an R+M z-blend (GRU $\times$ Markov)
beats the bar by $\sim$$+61\%$ Recall@10 (\num{0.1726}, three-seed confirmed),
because the two legs rank \emph{different} correct tracks; a learned XGB stacker
underperforms the fixed z-blend and adding redundant neural legs does not help.
The one genuinely additive new signal is \emph{content}: an item-representation
sweep found latent \emph{dimension} (not the compressor) to be the lever, with a
peak at PCA-192, and on that rich space a three-leg R+M+C z-blend
(GRU $\times$ Markov $\times$ content-kNN) is the current best-on-record row at
Recall@10 \num{0.1859}, MRR \num{0.1025} (paired $\Delta$ over the prior
champion $+\num{0.0133}$, CI above zero). Two caveats bound the crown --- the
reproduction confirm had zero spread because the trainer is deterministic (it
verified determinism, not seed-robustness), and the paired-$\Delta$ lower bound
clears zero by only $+\num{0.0007}$ on a single split. All ranking predictors
here are train-only and off-server, so a confirmed win is a best-on-record
\emph{row}, not a promoted model, and the prior champion R+M on AE-64 remains
the productization anchor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
This document is the living synthesis of the next-track sequence-recommendation
campaigns; the markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/} are the primary
record. Each section transcribes (never recomputes) the quantitative results of
a campaign, preserves its run and dataset identifiers verbatim for
traceability, and adds the rationale and interpretation the terse reports omit.
This instance was cloned from a sibling taste-fit / rotation lab and retargeted
to a sequence ranking goal; the rotation campaigns are a separate line of work
and are \emph{not} covered here.

\paragraph{Task and split.} The lab predicts, within a listening
\emph{session}, which track the user plays \emph{next} --- a next-item
retrieval task. The model consumes the ordered session prefix and scores every
catalog item; candidates are ranked and the held-out next \emph{distinct} track
is the target (a leave-last-out evaluation that excludes prefix items, matching
the Markov parity convention). All work runs on one sequence artifact,
\dsid{seq-20260713-014226} (\num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items), split
\emph{chronologically by session start} into \num{5723} train / \num{1431} test
at cut 2024-08-24. The defining property of this split is its \textbf{cold-item
rate of \num{0.55}}: more than half of test-session target tracks never appear
in any training session. This is the leak-free forward reality, and it caps
absolute recall by construction --- every result is read against the incumbent
baseline bar, never an absolute number.

\paragraph{Metrics.} The headline metric is \textbf{Recall@10} (R@10, the
fraction of test sessions whose held-out next track appears in the top ten
retrieved), reported beside \textbf{mean reciprocal rank} (MRR, the average
inverse rank of the true next track) and Recall@20. Since 2026-07-14 the
evaluation also emits first-class \emph{graded-relevance} metrics over the same
prefix-excluded ranking (Section~\ref{sec:graded}): \textbf{artist@$k$} (a
top-$k$ candidate shares the true next track's artist), \textbf{genre@$k$}
(likewise on primary genre), and \textbf{artist\_mrr} (MRR of the first
same-artist candidate). All confidence intervals are bootstrap 95\% CIs over
\num{2000} resamples of the \num{1431} test sessions, and the decisive
statistic is the \emph{paired} per-session $\Delta$ against a named reference.
A board \emph{win} requires beating the reference on \emph{both} R@10 and MRR
beyond non-overlapping marginal CIs (the strict bar), then a reproduction
confirm; the paired-$\Delta$ CI-entirely-above-zero test is the more powerful
alternative statistic (it crowned both champions) and can disagree with the
strict bar. The graded metrics are the honest scorecard: exact recall is modest
by construction, but the champion lands the right \emph{artist} in the top ten
$\sim$32\% of the time and the right \emph{genre} $\sim$40\% --- far more useful
than the $\sim$17\% exact number the cold-item rate imposes.

\paragraph{The standing champion and the productization anchor.} The current
best-on-record \emph{row} is a three-leg rank-fusion blend, \emph{R+M+C} (a
session GRU $\times$ a first-order Markov co-occurrence model $\times$ a
content-kNN leg) on a PCA-192 item space, at R@10 \num{0.1859}, MRR
\num{0.1025} (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}). The prior champion, the two-leg
\emph{R+M} z-blend on the 64-dim song-autoencoder space (AE-64), at R@10
\num{0.1726} / MRR \num{0.0963} (Section~\ref{sec:blend}), remains the
\emph{productization anchor}: it is the two-leg blend that maps directly onto
the sibling app's transition slot, and it stays the deployable model until the
three-leg blend is registered as a predictor. \textbf{No ranking predictor here
is best-models-promotable}: they are train-only (no \texttt{predict}
subcommand), and R+M+C is an off-server fusion driver, so a confirmed win is a
best-on-record row, not a promoted model.

\section{Background: the sequence task, the baselines, and the metrics}
\label{sec:background}
This section gives the minimum theory for a reader who knows machine learning
but not this repo. It is keyed to the project's living facts file and grows only
when a new predictor family or metric enters the reports.

\paragraph{The models and baselines in play.}
\begin{description}
  \item[Session GRU / LSTM (\texttt{seq-nexttrack}).] A recurrent neural network
  that reads the ordered session prefix one item at a time (each item
  represented by its item-latent vector) and emits a predicted next-item latent;
  candidates are ranked by cosine similarity to that prediction. The GRU (gated
  recurrent unit) and LSTM (long short-term memory) are the two recurrent cells
  tried. The recurrence captures \emph{session context} --- the trajectory of
  the listening session --- rather than a single previous item.
  \item[Training loss: InfoNCE versus cosine.] The sequence model can be trained
  to regress the next-item latent by \emph{cosine} loss (pull the prediction
  toward the true next latent) or by \textbf{InfoNCE}, a contrastive objective
  that pulls the prediction toward the true next item and pushes it away from a
  batch of negatives. InfoNCE is the single most important lever in the whole
  arc: it is $\approx 2.3\times$ cosine on Recall@10, because ranking retrieval
  rewards a representation that \emph{separates} the true next item from
  distractors, which a contrastive loss optimizes directly and a pointwise
  regression does not.
  \item[First-order Markov co-occurrence (the bar, \texttt{B2}).] A train-only
  transition model: a \texttt{track\_bigram} counting ``what track follows what''
  with artist/genre back-off for unseen pairs (the transition term the sibling
  app already ships). It directly \emph{memorizes} adjacency, which is exactly
  what exact next-track ranking rewards, and it is the reference bar every arm is
  measured against. A second-order Markov (trigram) back-off was also tried and
  is essentially neutral --- sessions are too short/sparse for a trigram to fire,
  so it falls through to the bigram.
  \item[item2vec.] A skip-gram (SGNS) co-listening embedding trained on the
  \emph{train} sessions only (a leak-safe analogue of word2vec, where ``words''
  are tracks and ``sentences'' are sessions); candidates are ranked by cosine to
  the mean-pooled prefix. On \num{5723} train sessions it is too sparse to
  localize and is the weakest leg on record; the full-history co-listening
  vectors are deliberately \emph{not} used because they leak across the split.
  \item[content-kNN.] A training-free retrieval leg: cosine of each candidate's
  item latent to the prefix latents (max-aggregated). It is weak standalone on
  exact recall but captures \emph{vibe} similarity the counts miss (its genre@10
  already edges the Markov bar), and it is the one genuinely additive third leg
  for the fusion blend.
  \item[Rank-fusion z-blend versus a learned stacker.] Two ways to combine legs.
  The \textbf{z-blend} normalizes each leg's candidate scores (z-score) and takes
  a weighted sum ($\alpha\cdot z(\text{leg})+\dots$) --- a fixed, parameter-light
  fuser. The \textbf{XGB stacker} is a gradient-boosted meta-learner over
  per-candidate features (leg scores $+$ popularity $+$ recency $+$
  artist/genre-match), trained pointwise with sampled negatives. Across two
  campaigns the fixed z-blend \emph{beats} the learned stacker decisively,
  because the stacker's pointwise binary training does not optimize the ranking
  objective as well as directly fusing normalized scores.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Item-latent spaces: song-AE versus PCA, and the wrong-collection
trap.} The sequence model predicts \emph{into}, and retrieves \emph{over}, a
per-item latent space, so the choice of that space is a first-class axis. Three
families appear:
\begin{description}
  \item[AE-64 (\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_song\_ae}).] A 64-dim nonlinear
  \emph{song-autoencoder} latent, the original champion item space. An
  autoencoder compresses each item by reconstructing it through a bottleneck;
  its equal-weight-per-block reconstruction loss spreads capacity evenly across
  content, acoustic and catalog blocks.
  \item[PCA-$k$ (\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_song\_pca$k$}).] A \emph{linear} principal
  components compression at dim $k \in \{32,64,128,192,256\}$, which orders
  dimensions by explained variance and decorrelates them. As
  Sections~\ref{sec:compression}--\ref{sec:dim} show, this variance-ordered,
  decorrelated geometry is \emph{easier} for the GRU to regress into than the
  AE's entangled equal-weight latents, so PCA beats the AE at every dim $\ge 64$.
  \item[The wrong-collection trap.] Both of the above are fit \emph{only} from
  the leak-safe \dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content} source (intrinsic
  content/acoustic/catalog fields; no behavioral or target field). The default
  \dsid{spotify\_tracks} 200-dim collection is a \emph{behavioral} co-listening
  embedding that soft-leaks replay behavior and must \textbf{never} be used as an
  item space.
\end{description}

\section{The sequence sweep: a GRU-infonce ties the Markov bar}
\label{sec:sweep}
\emph{Rationale.} The instance's opening campaign asked the founding question:
does a session-context sequence model --- a GRU/LSTM over the ordered prefix,
predicting the next track's 64-dim song-AE latent and retrieving by cosine ---
beat the first-order Markov transition model the sibling app already ships,
\emph{forward}, on the leak-free session split? Four arms crossed the recurrent
cell (GRU, LSTM), capacity (\texttt{hidden} 128, 256) and, decisively, the
training loss (cosine versus InfoNCE), at 60 epochs.

\emph{Results.} No arm beat the bar. The best arm (M3, GRU \texttt{hidden}128
InfoNCE) reached a statistical \emph{tie} with the Markov bar on R@10
(\num{0.0894}, paired-$\Delta$ CI includes zero) but \emph{lost} on MRR
(\num{0.0388} versus \num{0.0694}) and Recall@20. The dominant lever was the
loss objective: InfoNCE was $\approx 2.3\times$ cosine on R@10 (\num{0.0894}
versus \num{0.0391}), while capacity (\texttt{h256}) and the LSTM cell barely
moved the cosine arms (all $\sim$0.038--0.041). The popularity and recency
floors sat near zero (R@10 \num{0.0007}).

\emph{Interpretation.} A latent-plus-single-cosine-hop is a harder route to the
\emph{exact} next track than a co-listening bigram: the bigram directly
memorizes ``what follows what,'' while the GRU must land near the right point in
a 64-d content space and then retrieve, losing on precise ranking (MRR). The
finding is consistent with a taste-drift reality --- \num{0.55} cold items,
identity/adjacency-driven behavior --- and it set the agenda: InfoNCE is the
base loss, and the natural next arm is a \emph{blend} of the GRU's candidate
scores with the Markov bigram.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-sequence-sweep.md}.}

\section{Topology scan: depth, width and direction are a spent lever}
\label{sec:topology}
\emph{Rationale.} With the loss fixed at InfoNCE, this scan isolated network
\emph{topology}: does depth (stacked layers), width (\texttt{hidden} 256), an
inter-layer residual stack or bidirectionality lift the sequence model over the
Markov bar? Because a bidirectional RNN under per-step teacher forcing would leak
future items, the bidirectional arm used a leak-free last-item objective (encode
the full prefix, predict only the held-out next item).

\emph{Results.} Refuted. No arm beat the bar on both metrics. The best arm
(width \texttt{h256}, R@10 \num{0.0957}) is a \emph{tie} on R@10 and loses on
MRR (\num{0.0411} versus \num{0.0694}). Depth is flat 1$\to$2 layers
(\num{0.0894}$\to$\num{0.0922}, in-band) and \emph{hurts} at 3 layers
(\num{0.0825}, out-of-band below the bar); the residual stack over depth-2/h256
added nothing. The bidirectional arm collapsed (\num{0.0482}), but this is an
\emph{objective} artifact, not a directionality verdict: the leak-free last-item
objective supplies $\sim$1 training target per session versus teacher forcing's
$\sim$13 next-step pairs, so it trained on $\sim$13$\times$ less signal.

\emph{Interpretation.} Architecture is not the lever for next-track on this
corpus. The transferable latent-retrieval signal caps at R@10 $\sim$0.09--0.10
across depth, width, residual and direction, below the co-listening bigram ---
consistent with the whole arc that the loss objective moved the needle and
capacity/topology do not. The one remaining unexplored lever was the candidate
framing / fusion, which the next campaign pulled decisively.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-topology-scan.md}.}

\section{The GRU$\times$Markov fusion champion, and why more ensembling fails}
\label{sec:blend}
\emph{Rationale.} The sweep and the topology scan agreed that the best single
GRU only \emph{ties} the Markov bar on R@10 while losing on MRR. This campaign
asked whether \emph{combining} models beats the bar, and how far ensembling
goes. Three legs were defined, each emitting full-vocab candidate scores:
\textbf{M} = the train-only Markov bigram (the bar), \textbf{R} = the
GRU-infonce-h256 recurrence, and \textbf{A} = an ANN bag-of-prefix MLP (a
feed-forward twin of R). They were combined by a weighted z-blend and, as a
learned alternative, an XGB stacker.

\emph{Results.} The \textbf{R+M z-blend} ($\alpha{=}0.5$) \emph{beats the bar
decisively}: R@10 \num{0.1726} $[0.154, 0.192]$, MRR \num{0.0963}, a paired
$\Delta$ over Markov of $+\num{0.0657}$ $[+0.049, +0.083]$ (CI entirely above
zero) --- $\sim$$+61\%$ R@10, $+39\%$ MRR --- on a robust plateau across
$\alpha \in [0.25, 0.75]$ and reproduced across three seeds (\num{0.1705} /
\num{0.1726} / \num{0.1726}). It was registered as the \texttt{seq-blend}
predictor and the \texttt{blend-gru-markov} definition, the first next-track
champion. Further ensembling then \emph{failed} to improve on it
(Table~\ref{tab:blend}): R+A+M (\num{0.1684}) is slightly worse, R+A ($\approx$
GRU alone, \num{0.0894}) is much worse, and both XGB stacker variants (M+R
\num{0.1020}; M+R+A \num{0.0887}) barely clear Markov alone.

\emph{Interpretation.} The win is about signal \emph{diversity}, not model
count. The GRU (latent similarity) and Markov (co-occurrence counts) rank
\emph{different} correct next-tracks, so fusing them roughly doubles the hit
rate; piling on more of the \emph{same} kind of signal --- another neural latent
predictor (A), redundant with R --- adds nothing. The learned XGB stacker
\emph{underperforms} the simple z-blend, counter to the usual ``stacking beats
blending'' intuition: its pointwise binary-with-negatives training does not
optimize the ranking objective as well as directly fusing normalized scores. The
remaining headroom is bounded by the \num{0.55} cold-item rate and requires
\emph{genuinely new signal}, not more model fusion --- the hypothesis the next
campaign tests.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-14-nexttrack-blend-and-ensemble.md}.}

\section{Graded-relevance metrics and new base learners: content is the only additive signal}
\label{sec:graded}
\emph{Rationale.} Two threads in one campaign. (A) Make the evaluation credit
landing the right \emph{band/vibe} even when the exact track is wrong --- the
observation that ``the first choice is usually not the exact track, but the
artist is usually right'' --- by promoting \emph{artist@k}, \emph{genre@k} and
\emph{artist\_mrr} to first-class metrics wired end-to-end
(\texttt{seq\_common.eval\_from\_scores} $\to$ Rust \texttt{Metrics} /
\texttt{MetricsSpec} $\to$ \texttt{domain.toml} $\to$ UI). (B) Try genuinely new
leak-free base learners --- \textbf{C} = content-kNN, \textbf{M2} = a
second-order Markov trigram with back-off, and \textbf{I} = a train-only
item2vec SGNS --- scored on both the exact and the new graded metrics.

\emph{Results.} The exact-metric path was byte-identical (purely additive keys);
the champion reproduced R@10 \num{0.1726} / MRR \num{0.0963} exactly. No new base
learner beat the bar standalone (M2 \num{0.1076} $\approx$ M \num{0.1069} --- the
trigram falls through to the bigram; item2vec \num{0.0517} is the weakest leg).
Blended into the champion, \textbf{content is the only additive signal}:
\textbf{R+M+C} (adding the content leg) is the top blend on MRR
(\num{0.0963}$\to$\num{0.1014}), artist@10 (\num{0.3215}$\to$\num{0.3298}) and
artist\_mrr, and ties the champion on exact R@10 (paired $\Delta$
$+\num{0.0014}$, CI straddles zero). Item2vec \emph{drags} the blend (R+M+I
\num{0.1349}); the trigram is neutral.

\emph{Interpretation.} Content-kNN ranks \emph{different} near-correct tracks
(latent vibe similarity) than the GRU (session recurrence) and Markov
(co-occurrence), exactly the diversity the fusion campaign predicted new signal
would need. On the AE-64 space the content leg only \emph{tied} the champion on
exact recall while lifting the graded metrics, so it was carried forward as the
graded-preferred third leg, not a new exact champion. That ``tie'' becomes a
\emph{win} once the item space is richer (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}): on PCA-192
the same R+M+C construction is the strongest on record. Co-listening item2vec and
the trigram Markov added nothing on this data.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-14-nexttrack-graded-relevance-and-new-legs.md}.}

\section{The item-representation compression bake-off}
\label{sec:compression}
\emph{Rationale.} The champion's item space --- a 64-dim AE latent --- was one
arbitrary early choice, and the sequence GRU predicts \emph{into} and retrieves
\emph{over} whatever geometry that space imposes. This campaign asks which
\emph{compression} is the best space for the GRU: linear PCA versus the
nonlinear autoencoder, each at latent dim 32 / 64 / 128. Six alternative spaces
were baked from the \emph{same} leak-safe 384-dim content source
(\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content}), each fit unsupervised over all \num{23529}
items using only intrinsic content/acoustic/catalog fields, then scored under a
\emph{frozen} GRU readout (\texttt{gru-infonce-h256}), so the item latent space
is the only axis that moves. The design bet was that the AE's
equal-weight-per-block reconstruction loss would \emph{preserve} the small
acoustic/catalog blocks that PCA's text-dominated global variance would starve;
the null was that dim, not the compressor, sets the ceiling.

\emph{Results.} The parity gate passed: the AE-64 control reproduced its
on-record R@10 \num{0.0957}. All six spaces trained cleanly
(Table~\ref{tab:compression}; Figure~\ref{fig:compression}). \textbf{PCA-128} is
the single-GRU winner at R@10 \num{0.1146}, a paired $\Delta$ of $+\num{0.0189}$
$[+0.0042, +0.0335]$ over the AE-64 control --- a CI \emph{entirely above zero},
the only arm that clears the control significantly --- and it also tops MRR,

The single-GRU win does \textbf{not} survive into the production blend:

compressor is secondary. The design's acoustic-preservation hypothesis is
\emph{refuted}: at generous dim the linear PCA beats the nonlinear AE, and

the bottom. What helps is a \emph{variance-ordered, decorrelated} target geometry
that the GRU finds easier to regress into than the AE's entangled equal-weight
latents. The AE's edge at dim 32 is a purely low-capacity phenomenon that
reverses once dim is generous. The flat content-kNN probe that fails to track the

of PCA's target geometry, not a demonstrably richer space. And the load-bearing

the blend, because the Markov leg is representation-independent and the extra

\section{The dimension ceiling: PCA peaks at 192}
\label{sec:dim}
\emph{Rationale.} With dimension established as the lever and PCA still climbing
at 128, this leg extended the PCA sweep upward (PCA-192, PCA-256) to locate the
real optimum before Phase 2 committed a base space, and added AE-192 to test
whether PCA $\ge$ AE holds at higher dimension. The protocol was identical (same
frozen \texttt{gru-infonce-h256} readout, same parity gate); the reference is
PCA-128 (\runid{run-20260715-042100-a1d9d}).

\emph{Results.} The PCA R@10-versus-dimension curve \textbf{peaks at $\sim$192}
(R@10 \num{0.1230}) and \emph{turns over} by 256 (\num{0.1167}); it was not still
climbing. The full trend is $32\!\to\!\num{0.041}$, $64\!\to\!\num{0.104}$,
$128\!\to\!\num{0.115}$, $\mathbf{192\!\to\!\num{0.123}}$, $256\!\to\!\num{0.117}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:dim}). PCA-192 is the point-optimum, but its paired $\Delta$
versus PCA-128 is $+\num{0.0084}$ $[-0.0007, +0.0189]$ --- it misses the ``CI
entirely above zero'' bar by \num{0.0007}, a whisker --- so \textbf{PCA-128
stands as the Phase-2 base}, with PCA-192 flagged as the confirm-and-promote
candidate. PCA $\ge$ AE holds strongly and the gap is widest at 192 (PCA-192
\num{0.123} versus AE-192 \num{0.098}); the AE plateaus flat at $\sim$0.09--0.10
from dim 64 upward.

\emph{Interpretation.} Extra variance past $\sim$192 (PCA-256 captures cumulative
explained-variance ratio \num{0.997}) is low-signal / noise dimensions the GRU
cannot exploit and that slightly dilute the target geometry. The AE's flat
plateau reconfirms the refuted acoustic-preservation hypothesis at higher
dimension: its equal-weight reconstruction objective keeps spending capacity on
the tiny numeric/acoustic blocks (reconstruction $R^2 \approx 0.999$ at 192)
rather than the high-variance text/catalog structure the GRU needs. As before,
the training-free content-kNN probe stays flat and does not reproduce the GRU's
peak, so the dimension optimum is about target \emph{learnability}, not intrinsic
retrieval richness.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-15b-nexttrack-compression-dim-extension.md}.}

\section{The model-family sweep and the new champion, R+M+C on PCA-192}
\label{sec:phase2}
\emph{Rationale.} With the compression axis settled (PCA-128 base, PCA-192
candidate), this campaign swept the model \emph{family} on the chosen spaces:
does a different recurrent cell (LSTM), a non-recurrent pooled MLP (ANN), or a
\emph{learned} XGB stacker beat the fixed GRU$\times$Markov z-blend --- and does
adding the content-kNN third leg (R+M+C) on the \emph{rich} PCA space finally
beat the champion? A frozen held config ran across all arms; only the model
family and the representation varied, and the server was serialized
(\texttt{-{}-max-runs 1}) to avoid a thread-oversubscription throughput trap.

\emph{Results.} A \textbf{new best-on-record row}: the \textbf{R+M+C z-blend on
PCA-192} (GRU $\times$ train-only Markov $\times$ content-kNN, equal thirds)
reaches R@10 \num{0.1859} $[0.166, 0.207]$, MRR \num{0.1025}, a paired $\Delta$
over the prior champion of $+\num{0.0133}$ $[+0.0007, +0.0259]$ --- entirely
above zero, with no MRR regression (Table~\ref{tab:phase2};
Figure~\ref{fig:phase2}). The content leg is genuinely additive on the rich
space, beating same-space R+M by $+\num{0.0154}$ (PCA-128) / $+\num{0.0147}$
(PCA-192), both CIs above zero --- \emph{stronger} than the AE-64 R+M+C that
only tied (Section~\ref{sec:graded}), because the richer, higher-EVR space makes
the content leg pay off. The learned \textbf{XGB stacker badly underperforms} the
fixed z-blend (\num{0.080}--\num{0.087}, losing even to its own single GRU base
leg); the \textbf{LSTM is the best single model} (PCA-128 \num{0.1272}, paired
$\Delta$ over the GRU $+\num{0.0126}$, CI above zero) but far below the blends;
and the Phase-2 single base \textbf{stays PCA-128}.

\emph{The crown, and its two honest caveats.} The board-event candidate was
confirmed by reproducing the GRU-192 leg five times (the original plus four
reruns) and recomputing R+M+C each time; the paired $\Delta$ over the prior
champion was $+\num{0.0133}$ $[+0.0007, +0.0259]$ \emph{every} rerun, with the
MRR lead intact, so R+M+C on PCA-192 was recorded as the new champion row. Two
caveats travel with the crown and must not be omitted. First, \textbf{the
reproduction spread was exactly zero} because \texttt{seq-nexttrack} training is
deterministic on this CPU --- all five GRU-192 checkpoints were byte-identical
--- so the confirm verified \emph{determinism, not seed-robustness}; the only
uncertainty quantified is the single-split bootstrap. Second, \textbf{the paired
$\Delta$ lower bound is $+\num{0.0007}$}: the crown clears zero by a whisker on a
single split. The crown is real by the pre-agreed criterion, but the margin over
the prior champion is thin, and hardening it needs a second leak-free test split
(or seed-varied legs once the blend is generalized to three legs).

\emph{Interpretation.} The compression-plus-model-family thread is otherwise
closed: PCA-128 is the single-model base, the LSTM is the best single model, the
learned stacker is dead as a combiner, and the fixed z-blend is the combiner of
record. The load-bearing result is that \emph{content is the one leg that adds
genuinely new ranking signal, and it pays off more on the higher-EVR PCA space}
--- the exception to the standing rule that single-model / representation gains
do not carry to the blend (the R+M pair on the rich PCA spaces only ties the
AE-64 champion). The productization gap remains: the champion is train-only and
off-server, so the prior champion R+M on AE-64 stays the deployable anchor until
the three-leg blend is registered with a \texttt{predict} subcommand.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-15c-nexttrack-phase2-model-sweep.md}.}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Seven campaigns (Jul 2026) on the next-track retrieval task converge on a clear
story. (1) A session sequence model, even at its best (GRU-infonce), only
\emph{ties} the first-order Markov co-listening bar on Recall@10 and loses on
MRR: predicting a next-item latent and retrieving by one cosine hop is a harder
route to the exact next track than a bigram that directly memorizes adjacency.
(2) The training \emph{loss} is the dominant single-model lever (InfoNCE
$\approx 2.3\times$ cosine); network \emph{topology} --- depth, width, residual,
direction --- is a spent lever, and no architecture beats the bar. (3) The
breakthrough is \emph{rank fusion}: an R+M z-blend of the GRU and Markov legs
beats the bar by $\sim$$+61\%$ Recall@10 (three-seed confirmed), because the two
legs rank \emph{different} correct tracks. The win is about signal
\emph{diversity}, not model count --- adding a redundant neural leg does not
help, and a learned XGB stacker \emph{underperforms} the fixed z-blend, because
its pointwise training does not capture the ranking objective. (4) An
item-representation sweep found latent \emph{dimension}, not the compressor, to
be the lever: linear PCA beats the nonlinear autoencoder at every dim $\ge 64$
(the acoustic-preservation hypothesis is refuted), the curve peaks at PCA-192 and
turns over by 256, and PCA-128 is the confirmed single-model base. (5) On the
rich PCA space the one genuinely additive new signal --- \emph{content-kNN},
which only \emph{tied} the champion on the AE-64 space --- becomes a win: a
three-leg R+M+C z-blend on PCA-192 is the current best-on-record row at Recall@10
\num{0.1859}, MRR \num{0.1025} (paired $\Delta$ over the prior champion
$+\num{0.0133}$, CI above zero, confirmed across five reproductions). (6) Two
honest caveats bound the crown: the reproduction confirm had zero spread because
\texttt{seq-nexttrack} is deterministic on this CPU (so it verified determinism,
not seed-robustness), and the paired-$\Delta$ lower bound clears zero by only
$+\num{0.0007}$ on a single split --- hardening it needs a second leak-free test
split. (7) Every ranking predictor here is train-only and off-server, so the
crown is a best-on-record \emph{row}, not a promoted, app-exportable model; the
prior champion R+M on AE-64 remains the productization anchor, and registering a
three-leg blend with a \texttt{predict} subcommand is the standing top follow-up.
