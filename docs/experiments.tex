\begin{abstract}
This document is the living synthesis of the taste-fit prediction campaigns;
the markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/} are the primary record. The
active target is a binary \emph{rotation} signal --- did a track earn a repeat
listen, $\text{play\_count}\ge 2$ --- predicted from a track's \emph{intrinsic
metadata} alone (genre, artist, artist/track popularity, follower count,
release year, release type) over the user's listening corpus. The headline
metric is the area under the ROC curve (AUC); the engagement-magnitude
regression target that the lab opened on ($\text{play\_count}\times
\text{completion\_ratio}$, $\log1p$-transformed) is retained as a documented
scientific field but is no longer delivered. The behavioral co-listening
embedding and all playback/curation fields are deliberately excluded from the
feature set. Five Jun-2026 campaigns are folded in below; they converge on a
single finding --- categorical artist/genre/album identity read by
gradient-boosted trees beats every dense modality and every dense
distance/margin family --- and they crowned the project's first registered,
promotable champion, \texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation}, at AUC
\num{0.7429} on the original DS-META corpus. Three Jun-12 campaigns then
re-ran the family question on a larger 2026 corpus (a full-64-d,
\num{23529}-row dataset): a four-family classifier bake-off in which
\texttt{xgboost-classifier} again wins (AUC \num{0.7204}), \texttt{catboost}
follows in-band, and a dense \texttt{pyramid-mlp} is last; a six-geometry
architecture scan that closes the dense-net family for AUC by \emph{refuting}
the budget hypothesis; and a topology bake-off of a new Rust/\texttt{burn}
\texttt{burn-deep-classifier} that, by feeding the identity one-hots
\emph{raw}, repairs the dense net's calibration completely (log-loss
$2.0\to 0.62$) and lifts its AUC $\sim$2 bands to \num{0.7038} --- yet still
lands $\sim$1--2 bands short of the tree tier, with learned entity embeddings
starved by the low identity cardinality. A seventh campaign then closes that
thread: a paired re-run of the topology bake-off on a \emph{higher}
identity-cardinality matrix (artist cap $60\to300$, genre $40\to120$) flips the
ranking --- \texttt{embeddings} now \emph{beats} \texttt{mlp} (a $+0.0130$ swing
that reverses the prior result, confirming the starvation hypothesis) --- and
lifts the net into the in-dataset tree tier (AUC \num{0.7192}, within $\sim$1
band of \texttt{catboost}), the first ANN to clear the rotation save bar, saved
as \texttt{burn-deep-embeddings-highvocab}. An eighth campaign then probes the inverse lever and refutes it: the classic Facebook GBDT$\to$LR trick generalized to GBDT$\to$MLP --- training a fresh head on the frozen champion tree's one-hot leaf indices --- caps \num{1.8}--\num{2.4} noise bands \emph{below} the bare xgboost champion (best head AUC \num{0.6978}) with badly broken calibration, a seventh convergent confirmation that learned representations layered on the trees do not help. A ninth campaign then probes how far the standing identity-resolution lever can go: a vocab-cap scan on the bare GBDT, holding the frozen champion config and moving \emph{only} the artist/genre one-hot cardinality, finds the lever \emph{largely spent} --- the first jump ($60/40\to300/120$) carries the gain, and past 300/120 each step pays only sub-band returns; xgboost reaches a new in-lineage single-split best AUC \num{0.7349} at 1000/300 (only $+0.005$, $\sim$0.4 band, over the anchor --- no save fired), \texttt{catboost} tracks it, and the full-vocabulary point could not be built (host memory ceiling). Section~\ref{sec:anns} consolidates
why artificial neural nets structurally underperform on this one-hot-heavy
feature matrix, how the representation-aware net resolves the diagnosis, and how
enough identity resolution finally lets the embedding topology pay off. A parallel line of work --- \emph{next-track} sequence recommendation, scored on a leak-free session split by Recall@10 --- is also consolidated (Sections~\ref{sec:nexttrack}--\ref{sec:nexttrack-phase2}): a GRU$\times$Markov rank-fusion blend beats the first-order co-listening bar by $\sim$$+61\%$ R@10, and adding a content-kNN third leg on a rich PCA-192 item space (R+M+C) is the current best-on-record row at R@10 \num{0.1859}. Its task, metrics and champion are unrelated to the rotation boards and must never be read against them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This document is the living synthesis of the experiment campaigns; the
markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/} are the primary record. Each section
transcribes (never recomputes) the quantitative results of a campaign,
preserves its run and dataset identifiers verbatim for traceability, and adds
the rationale and interpretation that the terse reports omit.

\paragraph{Task and target.} The lab predicts \emph{taste fit} from a track's
intrinsic metadata over the user's listening corpus. It opened on an
engagement-magnitude regression target ---
$\text{play\_count}\times\text{completion\_ratio}$, trained against a
$\log1p$-transform and mapped back with $\text{expm1}$ --- but on 2026-06-08
the active target pivoted to a binary \emph{rotation} signal:
$\text{play\_count}\ge 2$, i.e.\ did the track earn a repeat listen
(Section~\ref{sec:rotation-pivot}). Rotation is the actionable input to the
Playlist Pathfinder's per-track desirability term, it is far more learnable
than engagement magnitude (trees reach $\text{AUC}\approx 0.73$ on rotation
versus \rsq{}$\approx 0.3$ on engagement), and with a base rate near
\SI{44.5}{\percent} it is naturally spread for path search. Engagement is
retained as a documented scientific field, but rotation is the delivered
target and every leaderboard below is on it.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Because the target is binary, the headline metric is the
area under the ROC curve (AUC, higher-better), the quantity the
classification leaderboard (Table~\ref{tab:lb-auc}) sorts on and the
best-models group auto-promotes by; it is reported alongside log-loss,
accuracy and the Brier score. Two distinct metric regimes nonetheless coexist
in the rotation campaigns. The campaigns opened with a \emph{regression} read
--- mean absolute error (MAE), RMSE and \rsq{} on the $[0,1]$ P(rotation)
scale --- and only later adopted the honest \emph{classification} read once
the framework gained a binary mode. MAE is retained on the historical
regression board (Table~\ref{tab:lb-mae}) for same-family band significance,
but it is documented there as \emph{degenerate} on a binary target: an
$\epsilon$-tube SVR can post a deceptively low MAE while being worse than
predicting the mean (Section~\ref{sec:svm}), an illusion the classification
metrics dissolve. The absolute-percentage metrics (MAPE/\medape{}) are
undefined on rotation, since half the labels are exactly zero. The historical
engagement board, where MAE \emph{is} the natural headline, is preserved in
\texttt{PROJECT-FACTS.md}; its numbers live on the $\log1p$ engagement scale
and are not comparable to anything below.

\paragraph{Features.} The feature space is a track's \emph{intrinsic metadata}
alone: genre, artist, artist/track popularity, follower count, release year,
credited-artist count and release type. The dense embedding modalities
(co-listening word2vec, content-text, acoustic, and the song-autoencoder
latent of Section~\ref{sec:ae-blend}) are \emph{behavioral or descriptive}
signals, not categorical identity, and the payload-based predictors over the
metadata one-hots are the canonical models here. Critically, the recipe is
\emph{leakage-free}: the playback fields that mechanically constitute or proxy
the target --- \texttt{play\_count}, \texttt{completion\_ratio},
\texttt{skip\_rate}, \texttt{shuffle\_rate}, \texttt{distinct\_sessions}, and
the demoted \texttt{engagement} field itself --- and the behaviorally
entangled curation fields are all excluded by default.

\paragraph{Three feature matrices.} The first three campaigns ran on the
original \emph{DS-META} corpus (\num{12256} rows, metadata-only). The Jun-12
campaigns ran on a larger 2026 corpus, the \emph{full-64-d} dataset
(\dsid{ds-20260609-204419-p64-s42}, \num{23529} rows, 177 columns: 64 song-AE
PCA latent $+$ 7 numeric $+$ 106 identity one-hots at vocab caps artist 60 /
genre 40 --- the \emph{60/40-vocab} matrix). A final Jun-12 campaign
(Section~\ref{sec:anns-highvocab}) re-ran on a \emph{high-vocab} variant
(\dsid{ds-20260612-143014-p64-s42}, \emph{same} \num{23529} rows / split seed 42
/ PCA64, differing \emph{only} in identity cardinality: artist cap $60\to300$,
genre $40\to120$; 497 columns including 426 one-hots --- artist 301, genre 121,
album-type 4). \textbf{These are three non-comparable matrices}: DS-META differs
from the full-64-d corpus in both size (\num{12256} versus \num{23529} rows) and
feature set (metadata-only versus $+$64 AE latent), and the 60/40 and high-vocab
matrices differ in identity cardinality (177 versus 497 columns). AUC numbers
must therefore never be read across them --- not against the \num{0.7429}
DS-META champion, and not between the 60/40 and high-vocab boards. Each matrix
carries its own leaderboard (Tables~\ref{tab:lb-auc},~\ref{tab:lb-auc-p64}
and~\ref{tab:lb-auc-highvocab}); nothing regressed when the 60/40 xgboost lands
at \num{0.7204}, and every family scores \emph{higher} on the wider high-vocab
matrix because the extra identity granularity is genuine signal.

\section{Background: model families and metrics}
\label{sec:background}
This section gives the minimum theory for a reader who knows ML but not this
repo. It is keyed to \texttt{PROJECT-FACTS.md} and grows only when a new
predictor family or metric enters the reports.

\paragraph{The families in play.}
\begin{description}
  \item[Gradient-boosted trees (\texttt{xgboost}, \texttt{xgboost-classifier},
  \texttt{catboost-classifier}, \texttt{ngboost-classifier}).]
  An additive ensemble of shallow trees, each fit to the residual gradient of
  the previous stage. As a \emph{regressor} its output is a leaf average,
  bounded by the range of training targets; as a \emph{classifier} it emits a
  calibrated probability through a logistic link, the form that wins on
  rotation. Trees split on individual columns, so they read a track's sparse
  artist/genre/album identity one-hots \emph{losslessly} and capture non-linear
  interactions without manual feature crosses --- the reason they dominate this
  corpus. \texttt{catboost} is an ordered-boosting GBDT variant with symmetric
  (oblivious) trees and native categorical handling; on this corpus it is the
  legitimate second tree family, co-leader \emph{in-band} with xgboost.
  \texttt{ngboost} is a natural-gradient boosting variant that fits a full
  predictive \emph{distribution}; its probabilistic machinery, paid for with
  shallow base learners, costs it raw ranking power here. The cost of the GBDT
  family generally is a non-deterministic fit (subsampling and column sampling
  carry an internal RNG seed), so two runs at different seeds differ slightly.
  The registered champion \texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation} is this family.
  \item[Logistic regression.] Linear log-odds passed through a sigmoid: a
  deterministic, $\ell_2$-regularized linear classifier. It is the natural
  \emph{linear floor} for a binary target --- the analogue of ridge in the
  regression world --- and serves as a cheap, reproducible control on the
  harness. Being a single linear boundary in a dense feature space, it blurs
  the sparse categorical identity that trees exploit, and it lands well below
  them on rotation.
  \item[Kernel SVM / SVC (\texttt{svc} with rbf / linear / poly / sigmoid
  kernels).] A maximum-margin classifier that operates on kernel-induced
  distances between points. As a \emph{regressor} (SVR) on a binary target the
  $\epsilon$-insensitive tube degenerates --- the source of the binary-MAE trap
  of Section~\ref{sec:svm}; as a proper probability \emph{classifier} (SVC) the
  divergence vanishes but every kernel still loses to the trees, because a
  dense-distance / large-margin model cannot read sparse identity one-hots the
  way an axis-aligned tree split does. The sigmoid kernel is the weakest and is
  the documented refutation echo.
  \item[Pyramid-MLP (\texttt{pyramid-mlp-classifier}).] A feed-forward
  artificial neural network with a tapering (``pyramid'') stack of hidden
  layers, ReLU activations and an Adam optimizer, fit on the StandardScaler-ed
  feature matrix. It is the project's only \emph{dense} (every-input-to-every-
  hidden-unit) learner on rotation. As Section~\ref{sec:anns} develops at
  length, a dense net is exactly the regime that one-hot-heavy taste data
  fights: it must learn dense weights over a mostly-zero input, and it lands
  last in the bake-off with a blown calibration that an architecture scan
  proves is structural, not a budget artifact.
  \item[Representation-aware net (\texttt{burn-deep-classifier}).] A
  full-Rust/\texttt{burn} feed-forward classifier built to repair the
  pyramid-MLP's pathologies. It splits the feature matrix into a
  \emph{standardized} continuous block (64 PCA $+$ 7 numeric) and a set of
  \emph{raw} (un-standardized) one-hot identity groups (artist, genre,
  album-type), so the scaler never turns rare categories into outlier spikes
  (Section~\ref{sec:anns-scaler}). A \texttt{topology} enum selects among a
  plain dense \texttt{mlp} over the concatenated inputs, learned per-group
  \texttt{embeddings}, and a \texttt{wide\_deep} split (a wide linear path over
  the one-hots beside a deep path over the continuous block). As
  Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn} reports, the raw-one-hot representation fixes the
  calibration completely and lifts AUC $\sim$2 bands over the pyramid, but the
  net still sits $\sim$1--2 bands below the tree tier, and the embedding
  variants gain nothing because the identity cardinalities here are too low to
  feed them.
  \item[AE-transplant net (\texttt{burn-ae-classifier}).] A two-phase
  Rust/\texttt{burn} network. Phase 1 pretrains an autoencoder to reconstruct
  the \emph{full} assembled feature vector --- the standardized continuous
  block beside the \emph{raw} identity one-hots, $D{=}177$ --- by mean-squared
  error; Phase 2 transplants the trained encoder into a deep softmax classifier
  (encoder $\to$ latent $\to$ head $\to$ logits) and fine-tunes it end-to-end
  with cross-entropy. A \texttt{freeze\_encoder} flag chooses a fixed feature
  extractor versus fine-tuning. It tests whether an \emph{unsupervised}
  reconstruction pretrain hands the classifier a latent that raw one-hots do
  not already give a from-scratch net; as Section~\ref{sec:anns-ae} reports it
  does not --- the reconstructive latent is task-agnostic, and the family ties
  the plain \texttt{burn-deep} \texttt{mlp} without reaching the tree tier.
  \item[Autoencoder-XGBoost blend (\texttt{autoencoder-xgboost}).] A weighted
  ensemble $\hat{y}=w\cdot\hat{y}_{\text{ae}}+(1-w)\cdot\hat{y}_{\text{xgb}}$
  that combines a regressor over a precomputed 64-dimensional
  song-autoencoder latent with a metadata-XGBoost model, the weight $w$ tuned on
  a train holdout. Its purpose was to test whether the dense AE latent carries
  rotation signal the metadata one-hots miss; it does not, and the tuner
  correctly drives $w\to 0$, collapsing the ensemble to metadata-only XGBoost
  (Section~\ref{sec:ae-blend}).
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why a binary target makes MAE degenerate.} For a continuous target,
mean absolute error is a faithful summary of how far predictions sit from the
truth. On a $\{0,1\}$ target it is not: a model can minimize MAE by hugging the
labels regardless of how it \emph{ranks} or \emph{calibrates} them. An SVR with
an $\epsilon{=}0.1$ tube snaps its outputs to a two-level step near
$\{0.1,0.9\}$, sitting only $\sim 0.1$ off every label --- a low MAE --- while
its ranking is worthless (negative \rsq{}). The metrics that matter for a
binary taste-fit signal are therefore the classification ones:
\begin{description}
  \item[AUC (area under the ROC curve).] The probability that a random positive
  is ranked above a random negative; \num{0.5} is chance, \num{1.0} is perfect.
  It is threshold-free and scale-free, depends only on the \emph{ordering} of
  the predicted probabilities, and is exactly the property the Pathfinder needs
  from its desirability term. It is the headline metric and the leaderboard
  sort key.
  \item[Log-loss.] The negative log-likelihood of the labels under the
  predicted probabilities; it punishes \emph{confident} mistakes severely and so
  rewards calibration, not just ranking.
  \item[Brier score.] The mean squared error between predicted probability and
  the $\{0,1\}$ label --- a calibration-and-refinement measure on the natural
  $[0,1]$ scale, lower-better.
\end{description}
A model that wins on AUC \emph{and} log-loss \emph{and} Brier, as the champion
does, is winning on ranking and calibration at once; the binary-MAE artifact
that flatters a degenerate SVR shows up on none of them. The same triad later
exposes the opposite pathology in a dense net (Section~\ref{sec:anns}): a model
can post a respectable AUC --- genuine ranking --- while its log-loss and Brier
are catastrophic, i.e.\ it ranks acceptably but is \emph{confidently wrong}.

\paragraph{A note on the historical log-target.} The lab's opening regression
target, engagement, is heavy-tailed and non-negative, so it was trained against
a $\log1p$-transform and mapped back with $\text{expm1}$. That transform pair
carries a hazard --- a model unbounded in log-space can produce a large
positive log prediction that $\text{expm1}$ amplifies into an enormous value,
the ``$\text{expm1}$ blow-up'' --- which is why bounded-by-construction families
(trees, rbf kernels) are preferred over unbounded ones (linear models, MLPs) on
that target. Engagement remains a documented field and its leaderboard lives in
\texttt{PROJECT-FACTS.md}, but it is no longer the delivered target, so the
blow-up hazard is now a historical footnote rather than a live design
constraint.

\section{The rotation pivot: from engagement magnitude to a binary taste-fit signal}
\label{sec:rotation-pivot}
On 2026-06-08 the active target pivoted away from engagement magnitude to a
binary \emph{rotation} signal, defined as $\text{play\_count} \ge 2$ --- did
the track earn a repeat listen? Rotation is the actionable input to the
Playlist Pathfinder's \texttt{W\_FIT} term, where a per-track $[0,1]$
desirability biases the A* path; a calibrated rotation probability is a
cleaner \texttt{fit\_norm} than a $\log1p$-then-$\text{expm1}$ engagement
estimate. Rotation also turns out to be far more learnable than engagement
magnitude (gradient-boosted trees reach $\text{AUC}\approx 0.73$ on rotation
versus \rsq{}$\approx 0.3$ on engagement) and is naturally spread for path
search, with a base rate near \SI{44.5}{\percent}. Engagement is retained as a
documented scientific field but is no longer the delivered target.

The pivot is decisive for how every number below must be read. Rotation lives
on the $[0,1]$ scale, so its MAE/\rsq{} are \emph{not} comparable to the
engagement leaderboard preserved in \texttt{PROJECT-FACTS.md}; and because half
the rotation labels are exactly zero, the absolute-percentage metrics
(MAPE/\medape{}) are undefined for those rows. Two distinct metric regimes
therefore coexist in the rotation campaigns: an early \emph{regression} read
(MAE, RMSE, \rsq{}, units P(rotation)) and the later, honest
\emph{classification} read (AUC, log-loss, accuracy, Brier). As the campaigns
below show, the choice of metric is not cosmetic --- under MAE a kernel SVR can
\emph{look} competitive while being worse than predicting the mean, an illusion
that the classification metrics dissolve.

\section{Rotation leaderboards}
\label{sec:leaderboard}
Four leaderboards. The first two are on the original DS-META corpus, one per
metric regime; the third and fourth are on the larger 2026 full-64-d corpus.
\textbf{None of the three AUC boards is comparable to the others} --- they sit
on three non-comparable feature matrices (DS-META metadata-only; the full-64-d
\emph{60/40-vocab} matrix; and the full-64-d \emph{high-vocab} matrix, which
shares the 60/40 corpus, rows and split but raises the identity cardinality
caps). Each board carries its own in-dataset references and must never be read
across (see the Introduction's ``Three feature matrices'' note). The DS-META

high-vocab board carries the first ANN to clear the rotation save bar

(train-on-earlier, test-on-later) split tells a sharply different story --- the

\section{Campaigns}

\subsection{Autoencoder-latent + XGBoost blend: a dead modality, isolated}
\label{sec:ae-blend}
\emph{Rationale.} This was the first campaign on the pivoted rotation target.
The hypothesis was that a precomputed 64-dimensional song-autoencoder latent
(from the Qdrant collection \texttt{spotify\_tracks\_song\_ae}) carries a
track's \emph{identity} in a way the metadata one-hots do not, and that
blending an AE-latent regressor with a metadata-XGBoost model ---
$\hat{y}=w\cdot\hat{y}_{\text{ae}}+(1-w)\cdot\hat{y}_{\text{xgb}}$, with the
weight $w$ tuned on a train holdout --- could beat metadata-XGBoost alone. All
six arms ran on \dsid{ds-20260608-204402-p64-s42} (64 AE latent + 114 metadata
$=$ 178 cols, 12{,}256 rows, 9{,}805/2{,}451 at seed 42), varying only the AE
branch (kNN at $k\in\{10,25,50,100,200\}$, plus a ridge-on-latent arm).

\emph{Results.} All six runs (\runid{run-20260609-131545-e34e4} and siblings)
succeeded, and all returned byte-identical blended metrics --- MAE 0.38789,
\rsq{} 0.191, RMSE 0.4475 --- because the weight tuner drove $w\to 0$ in every
arm. The only axis that moved was the AE branch's standalone error
$mae_{\text{ae}}$, which grew monotonically with the neighborhood size, from
0.4259 at $k=10$ to 0.4702 at $k=200$ (the ridge arm landed at 0.4623, between
$k=100$ and $k=200$). Figure~\ref{fig:ae-knn} shows the curve against the three
horizontal references that matter: even the best AE read ($k=10$, 0.4259) never
reaches the metadata-only tree (0.3879), nor even the AE-latent-only
\emph{xgboost} on record (0.4098).

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{ae_latent_knn}
  \caption{The song-AE latent carries no rotation signal the metadata
  one-hots do not already carry better. The kNN standalone error
  $mae_{\text{ae}}$ rises monotonically as the neighborhood widens (the
  latent's signal is strongest in tight local neighborhoods and washes toward
  the base rate), and even its best point sits above both the AE-latent-only
  tree and the metadata-only tree. The weight tuner correctly drives the blend
  weight to zero, so the ensemble collapses to metadata-only XGBoost.}
  \label{fig:ae-knn}
\end{figure}

\emph{Interpretation.} The collapse to $w=0$ is the correct answer, not a bug
to fix: given an AE branch strictly worse than the XGB branch on the holdout,
the optimal convex weight \emph{is} zero. The architecture's one real value was
that it \emph{isolated} the latent into a branch the tuner could discard,
recovering a cleaner result than feeding the latent into a unified XGBoost ---
which actually \emph{dilutes} the tree (MAE 0.3965 with the 64 latent columns
present versus 0.3879 with them dropped). The song-AE latent thus joins
co-listening, content-text and acoustic features as a dense modality that loses
to categorical identity one-hots. The one caveat at the time was honesty about
significance: the $0.3879$ versus $0.3965$ edge is only $0.0086$ MAE on a
$[0,1]$ target, and the rotation noise band was then unmeasured --- so the
campaign reported the number but asserted no significant win and promoted
nothing.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-06-09-ae-xgboost-blend.md}.}

\subsection{Kernel SVM on rotation: a refutation, and the binary-MAE trap}
\label{sec:svm}
\emph{Rationale.} With the latent ruled out, this campaign tested whether any
SVM kernel (rbf / linear / poly / sigmoid) produces a rotation predictor
competitive with the metadata-only XGBoost leader, and --- only if one passed
--- whether it added orthogonal signal in a grid-weighted blend. The metadata
matrix was reduced to the pca floor in a new dataset
(\dsid{ds-20260609-141622-p1-s42}, 115 cols), with two split-seed siblings
(seeds 17 and 101) built to measure the noise band, and the champion reproduced
as a control (\runid{run-20260609-141717-9a8ba-autoencoder-xgboost}, blend
weight 0). A secondary outcome was the campaign's central deliverable: the
\textbf{first measured rotation noise band}.

\emph{Results.} Across the three split seeds of the metadata-only XGBoost
control (s42 0.387891, s17 0.393072, s101 0.396417) the sample standard
deviation is $\sigma_{\text{split}}=0.00430$, giving a $2\sigma$ significance
band of \textbf{0.0086 MAE} on the $[0,1]$ scale. This retroactively settles
the previous campaign's open question: the $0.3879$ versus $0.3965$ edge is
$\Delta 0.0086 \approx 1{\cdot}\text{band}$ (likely better, not confident),
while the $0.3879$ versus $0.4098$ gap (AE-latent-only) is $\sim
2.5{\cdot}\text{band}$ and therefore real. As for the SVM arms, rbf, linear and
poly all posted MAE inside the control band (0.381--0.390), \emph{lower} than
the champion in the poly case (0.3814) --- yet every one of them is
worse-than-mean on \rsq{} ($-0.13$ to $-0.20$), with RMSE 0.53--0.54 against
the tree's 0.4475. Sigmoid SVR diverged outright: MAE 0.624, RMSE 1.531, \rsq{}
$-8.48$, with predictions reaching 24.7 on a $[0,1]$ target --- the classic
unbounded sigmoid-SVR blow-up. Figure~\ref{fig:svm} contrasts the two metric
views directly.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{svm_mae_vs_r2}
  \caption{On a binary target, low SVR MAE is an artifact, not skill. With
  $\epsilon=0.1$ on a $\{0,1\}$ target the SVR snaps its outputs to the
  $\epsilon$-tube edges (a hard two-level step, $p_{50}\approx 0.10$,
  $p_{90}\approx 0.90$), so each row sits only $\sim 0.1$ off its label and the
  \emph{MAE} (left) reads competitive. The honest \emph{$R^2$} (right) exposes
  it: every kernel is worse than predicting the mean. Sigmoid is off-scale at
  $R^2=-8.48$.}
  \label{fig:svm}
\end{figure}

\emph{Interpretation.} The low SVR MAE is a degeneracy of the metric on a
binary target. With an $\epsilon$-tube of width $0.1$ on $\{0,1\}$ labels, the
SVR collapses to a two-level step function pinned near the right classes ---
each row is about $0.1$ off its label, so the MAE is small, while calibration
and ranking are destroyed (negative \rsq{}). The metric that matters for the
Pathfinder \texttt{fit\_norm} (ranking and calibration, i.e.\ \rsq{}/RMSE/AUC)
says the SVM loses decisively. Worse, the two stage-2 blends
(\runid{run-20260609-143114-528ef-blend} and its lgbm twin) fitted weights of
exactly $[0.0, 1.0]$ --- the grid, optimizing the same degenerate validation
MAE, \emph{mis-selected pure SVM} and discarded the tree, the opposite of the
self-disabling seen in the blend campaign. The lesson is sharp: a grid-by-MAE
blend objective is itself unsafe on a binary target. Nothing was registered or
promoted; kernel SVM became the fifth dense-regime family to lose to one-hot
identity on this corpus.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-06-09-svm-kernel-rotation.md}.}

\subsection{The honest AUC bake-off and the first AUC champion}
\label{sec:auc}
\emph{Rationale.} The SVM campaign exposed that MAE is the wrong lens for a
binary target. The framework had meanwhile gained a classification mode
(\texttt{domain.toml} \texttt{task = binary}, primary metric AUC,
higher-better; the best-models group ranks by AUC and auto-promotes the top
run). This campaign re-ran the comparison on that honest footing: xgboost-classifier
against logistic and a four-kernel SVC scan, all on DS-META, plus a
178-column arm with the AE latent re-added to settle the latent-dilution
question on the AUC axis, and three control seeds to measure the AUC noise
band. The hypothesis --- a sixth test of the convergent finding --- was that
xgboost-classifier wins and every dense family loses.

\emph{Results.} All nine runs succeeded. The champion config
(\runid{run-20260609-172920-627d6-xgboost-classifier}, control hyperparameters,
seed 42) reached \textbf{AUC 0.7429} (log-loss 0.5883, accuracy 0.6846, Brier
0.2017) and was registered as \texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation} and
auto-promoted to best-models rank 1 --- the project's first saved, AUC-scored,
promotable rotation champion. The first measured \emph{AUC} noise band comes
from the three control seeds (s42 0.742934, s17 0.730659, s101 0.734469, mean
0.736020): $\sigma_{\text{AUC}}=0.00628$, $2\sigma=\textbf{0.0126}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:auc-band}). This band is a different metric from the 0.0086
MAE band and does not replace it. Every dense family lost by many bands ---
svc-poly 0.6855, logistic 0.6762, svc-rbf 0.6722, svc-linear 0.6363,
svc-sigmoid 0.6255 --- a spread of 4.6 to 9.3 noise bands below the champion
(Figure~\ref{fig:auc-bakeoff}). The 178-column arm (AE latent re-added) scored
0.7350, $-0.0079$ below the metadata-only champion --- the same dilutive
direction as the regression result, but inside the AUC band, so
neutral-leaning-dilutive at a single seed.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{auc_bakeoff}

\emph{Interpretation.} Reframing rotation as classification did two things at
once. It dissolved the SVR illusion of Section~\ref{sec:svm} --- under AUC,
log-loss \emph{and} Brier, every SVC kernel is now visibly worse than the
trees, all in the same direction, so the $\epsilon$-tube artifact that made
SVR's MAE look competitive simply vanishes. And it confirmed that the metric
was only ever a problem for the \emph{fairness of the comparison}; the dense
family was always the loser. Notably svc-sigmoid does not collapse to chance
under classification (it holds AUC 0.6255 rather than blowing up): a proper
probability objective removes the unbounded divergence that wrecked the
SVR-sigmoid run. The convergent finding now stands on an honest metric: the
taste-fit signal lives in artist/genre/album \emph{identity}, captured by
sparse one-hots and exploited by trees, not in dense distance or margin models.
The remaining lever is identity \emph{resolution} --- raising the vocabulary
caps so the \texttt{artist=\_\_other\_\_} tail gets real columns --- not new
modalities or families. The next planned campaign is a champion hyperparameter
scan now that the family and metric are settled.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-06-09-rotation-classification.md}.}

\subsection{Pyramid-MLP architecture scan: refuting the budget hypothesis}
\label{sec:mlp-scan}
\emph{Rationale.} The bake-off flagged the pyramid-MLP's last place (AUC
\num{0.6781}, log-loss \num{2.4085}) as a 300-iteration-cap under-training
artifact, prescribing ``raise \texttt{max\_iter} so the net converges.'' This
follow-up tests that mechanism directly: six pyramid geometries, all with
\texttt{max\_iter} raised to \textbf{800}, alpha \num{1e-4}, learning rate
\num{1e-3}, seed 42, on the same dataset --- so the comparison reflects
\emph{architecture}, not budget. Does any geometry, given the budget to
converge, lift the dense family to a competitive AUC?

\emph{Results.} Refuted on every count (Table~\ref{tab:mlp-scan};
Figures~\ref{fig:mlp-arch} and~\ref{fig:mlp-calib}). \textbf{No arm hit the 800
cap}: every geometry self-stopped at \textbf{73--100 iterations} via sklearn's
\texttt{n\_iter\_no\_change} loss-plateau tolerance --- far below even the old
300 cap, so the budget was never the binding constraint. The decisive tell: the
re-baseline arm M0 ([256,128,64], the bake-off geometry now at \texttt{max\_iter}
800) reproduced the under-trained run's AUC to all digits ---
\num{0.6780926} versus \num{0.6780926} --- because it ran the identical 73
iterations. Raising the cap from 300 to 800 changed nothing. The best converged
geometry was M1 [512,256] at AUC \num{0.6792} (\runid{run-20260612-123155-0ace5}),
a statistical tie with the under-trained default ($+0.0011 \approx$ zero) and
$\sim$3.3 bands below xgboost. The whole family lands in a 0.0080-wide AUC strip
(0.6712--0.6792), entirely inside one noise band --- the geometry ranking is not
resolvable at a single seed --- with the log-loss (2.06--2.78) and Brier
(0.32--0.34) blown at the same level as the under-trained default across every
arm. No definition was saved (below the 0.683 save bar).

\section{Why artificial neural nets underperform on one-hot-heavy taste data}
\label{sec:anns}
The dense \texttt{pyramid-mlp} is the only artificial neural net in the
rotation campaigns, and across two Jun-12 campaigns it lands last with a
distinctive failure pattern. This section consolidates, rigorously, \emph{why}
--- grounding every claim in the campaign evidence. The short version is the
project's documented \emph{convergent finding}: on this feature matrix the
binding constraint is the input \emph{representation}, and a dense net is the
one model class structurally mismatched to it. We build the argument in five
parts and close with the motivated next step.

\subsection{The feature matrix is mostly sparse identity}
\label{sec:anns-matrix}
The full-64-d matrix has 177 columns, and their \emph{composition} is the whole
story. \textbf{106 of the 177 columns are sparse identity one-hots}: artist
($\times 61$), \texttt{genre\_primary} ($\times 41$) and \texttt{album\_type}
($\times 4$). Only 71 columns are continuous --- the 64 song-AE PCA latents and
7 numeric metadata fields (popularities, follower count, release year,
credited-artist count). For any given track exactly one artist column, one genre
column and one album-type column is hot; the rest of the 106 are zero. The
matrix is, by construction, dominated by a mostly-zero block of mutually
exclusive indicators.

A gradient-boosted tree reads this block \emph{losslessly and cheaply}: a single
axis-aligned split on column \texttt{artist=X} partitions the data on exactly
that identity, with no interaction among the one-hots forced and no cost paid for
the columns that are zero. Capturing ``tracks by artist $X$ in genre $Y$ rotate''
costs the tree two nested splits. A dense net cannot do this. It must learn a
\emph{dense} weight from every one of the 106 mostly-zero inputs into every
hidden unit, then back out a non-linear combination --- spending capacity and
gradient signal modeling the structure of a block that the tree handles with one
comparison. This is precisely the regime in which trees structurally dominate
dense nets, and it is the project's \emph{convergent finding}, now observed
across four feature modalities and five model families: the taste-fit signal is
carried by artist/genre identity, captured by one-hots and exploited by trees,
not by descriptive attributes or distance/margin/dense models. The pyramid-MLP
is simply the dense family's turn to lose to it.

\subsection{Standardization turns rare categories into outlier spikes}
\label{sec:anns-scaler}
The dense net is fed a StandardScaler-ed matrix, and standardization interacts
pathologically with sparse one-hots. A one-hot column for a category present in
a fraction $p$ of rows has mean $p$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.
Standardizing it maps the ``on'' rows to
\[
  z_{\text{on}} = \frac{1-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}} = +\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}},
  \qquad
  z_{\text{off}} = \frac{0-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}} = -\sqrt{\frac{p}{1-p}}.
\]
For a \emph{rare} category --- the long tail of artists and genres, where $p$ is
small --- the ``on'' value $+\sqrt{(1-p)/p}$ becomes a large positive spike (for
$p=0.01$ it is $\approx +9.9$), while the ``off'' value sits at a tiny negative
$-\sqrt{p/(1-p)}\approx -0.1$. Every rare identity therefore arrives at the net
as an occasional, enormous outlier on its own axis. ReLU units with Adam at
learning rate \num{1e-3} swing hard on these spikes --- a single rare-category
activation drives a large pre-activation that the optimizer chases --- and the
softmax/sigmoid head saturates, emitting near-0 or near-1 probabilities on thin
evidence. Trees are immune: a split threshold on a one-hot is invariant to any
monotone rescaling, so the scaler's spike does nothing to them. This is a direct
mechanistic contributor to the blown calibration documented next.

\subsection{The diagnostic signature: genuine ranking, broken calibration}
\label{sec:anns-signature}
The dense net's failure has a precise, reproducible signature in the metrics ---
and it is \emph{not} the failure of a weak model. The pyramid-MLP posts AUC
$\approx 0.68$ (0.6781 in the bake-off, 0.6792 best in the arch scan): genuine
ranking signal, well above the 0.5 chance line, only $\sim$3 bands below the
trees. Yet \emph{at the same time} its log-loss is $\approx 2.0$--$2.8$ and its
Brier $\approx 0.32$--$0.34$, against the trees' log-loss $\approx 0.60$--$0.63$
and Brier $\approx 0.21$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mlp-calib}). A constant base-rate
predictor (always outputting $p\approx 0.445$) scores a log-loss of only
$\approx 0.69$. \textbf{The net's log-loss of 2.0--2.8 is thus far worse than the
random baseline, while xgboost's 0.61 is well below it.}

This combination --- acceptable ranking, worse-than-random log-loss --- means the
net is \emph{confidently wrong}: it pushes many predictions to the saturated
extremes (near 0 or near 1) on the wrong side of the label, and log-loss punishes
each such confident error severely. It is miscalibrated, not weak: the ordering
is there, but the probabilities attached to that ordering are unusable as a
\texttt{fit\_norm}. The mechanism is exactly the scaler-spike saturation of
Section~\ref{sec:anns-scaler}. Contrast the trees, whose leaf-frequency outputs
are calibrated by construction: a leaf reporting probability $q$ is the empirical
rotation frequency of the training rows that fall in it, so the tree's
probabilities are honest by design --- the reason it wins on log-loss and Brier as
well as AUC.

\subsection{Optimization versus representation: the binding constraint}
\label{sec:anns-binding}
A natural objection is that the net is merely under-optimized --- too few
iterations, the wrong geometry. The architecture scan (Section~\ref{sec:mlp-scan})
forecloses both. On the \emph{budget} axis, every one of the six geometries
self-stops at 73--100 iterations, well below even the old 300 cap (let alone the
raised 800): the optimizer reaches a loss plateau it cannot escape, so more budget
buys nothing --- the re-baseline arm M0 reproduced the under-trained AUC to all
digits because it ran the identical 73 iterations. On the \emph{architecture}
axis, all six geometries land inside a single noise band, and the best converged
geometry reproduces the under-trained AUC to four digits (0.6792 versus 0.6781).
Both knobs internal to the dense family are therefore exhausted. By elimination,
the binding constraint is neither budget nor architecture but the input
\emph{representation} --- the raw one-hots of Section~\ref{sec:anns-matrix}, which
the net cannot consume the way a tree does. This is the diagnostic that points at
representation-aware topologies (entity embeddings, wide-and-deep) as the only
untested lever, since those are designed precisely to replace raw one-hots with a
learned dense representation.

\subsection{PCA has already pre-empted the net's nonlinear job}
\label{sec:anns-pca}
There is a second reason the dense net has little to do. The 64 song-AE PCA
latents are already a \emph{linear, decorrelated} compression of a
1536-dimensional embedding: PCA has extracted the dominant linear structure and
handed the net orthogonal components with most of the easy linear variance
already separated. Whatever nonlinear interaction a hidden layer might discover
in that block has largely been pre-empted; little nonlinear structure remains for
the net's depth to exploit. The evidence is direct: a plain logistic regression
reaches AUC 0.676 on this corpus and the converged MLP reaches 0.679 --- a dead
heat. A multi-layer ReLU network buys essentially nothing over a single linear
boundary, which is exactly what one expects when the continuous block is already
linearly pre-conditioned and the categorical block is the part that carries
signal the net cannot read.

\subsection{The open question and the motivated next step}
\label{sec:anns-next}
The diagnosis is now sharp: the dense net's ceiling is set by its inability to
consume raw one-hot identity, not by budget, architecture, or the continuous
block. That isolates a single, testable open question --- \emph{can a
representation-aware topology close the gap to the trees?} The two ingredients
that target the diagnosis directly are (i) \textbf{entity embeddings} for the
identity groups (artist, genre, album-type), which replace each sparse one-hot
block with a learned low-dimensional dense vector --- the standard fix for
high-cardinality categoricals in nets, removing both the dense-weights-over-zeros
problem of Section~\ref{sec:anns-matrix} and the scaler-spike problem of
Section~\ref{sec:anns-scaler}; and (ii) a \textbf{wide-and-deep} split --- a wide
linear path over the one-hots (so identity is read directly, as the tree reads
it) alongside a deep path over the continuous block. A Rust/\texttt{burn}
implementation of such a representation-aware network --- the
\texttt{burn-deep-classifier} --- was built precisely to answer this question.
Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn} reports its results, and they resolve the diagnosis
cleanly: the calibration pathology was indeed a representation artifact and is
fixed, but the AUC gap to the trees only \emph{narrows}, it does not close.

\emph{Sources: \texttt{2026-06-12-classifier-bakeoff-p64.md},
\texttt{2026-06-12-pyramid-mlp-arch-scan.md}.}

\subsection{Resolved: the burn-deep-classifier fixes calibration but not the AUC gap}
\label{sec:anns-burn}
\emph{Rationale.} The preceding subsections left a single sharp open question:
the dense net's ceiling is set by its inability to consume raw one-hot identity,
so \emph{can a representation-aware topology close the gap to the trees?} The
\texttt{burn-deep-classifier} --- a full-Rust/\texttt{burn} network --- was built
to answer it. Its design targets the two diagnosed pathologies directly: it keeps
the identity one-hots \emph{raw} (standardizing only the 64 PCA $+$ 7 numeric
continuous block), so the scaler-spike mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:anns-scaler}
never fires, and it exposes a \texttt{topology} enum over a plain dense
\texttt{mlp}, learned entity \texttt{embeddings}, and a \texttt{wide\_deep} split.
Phase 1 compared the three topologies (epochs 250, patience 20, early-stop on
validation log-loss, hidden $[128,64]$, embed\_dim 8, lr \num{1e-3}, batch 256);
Phase 2 tuned the winner. All nine runs ran on the same full-64-d dataset
(\dsid{ds-20260609-204419-p64-s42}) and succeeded cleanly.

\emph{Results.} The headline is the calibration recovery
(Figure~\ref{fig:burn-topology}). Every topology lands at log-loss
\num{0.621}--\num{0.628} and Brier \num{0.217}--\num{0.220} --- squarely in the
trees' $\sim$0.60--0.63 / $\sim$0.21 regime --- against the sklearn pyramid's
log-loss \num{2.0}--\num{2.4} and Brier \num{0.33}. \textbf{The calibration
pathology is fixed completely.} On the topology axis (Table~\ref{tab:burn-topo}),
the plain \texttt{mlp} wins at AUC \num{0.7001}, with \texttt{embeddings}
(\num{0.6934}) and \texttt{wide\_deep} (\num{0.6918}) both inside the
$2\sigma=0.0126$ band of it and of each other --- \texttt{embeddings} edges
\texttt{wide\_deep} by a sub-band $+0.0016$. The Phase-2 \texttt{mlp} tune
(Table~\ref{tab:burn-tune}) tops out at AUC \num{0.7038} (lr \num{5e-4},
$[128,64]$): the whole 6-cell grid is a \num{0.0081}-wide strip
(\num{0.6957}--\num{0.7038}), every $[128,64]$ cell beats every $[256,128]$ cell,
and the best config is only $+0.0037$ (inside the band) over the Phase-1 winner,
so the Phase-1 \texttt{mlp} config stands. At \num{0.7038} the net sits
$-0.0073$ below \texttt{catboost} ($\sim$0.6 band) and $-0.0166$ below
\texttt{xgboost} ($\sim$1.3 bands); below the $\sim$0.71 save bar, so no
definition was persisted.

finally start to pay off --- a prediction that the high-vocab re-run of

\subsection{Confirmed: raising identity cardinality lifts the embedding net into the tree tier}
\label{sec:anns-highvocab}
\emph{Rationale.} Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn} closed on a sharp, testable
prediction: the entity-\texttt{embeddings} topology lost to plain \texttt{mlp}
\emph{only} because the identity cardinalities (artist 61, genre 41) were too
low to feed a learned embedding, so raising the vocabulary caps should flip the
ranking. This campaign is the paired re-run that tests it directly. A new matrix
(\dsid{ds-20260612-143014-p64-s42}) was built \emph{identical} to the 60/40
full-64-d dataset of Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn} --- same \num{23529} rows, same
split seed 42, byte-identical PCA64 --- differing in \emph{one} axis only:
identity cardinality, with the artist cap raised $60\to300$
(\SI{50.4}{\percent} coverage) and \texttt{genre\_primary} $40\to120$
(\SI{78.8}{\percent}). It is a \emph{third} non-comparable matrix (497 columns:
64 PCA $+$ 9 numeric $+$ 426 one-hots --- artist 301, genre 121, album-type 4 ---
versus the 60/40 matrix's 177), so all metrics here are fresh in-dataset
references and must never be read against the 60/40 or DS-META boards. The
headline question: with cardinality $5\times$ (artist) and $3\times$ (genre)
larger, does \texttt{embeddings} now beat \texttt{mlp}? Phase 0 re-ran the two
trees for an in-dataset tree tier; Phase 1 re-ran the topology bake-off (the
embeddings arm now at embed\_dim 16, since higher cardinality justifies a wider
table); Phase 2 tuned the winning topology over embed\_dim $\times$ lr.

\emph{Results.} The ranking \textbf{flipped} (Table~\ref{tab:highvocab-topo};
Figure~\ref{fig:burn-highvocab}). \texttt{embeddings}
(\runid{run-20260612-143411-e8db6-burn-deep-classifier}) wins at AUC
\num{0.7192}, with \texttt{mlp} at \num{0.7129} ($+0.0063$) and \texttt{wide\_deep}
last at \num{0.7061} ($+0.0131$). On the 60/40 matrix \texttt{mlp} had beaten
\texttt{embeddings} by $+0.0067$; here \texttt{embeddings} beats \texttt{mlp} by
$+0.0063$ --- a \textbf{swing of $+0.0130$} in the embedding's favour, crossing
the band, the qualitative reversal the starvation hypothesis predicted. Phase 0
shows both trees also moved \emph{up} on the wider matrix: xgboost
$0.7204\to\num{0.7299}$ ($+0.0095$, \runid{run-20260612-143411-02fda-xgboost-classifier},
log-loss \num{0.6022}) and catboost $0.7111\to\num{0.7252}$ ($+0.0141$,
\runid{run-20260612-143411-8f15e-catboost-classifier}), so high vocab is genuine
signal for \emph{every} family --- it does not hand the embedding a unique
advantage, it \emph{unlocks} a topology that was previously starved. The Phase-2
embed\_dim$\times$lr tune (best embed\_dim 8 / lr \num{5e-4}, AUC \num{0.7228},
\runid{run-20260612-143508-3310f-burn-deep-classifier}) shows the win came from
\emph{enabling} embeddings plus a slower learning rate, not from a wider table:
AUC is monotone-decreasing in embed\_dim at the slow lr (ed8 \num{0.7228} $>$
ed16 \num{0.7210} $>$ ed32 \num{0.7200}, a sub-band $\sim$\num{0.0028} spread),
so embed\_dim is already saturated at 8. The best tuned cell beats the Phase-1
winner by only $+0.0036$ (sub-band), so the decision rule saved the \emph{Phase-1}
config (embeddings, embed\_dim 16, lr \num{1e-3}) as
\texttt{burn-deep-embeddings-highvocab}. A determinism twin reproduced the
Phase-1 run to four digits ($0.71923$ versus $0.71923$). At \num{0.7192} the
saved net sits $-0.0060$ below catboost ($<$ 1 band) and $-0.0107$ below xgboost,
inside the band of the second tree family --- the first ANN to reach the
in-dataset tree tier and clear the rotation save bar.

moving from the bottom of the 60/40 ranking to the top of this one. Second, high
vocab helps \emph{everyone}: the trees gained as much as (catboost) or more than
the embedding arm, so the correct reading is ``higher resolution is better
signal for every family, and it specifically unlocks the embedding topology'',
not ``embeddings now win outright'' --- \texttt{xgboost} at \num{0.7299} is still
the in-dataset leader, $\sim$\num{0.0107} ($\sim$1 band) above the saved net.
But the qualitative arc that ran through Section~\ref{sec:anns} now closes
cleanly: a dense net that consumes raw one-hots is calibrated in the tree regime
(Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn}), and given \emph{enough} identity resolution its
embedding topology reaches the tree tier --- the standing ``identity resolution
is the lever'' lever, demonstrated. Logloss ($\sim$0.61) and Brier ($\sim$0.21)
stay in the trees' calibrated regime throughout, so there is no calibration
penalty for the win. One data-hygiene note: the high-vocab manifest's redundancy
report flags 21 near-perfect genre$\leftrightarrow$artist one-hot collinearities
(e.g.\ \texttt{genre\_primary=australian psych} $\equiv$ \texttt{artist=Tame
Impala}, correlation \num{1.000}; 19 more $\ge\num{0.957}$), an artifact of
admitting rare genres carried by a single newly-admitted artist; the trees and
the net's regularizers absorb it with no observed run pathology, but it is worth
knowing before reading individual one-hot importances on this matrix. The saved
\texttt{burn-deep-embeddings-highvocab} is flagged for best-model-selector as
the first tree-tier ANN and a natural family-diversity candidate.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-06-12-high-vocab-topology.md}.}

\subsection{A second representation-aware net: the AE-transplant confirms the ceiling}
\label{sec:anns-ae}
\emph{Rationale.} Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn} established that a net which keeps
the identity one-hots \emph{raw} is calibrated in the tree regime yet still caps
$\sim$1 band below the trees. A natural next question is whether a \emph{learned
unsupervised representation} of the same inputs --- rather than the raw inputs
themselves --- lifts the net further. The \texttt{burn-ae-classifier} tests it:
Phase 1 pretrains an autoencoder to reconstruct the full assembled feature
vector (standardized continuous block beside the raw one-hots, $D{=}177$) by
MSE, Phase 2 transplants the encoder into a softmax classifier and fine-tunes.
This campaign is the family's first characterization --- a 13-config scan on the
60/40 full-64-d matrix (\dsid{ds-20260609-204419-p64-s42}, seed 42) over latent
size, head depth/width, activation, and the \texttt{freeze\_encoder} flag, read
against the in-corpus xgboost champion (\num{0.7204}) and the comparable
\texttt{burn-deep} \texttt{mlp} (\num{0.7038}).

\emph{Results.} \textbf{Capacity does not matter for this family on rotation}
(Table~\ref{tab:burn-ae}; Figure~\ref{fig:burn-ae}). The 12 fine-tuned configs
span a single \num{0.0054}-wide strip (\num{0.69175}--\num{0.69719}), entirely
inside the $2\sigma=0.0126$ band --- a flat tie across a $4\times$ latent range
(32 to 128), across head depth and width, and across activation. The nominal
best (cfg3, enc$[128,64]$/clf$[128,64]$, AUC \num{0.69719}) beats the
\num{0.69225} baseline by only $+0.00494$ (sub-band), and even the bare linear
probe (cfg2, clf$[]$, \num{0.69175}) is only $-0.00544$ below it: a single linear
layer on the fine-tuned latent already captures essentially all the family's
skill. The one out-of-band effect is a \emph{trap}: freezing the MSE-pretrained
encoder is safe under a shallow head (cfg11, \num{0.69530}, in-band) but
\emph{collapses} under a deep head (cfg10, \num{0.68210}, $-0.0151$ $\approx$
1.2 bands, the worst row). relu edges gelu and silu by a sub-band margin.
Calibration is healthy throughout (log-loss $\sim$0.62, Brier $\sim$0.22 --- the
trees' regime), so this is a legitimate calibrated dense member, just not an AUC
contender: the best config sits $\sim$1.8 bands below \texttt{xgboost} and is
\emph{statistically equal} to the plain \texttt{burn-deep} \texttt{mlp}
($-0.0066$, sub-band). No definition was saved.

\subsection{Refuted: a GBDT-leaf-embedding head cannot recover the tree that generated its features}
\label{sec:leaf-head}
\emph{Rationale.} The ANN thread above closed on a clean lesson --- a learned
representation fed downstream of the identity one-hots does not buy ranking
power the trees do not already have (the AE-latent dilution of
Section~\ref{sec:ae-blend}; entity embeddings starved unless cardinality is
high, Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn}). This campaign stress-tests that lesson at
its hardest point with the classic Facebook GBDT$\to$LR trick, generalized to
GBDT$\to$MLP. The frozen champion tree --- the registered
\texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation-p64} config, \emph{hard-frozen}, not a
tunable --- emits, for each row, the index of the leaf it falls into in every
one of its 500 boosted trees; those indices are one-hot-encoded into the tree's
own learned axis-aligned partition ($\sim$10k leaf-membership columns), and a
fresh head (an $\ell_2$-logistic linear head, or a shallow MLP) is trained on
top. The input to the head is thus the tree's \emph{own} partition; the
hypothesis --- a seventh test of the convergent finding --- was that even this
would \emph{not} beat the bare tree, the expected refutation being the citable
finding. All arms ran on the 60/40 full-64-d matrix
(\dsid{ds-20260609-204419-p64-s42}, seed 42); the only valid reference is the
in-dataset \num{0.7204} champion, never the \num{0.7429} DS-META or
\num{0.7299} high-vocab numbers (different matrices).

\emph{Results.} Refuted exactly as predicted (Table~\ref{tab:leaf-head};
Figure~\ref{fig:leaf-head}). The control C0
(\runid{run-20260615-151551-f2911-xgboost-classifier}) reproduced the champion
to all 16 digits (\num{0.7204383403100314}), so the determinism gate passed and
the treatments are interpretable. Every head then capped \emph{below} the bare
tree by more than one noise band: GBDT$\to$linear at AUC \num{0.6978}
($-0.0227$, $\sim$1.8 bands), GBDT$\to$MLP$[256,128]$ at \num{0.6921}
($\sim$2.2 bands), GBDT$\to$MLP$[128]$ at \num{0.6897} ($\sim$2.4 bands) --- the
best of them is itself \num{0.0100} below the refutation threshold
(champion $-$ band $=$ \num{0.7078}). The in-band-tie branch (which would have
fired a 3-seed spot-check) and the win branch ($>\num{0.7330}$) never triggered;
no definition was saved. The linear head --- the literal Facebook recipe --- is
the best of the three, and adding MLP capacity over the leaf one-hots does not
recover the tree, consistent with the recipe's original framing as a cheap
\emph{linear} trick. Worse than the AUC deficit, \emph{every head is badly
mis-calibrated}: log-loss \num{0.8162}--\num{1.3119} and Brier
\num{0.2603}--\num{0.2951} against the tree's \num{0.6075} / \num{0.2109}. The
$\ell_2$-logistic head at the default inverse-$C{=}1$ is under-regularized over
$\sim$10k sparse leaf indicators --- it drives a handful of leaf weights to
large magnitudes and emits over-confident probabilities, the same
sklearn-pyramid over-confidence signature (log-loss far above the trees' while
AUC is only modestly behind) seen in Section~\ref{sec:anns-signature}, and a
calibration red flag for best-model-selector should any of these runs ever be
considered for the group.

additive leaf-value scoring already weights every leaf \emph{jointly} across 500
boosting rounds with shrinkage; re-encoding those leaves as one-hot membership
indicators and fitting a head \emph{post-hoc} discards the boosting structure
and re-estimates it worse. The leaf one-hots are a strictly lossier view of a
tree that already scores better directly --- the dense head cannot even recover
the tree that generated its own features. This is the same family as the
AE-latent dilution (Section~\ref{sec:ae-blend}) and the dense-head ceiling
(Section~\ref{sec:anns}), pushed to its limit: here the head's \emph{input} is
the tree's optimal partition, and it still caps $\sim$2 bands below. Re-tuning
the head regularization could tame the over-confidence but cannot close an
$\sim$1.8-band AUC deficit, so the leaf-head family is closed for rotation. This
is the seventh convergent confirmation that the durable lever is identity
\emph{resolution} (raising vocab caps, which already lifted xgboost to
\num{0.7299} on the high-vocab matrix), not new heads or representations layered
on the tree.

\subsection{The identity-resolution vocab-cap scan: a largely-spent lever}
\label{sec:vocab-cap}
\emph{Rationale.} Every campaign above closes on the same standing lever ---
identity \emph{resolution}: raising the artist/genre vocabulary caps so the
\texttt{\_\_other\_\_} tail gets real one-hot columns, which already lifted
xgboost from \num{0.7204} (60/40) to \num{0.7299} (300/120,
Section~\ref{sec:anns-highvocab}) and flipped the embedding net into the tree
tier. But only \emph{two} points existed on that cap$\to$AUC curve, and the
high end was the in-lineage record. This campaign maps where the lever stops
paying. It holds the registered champion \texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation-p64}
config \emph{frozen} (\texttt{catboost} defaults as a secondary arm), holds
\emph{everything else} fixed --- same \num{23529}-row corpus, same split seed 42
(\num{18823} train / \num{4706} test), same PCA64, same field block --- and
moves the \emph{single} axis \texttt{vocab\_top\_n}, adding three higher-cap
points: 500/200 (777 cols), 1000/300 (1377 cols) and the full vocabulary,
5594/962 (6633 cols). The hypothesis to discriminate: \emph{saturation} (gain
per step $<$ one band) versus \emph{reversal} (AUC drops as singleton-artist
columns inject memorizable variance).

\emph{Results.} All eight launched runs succeeded; the controls reproduced both
anchors to four digits (60/40 xgboost \num{0.72044} versus reference
\num{0.7204}, $\Delta+0.00004$; 300/120 xgboost \num{0.72988} versus
\num{0.7299}, $\Delta-0.00002$), so the whole curve sits under one consistent
measurement (Table~\ref{tab:vocab-cap}; Figure~\ref{fig:vocab-cap}). xgboost
rose to a new in-lineage single-split best of \textbf{\num{0.73490}} at 1000/300
(\runid{run-20260615-163803-e75be-xgboost-classifier}), but the step structure
tells the real story: the jump $60\to300$ was $+0.00944$ ($+0.75$ band), then
$300\to500$ was \emph{flat} ($-0.00022$, within noise) and $500\to1000$ was
$+0.00524$ ($+0.42$ band). The best point is only $+0.00502$ ($\sim$0.40 band)
over the 0.7299 anchor --- a \emph{sub-band} improvement, well short of the
$+0.0126$ a confident beat would require. \texttt{catboost} tracks xgboost
qualitatively at every cap (\num{0.71105}$\to$\num{0.72516}$\to$\num{0.72372}%
$\to$\num{0.72633}), staying $\sim$0.005--0.009 below it throughout and rising
just as gently after 300/120 --- its native categorical handling does
\emph{not} scale better at high cardinality. The top-of-curve all-vocab point
(6633 cols) \textbf{could not be built}: two POST attempts each returned a
build id but died mid-materialization against the host memory ceiling
($\sim$2.3 GB free of 15 GB under concurrent load), leaving no dataset and no
artifacts. That is an infrastructure ceiling, \emph{not} a modeling result, and
the curve's top end is recorded as unmeasured.

\subsection{Reading the nets: per-model sparse autoencoders}
\label{sec:anns-sae}
\emph{Rationale.} Every result so far has been read off the leaderboard --- AUC,
log-loss, Brier. But two nets can post identical AUC while carving the input
into very different internal representations, a distinction the leaderboard
cannot see. This campaign opens that box. It first ports a \emph{per-model
sparse autoencoder} (\texttt{POST /api/interp/model-sae}) into the
\texttt{burn-deep-classifier} family (the SAE head previously existed only for a
regressor), so that promotion now auto-queues an interpretability read: a
$2\times$-overcomplete SAE (l1 \num{0.0015}, 40 epochs) is fit on each hidden
layer's activations, its atoms are probed for interpretable \emph{concepts}, and
the linear \emph{decodability} of rotation is measured layer by layer. The first
use compares two geometries at fixed AUC on the canonical full-64-d matrix
(\dsid{ds-20260609-204419-p64-s42}, seed 42): a tapering \texttt{pyramid}
$[256,128,64]$ against a uniform \texttt{rect} $[128,128,128]$, differing
\emph{only} in hidden shape.

\emph{Results.} The two nets are \textbf{statistically tied} on AUC (pyramid
\num{0.69857}, rect \num{0.69937}; $\Delta\num{0.0008}\ll$ band) --- both in the
on-record \texttt{burn-deep} \texttt{mlp} tier and $\sim$1.7 bands below the
xgboost champion. They also capture the \emph{same amount} of decodable signal:
linear decodability of rotation barely lifts above the raw input ($\sim$0.111
$\to\sim$0.12) for both. The difference is purely in \emph{layout}
(Figure~\ref{fig:sae-pyramid-rect}). The pyramid's interpretable concepts peak
in its middle 128-d layer (72) and then compress $\sim$40\% out at the 64-d
bottleneck (42); the uniform net spreads them more evenly and \emph{retains}
more to the end ($17\to66\to54$), because it has the width to. Neither net is
starved --- both fully utilize every SAE atom (0 dead / 0 rare), give
essentially every identity segment ($\sim$103--105/105) a dedicated separating
atom, and reconstruct $>96\%$ of activation variance --- so the concept-count
gap is genuine allocation, not capacity. A no-early-stop control confirmed a
registered pitfall: run to the full 100 epochs with patience 0, both 3-layer
nets overfit (AUC $-\num{0.015}$, log-loss blows to \num{0.96}--\num{1.22}); the
$\sim$0.699 / 0.625 calibrated regime is recovered only \emph{because} the
production runs early-stop. \textbf{Deeper \texttt{burn-deep} nets need
patience.}

mechanistically, what the leaderboard only implies: the dense nets are not
finding structure the trees miss; they are re-packaging the same identity signal
in a representation that AUC cannot distinguish. This ``same signal, different
allocation'' pattern recurs across families at higher vocab
(Section~\ref{sec:anns-capacity}). \emph{Source:
\texttt{2026-07-12-pyramid-vs-rect-sae.md}.}

\subsection{Capacity is a spent lever at high vocab, and what the SAE sees}
\label{sec:anns-capacity}
\emph{Rationale.} The high-vocab matrix (Section~\ref{sec:anns-highvocab}) was
where the embedding net first reached the tree tier, but it was never
depth/width-tuned. This campaign asks the direct question: does \emph{capacity}
--- width or depth --- lift the ANN family across the remaining $\sim$0.2--0.6
band gap to the trees on that matrix (\dsid{ds-20260612-143014-p64-s42}, 497
cols)? Two one-axis geometry ladders, lr held at each topology's on-record best:
an \texttt{embeddings} ladder (ed8, lr \num{5e-4}) and an \texttt{mlp} ladder
(lr \num{1e-3}), each stepping $[128,64]$ (control) $\to[256,128]$ (wider)
$\to[256,128,64]$ (deeper). Each promoted arm also gets the per-model SAE of
Section~\ref{sec:anns-sae}, for a cross-family representation read at high vocab.

\emph{Results.} \textbf{CEILING --- capacity is saturated for both topologies}
(Table~\ref{tab:highvocab-capacity}; Figure~\ref{fig:highvocab-capacity}).
Neither widening nor deepening beats its own $[128,64]$ control by more than one
band; the best config stays the on-record \texttt{embeddings}
ed8/lr\num{5e-4}/$[128,64]$ (reproduced here at \num{0.72274}, $\Delta$
$-\num{0.0001}$). Deepening \texttt{embeddings} to three layers actively
\emph{hurts} ($-\num{0.0152}\approx$ 1.2 bands, the only out-of-band move);
deepening \texttt{mlp} helps a sub-band touch ($+\num{0.0057}$). Crucially, the
best \texttt{mlp} (B6, \num{0.71800}) closes to within $+\num{0.0047}$ of the
best \texttt{embeddings} (B1, \num{0.72274}): at their best geometries the two
topologies tie within a band, so \textbf{capacity, not the embeddings mechanism,
was the lever} --- the earlier ``embeddings wins at high vocab'' reading is an
in-band tie, and the ANN family remains $\sim$0.2--0.6 band below the tree tier
(catboost \num{0.7252}, xgboost \num{0.7299}). The saved
\texttt{burn-deep-embeddings-highvocab} definition and the champion are both
unchanged; the best arm was promoted for the record as

\section{Taste drift: the leaderboard is an upper bound on forward prediction}
\label{sec:drift}
\emph{Rationale.} Every AUC in this document is measured on a \emph{random}
train/test split, which lets a track discovered in a given period land on both
sides of the split. That answers ``can the model rank tracks from the same era
as its training data?'' --- but the deployed question is forward-looking:
\emph{will I replay a song I discover next?} This campaign asks it honestly by
adding a first-class chronological-split capability to the builder
(\texttt{split\_order\_field} on the build request): rows are ordered by a
payload field (here \texttt{first\_played}), the earliest go to train and the
latest to test, and PCA, vocabulary and imputation are all fit on the
early-train rows only, so the split is leak-free. Four datasets were built from
the active \texttt{spotify\_tracks\_song\_ae} collection --- full and intrinsic,
each random and chronological --- and the champion plus a model sweep were
evaluated on each dataset's own native split. If AUC collapses under the
chronological split, taste drifts and the leaderboard overstates real forward
skill.

\emph{Results.} \textbf{Taste is not stationary} (Table~\ref{tab:drift};
Figure~\ref{fig:taste-drift}). The full-random build reproduces the on-record
champion to five digits (\num{0.73490}), validating the fresh builds; the
chronological build of the \emph{same} features drops it to \num{0.61894} --- a
$+\num{0.116}$ collapse ($\approx$9 noise bands). The drop is \num{0.109}--%
\num{0.116} for \emph{every} model family (\texttt{catboost} \num{0.72889}$\to$%
\num{0.61294}, \texttt{logistic} \num{0.72141}$\to$\num{0.61234}), so it is the
data/taste, not a model quirk. Identity one-hots barely help forward:
\emph{dropping} them (the intrinsic build) costs only $\sim$0.02 chronological
(\num{0.61894}$\to$\num{0.59858}) versus $\sim$0.025 random --- they mostly
memorize same-era artists. A recency-weighted \texttt{xgboost} (up-weighting
recent discoveries) recovers only $+\num{0.003}$ (sub-band), so drift is not a
simple recency effect. And the walk-forward read --- train on
\texttt{first\_played}~$<Y$, test on year $Y$ --- lands at \num{0.59}--\num{0.67}
for every single year from 2019 to 2026, \emph{never} near the \num{0.735}
random baseline, and more training history does not close the gap. An
off-framework re-split of the pre-existing random-built features agrees to
$\sim$0.007 AUC (\num{0.626}), and controlling for right-censoring (restricting
the chronological test to tracks with $\ge$180 days of replay opportunity)
barely moves it (\num{0.62579}$\to$\num{0.62319}) --- so the penalty is genuine
non-stationarity, not the base-rate shift of mechanically-less-rotated recent
tracks.

\emph{Interpretation.} The mechanism is identity churn, and it ties the whole
document together. Rotation is driven by artist/genre \emph{identity} one-hots
(the convergent finding of every campaign above); in a chronological split the
test period is full of newly discovered artists that were unseen in training and
fall into \texttt{\_\_other\_\_}, so the model must fall back on the weaker
intrinsic acoustic/metadata signal --- which tops out near \num{0.60}. That is
exactly why raising vocab caps (Section~\ref{sec:vocab-cap}) and adding net
capacity (Section~\ref{sec:anns-capacity}) are spent levers for forward skill:
they only sharpen same-era \emph{memorization}, which does not transfer. It is
also the same-signal story the per-model SAEs tell --- linear decodability
$\sim$0.14 with no clean monosemantic taste atom (Sections~\ref{sec:anns-sae},
\ref{sec:anns-capacity}): remove identity memorization and little generalizable
taste signal remains. The practical reading: every on-record AUC is an upper
bound on same-era ranking, not forward skill; a deployed model needs periodic
retraining because taste drifts; and the only lever that would move
\emph{forward} AUC is richer generalizable intrinsic signal (better acoustics,
cross-artist structure), since identity resolution and model capacity are both
spent and same-era-only. This does not overturn any leaderboard number --- the
random-split AUCs are valid measurements of same-era ranking --- but it reframes
what they mean. \emph{Source:
\texttt{2026-07-12-taste-drift-temporal-split.md}.}

\section{Next-track sequence recommendation: the task, the Markov bar, and the fusion champion}
\label{sec:nexttrack}
Every campaign above targets the binary \emph{rotation} signal. The remaining
sections fold in a \emph{separate} line of work --- \emph{next-track} sequence
recommendation --- whose task, metrics and champion are unrelated to the
rotation leaderboards and must never be read against them. This section
consolidates the four campaigns that established the task, its baseline bar and
its fusion champion; the two sections that follow
(Sections~\ref{sec:nexttrack-compression} and~\ref{sec:nexttrack-phase2})
develop the item-representation and model-family axes and crown the current
best-on-record row.

\paragraph{Task and split.} The task is next-item \emph{retrieval}: given a
listening \emph{session} (an ordered sequence of tracks), rank the catalog and
recover the next distinct track the user actually played. It is scored on a
single leak-free chronological \emph{next-distinct} split of the sequence
artifact \dsid{seq-20260713-014226} (\num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items;
\num{5723} train / \num{1431} test at cut 2024-08-24). The \textbf{cold-item
rate is 0.55} --- more than half of test-session targets never appear in any
training session --- so absolute recall is modest \emph{by construction} and
every result is read against the incumbent baseline, not an absolute bar.

\paragraph{Metrics.} The headline metric is \textbf{Recall@10} (R@10, the
fraction of test sessions whose held-out next track appears in the top ten
retrieved), reported beside mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and, since 2026-07-14,
the first-class \emph{graded-relevance} diagnostics: \emph{artist@10} /
\emph{genre@10} (a top-ten candidate shares the truth's artist / primary genre)
and \emph{artist\_mrr}. All confidence intervals here are bootstrap 95\% CIs
over \num{2000} resamples of the \num{1431} test sessions, and the decisive
statistic is the \emph{paired} per-session $\Delta$ against a named reference
(the Markov bar, a same-space GRU anchor, or the champion). The graded metrics
are the honest scorecard: the champion lands the right \emph{artist} in the
top ten $\sim$32\% of the time and the right \emph{genre} $\sim$40\%, far more
useful than the $\sim$17\% exact-recall number the cold-item rate caps.

\paragraph{The Markov bar.} The reference every arm is measured against is the
first-order \emph{Markov} co-occurrence model (\texttt{B2}: a train-only
\texttt{track\_bigram} with artist/genre back-off, the transition term the
sibling app already ships) at R@10 \num{0.1069}, MRR \num{0.0694}. Popularity
(\texttt{B0}) and recency (\texttt{B1}) floors sit near zero (R@10
\num{0.0007}). A board \emph{win} requires beating the reference on
\emph{both} R@10 and MRR beyond the paired-$\Delta$ / marginal-CI tests, then a
reproduction confirm.

Table~\ref{tab:nexttrack-lb} is the standing next-track best-on-record board.
The current champion \emph{row} --- the rank-fusion \emph{R+M+C} z-blend on the
PCA-192 item space --- is crowned in Section~\ref{sec:nexttrack-phase2}; the
prior champion \emph{R+M} z-blend on the AE-64 space, crowned in
Section~\ref{sec:nexttrack-blend} below, remains the productization anchor (the
two-leg blend that maps onto the app's transition slot until the three-leg
blend is registered as a predictor). \textbf{None of these ranking predictors
is best-models-promotable}: they are train-only (no \texttt{predict}
subcommand), and the R+M+C champion is an off-server fusion driver, so a
confirmed win is a best-on-record \emph{row}, not a promoted model.

\subsection{The sequence sweep: a GRU-infonce ties the Markov bar}
\label{sec:nexttrack-sweep}
\emph{Rationale.} The instance's opening campaign asked the founding question:
does a session-context sequence model --- a GRU/LSTM over the ordered prefix,
predicting the next track's 64-dim song-AE latent and retrieving by cosine ---
beat the first-order Markov transition model the sibling app already ships,
\emph{forward}, on the leak-free session split? Four arms crossed the recurrent
cell (GRU, LSTM), capacity (\texttt{hidden} 128, 256) and, decisively, the
training loss (cosine regression versus \texttt{InfoNCE} contrastive), all on
\dsid{seq-20260713-014226} at 60 epochs.

\emph{Results.} No arm beat the bar. The decision rule required beating
\texttt{B2} on \emph{both} R@10 and MRR beyond non-overlapping CIs; the best arm
(M3, GRU \texttt{hidden}128 \texttt{InfoNCE}) reached a statistical \emph{tie}
with the Markov bar on R@10 (\num{0.0894}, paired-$\Delta$ CI includes zero) but
\emph{lost} on MRR (\num{0.0388} versus \num{0.0694}) and Recall@20. The
dominant lever was the loss objective: \texttt{InfoNCE} was $\approx 2.3\times$
cosine on R@10 (\num{0.0894} versus \num{0.0391}), while capacity (\texttt{h256})
and the LSTM cell barely moved the cosine arms (all $\sim$0.038--0.041).

\emph{Interpretation.} A latent-plus-single-cosine-hop is a harder route to the
\emph{exact} next track than a co-listening bigram: the bigram directly
memorizes ``what follows what,'' while the GRU must land near the right point in
a 64-d content space and then retrieve, losing on precise ranking (MRR). The
finding is consistent with the parent lab's taste-drift result --- \num{0.55}
cold items, identity/adjacency-driven behavior --- and it set the agenda:
\texttt{InfoNCE} is the base loss, and the natural next arm is a \emph{blend} of
the GRU's candidate scores with the Markov bigram.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-sequence-sweep.md}.}

\subsection{Topology scan: depth, width and direction are a spent lever}
\label{sec:nexttrack-topology}
\emph{Rationale.} With the loss fixed at \texttt{InfoNCE}, this scan isolated
network \emph{topology}: does depth (stacked layers), width (\texttt{hidden}
256), an inter-layer residual stack or bidirectionality lift the sequence model
over the Markov bar? The \texttt{seq-nexttrack} predictor gained
\texttt{num\_layers}, \texttt{residual} and \texttt{bidirectional}; because a
bidirectional RNN under per-step teacher forcing would leak future items, the
bidirectional arm used a leak-free last-item objective (encode the full prefix,
predict only the held-out next item).

\emph{Results.} Refuted. No arm beat the bar on both metrics. The best arm
(width \texttt{h256}, R@10 \num{0.0957}) is a \emph{tie} with the bar on R@10
and loses on MRR (\num{0.0411} versus \num{0.0694}). Depth is flat 1$\to$2
layers (\num{0.0894}$\to$\num{0.0922}, in-band) and \emph{hurts} at 3 layers
(\num{0.0825}, out-of-band below the bar); the residual stack over depth-2/h256
added nothing. The bidirectional arm collapsed (\num{0.0482}), but this is an
\emph{objective} artifact, not a directionality verdict: the leak-free last-item
objective supplies $\sim$1 training target per session versus teacher forcing's
$\sim$13 next-step pairs, so it trained on $\sim$13$\times$ less signal.

\emph{Interpretation.} Architecture is not the lever for next-track on this
corpus. The transferable latent-retrieval signal caps at R@10 $\sim$0.09--0.10
across depth, width, residual and direction, below the co-listening bigram ---
consistent with the whole arc that the loss objective moved the needle and
capacity/topology do not. The one remaining unexplored lever was the candidate
framing / fusion, which the next campaign pulled decisively.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-13-nexttrack-topology-scan.md}.}

\subsection{The GRU$\times$Markov fusion champion, and why more ensembling fails}
\label{sec:nexttrack-blend}
\emph{Rationale.} The sweep and the topology scan agreed that the best single
GRU only \emph{ties} the Markov bar on R@10 while losing on MRR. This campaign
asked whether \emph{combining} models beats the bar, and how far ensembling
goes. Three legs were defined, each emitting full-vocab candidate scores:
\textbf{M} = the train-only Markov bigram (the bar), \textbf{R} = the
GRU-infonce-h256 recurrence, and \textbf{A} = an ANN bag-of-prefix MLP (a
feed-forward twin of R). They were combined by a weighted z-normalized blend
($\alpha\cdot z(\text{leg})+\dots$) and, as a learned alternative, an XGB
stacker (a GBDT meta-learner over per-candidate features).

\emph{Results.} The \textbf{R+M z-blend} ($\alpha{=}0.5$) \emph{beats the bar
decisively}: R@10 \num{0.1726} $[0.154, 0.192]$, MRR \num{0.0963}, a paired
$\Delta$ over Markov of $+\num{0.0657}$ $[+0.049, +0.083]$ (CI entirely above
zero) --- $\sim$$+61\%$ R@10, $+39\%$ MRR --- on a robust plateau across
$\alpha \in [0.25, 0.75]$ and reproduced across three seeds (\num{0.1705} /
\num{0.1726} / \num{0.1726}). It was registered as the \texttt{seq-blend}
predictor and the \texttt{blend-gru-markov} definition, the first next-track
champion. Further ensembling then \emph{failed} to improve on it: R+A+M
(\num{0.1684}) is slightly worse, R+A ($\approx$ GRU alone, \num{0.0894}) is
much worse, and both XGB stacker variants (M+R \num{0.1020}; M+R+A \num{0.0887})
barely clear Markov alone.

latent predictor (A), redundant with R --- adds nothing. The learned XGB

``stacking beats blending'' intuition: its pointwise binary-with-negatives
training does not optimize the ranking objective as well as directly fusing
normalized scores. The remaining headroom is bounded by the \num{0.55}
cold-item rate and requires \emph{genuinely new signal}, not more model fusion
--- the hypothesis the graded-relevance campaign tests next.

\subsection{Graded-relevance metrics and new base learners: content is the only additive signal}
\label{sec:nexttrack-graded}
\emph{Rationale.} Two threads in one campaign. (A) Make the evaluation credit
landing the right \emph{band/vibe} even when the exact track is wrong --- the
observation that ``the first choice is usually not the exact track, but the
artist is usually right'' --- by promoting \emph{artist@k}, \emph{genre@k} and
\emph{artist\_mrr} to first-class metrics wired end-to-end
(\texttt{seq\_common.eval\_from\_scores} $\to$ Rust \texttt{Metrics} /
\texttt{MetricsSpec} $\to$ \texttt{domain.toml} $\to$ UI). (B) Try genuinely new
leak-free base learners --- \textbf{C} = content-kNN (cosine of each candidate's
song-AE latent to the prefix, no training), \textbf{M2} = a second-order Markov
trigram with back-off, and \textbf{I} = a train-only item2vec SGNS --- scored on
both the exact and the new graded metrics.

\emph{Results.} The exact-metric path was byte-identical (purely additive
keys); the champion reproduced R@10 \num{0.1726} / MRR \num{0.0963} exactly. No
new base learner beat the bar standalone (M2 \num{0.1076} $\approx$ M
\num{0.1069}; the trigram falls through to the bigram --- sessions are too short
to fire; item2vec \num{0.0517} is the weakest leg). Blended into the champion,
\textbf{content is the only additive signal}: \textbf{R+M+C} (adding the content
leg) is the top blend on MRR (\num{0.0963}$\to$\num{0.1014}), artist@10
(\num{0.3215}$\to$\num{0.3298}) and artist\_mrr, and ties the champion on exact
R@10 (paired $\Delta$ $+\num{0.0014}$, CI straddles zero). Item2vec \emph{drags}
the blend (R+M+I \num{0.1349}); the trigram is neutral.

\emph{Interpretation.} Content-kNN ranks \emph{different} near-correct tracks
(latent vibe similarity) than the GRU (session recurrence) and Markov
(co-occurrence), exactly the diversity the fusion campaign predicted new signal
would need. On the AE-64 space the content leg only \emph{tied} the champion on
exact recall while lifting the graded metrics --- so it was carried forward as
the graded-preferred third leg, not a new exact champion. That ``tie'' becomes a
\emph{win} once the item space is richer (Section~\ref{sec:nexttrack-phase2}):
on PCA-192 the same R+M+C construction is the strongest on record. Co-listening
item2vec and the trigram Markov added nothing on this data.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-14-nexttrack-graded-relevance-and-new-legs.md}.}

\section{Next-track sequence recommendation: the item-representation compression bake-off}
\label{sec:nexttrack-compression}
Every campaign above targets the binary \emph{rotation} signal. This section
folds in the first report from a \emph{separate} line of work --- \emph{next-track}
sequence recommendation --- whose task, metrics and champion are unrelated to the
rotation leaderboards and must never be read against them. The task is
retrieval: given a listening \emph{session} (an ordered sequence of tracks),
rank the catalog and recover the next distinct track the user actually played.
It is scored on a leak-free chronological \emph{next-distinct} split (\num{7154}
sessions / \num{19402} items; \num{5723} train / \num{1431} test at cut
2024-08-24; cold-item rate 0.55), and its headline metric is \textbf{Recall@10}
(R@10, the fraction of test sessions whose held-out next track appears in the
top ten retrieved), reported beside mean reciprocal rank (MRR) and the
category-hit diagnostics artist@10 / genre@10 / artist\_mrr. All confidence
intervals here are bootstrap 95\% CIs over \num{2000} resamples of the
\num{1431} test sessions. The standing champion of this track --- the reference
this campaign is read against --- is a rank-fusion \emph{R+M z-blend}
(\texttt{blend-gru-markov}, $\alpha{=}0.5$) of a sequence GRU with a first-order
Markov co-occurrence model on the 64-dim multimodal song-autoencoder space
(\texttt{spotify\_tracks\_song\_ae}, ``AE-64''), at R@10 \num{0.173}
$[0.154, 0.192]$, MRR \num{0.096}, three-seed confirmed.

\emph{Rationale.} The champion's item space --- a 64-dim AE latent --- was one
arbitrary early choice, and the sequence GRU predicts \emph{into} and retrieves
\emph{over} whatever geometry that space imposes. This campaign asks which
\emph{compression} is the best space for the GRU: linear PCA versus the
nonlinear autoencoder, each at latent dim 32 / 64 / 128. Five alternative spaces
were baked from the \emph{same} leak-safe 384-dim content source
(\texttt{spotify\_tracks\_content}), each fit unsupervised over all \num{23529}
items using only intrinsic content/acoustic/catalog fields (text-384 $+$
z-scored numerics $+$ \texttt{af\_*} acoustics $+$ genre/album-type multi-hot),
never a behavioral or target field. Each space was then scored under a
\emph{frozen} GRU readout (\texttt{gru-infonce-h256}: \texttt{arch=gru},
\texttt{loss=infonce}, \texttt{hidden=256}, one layer, dropout 0.1, 40 epochs,
lr \num{1e-3}, batch 128, $k{=}10$), so the item latent space is the only axis
that moves. The design bet was that the AE's equal-weight-per-block
reconstruction loss would \emph{preserve} the small acoustic/catalog blocks that
PCA's text-dominated global variance would starve; the null was that dim, not
the compressor, sets the ceiling. Every artifact passed an identical parity gate
(same \num{7154} sessions / \num{19402} items / split / cut), so the comparison
is apples-to-apples, and the paired-$\Delta$ is the per-session R@10 difference
against the in-batch AE-64 control.

\emph{Results.} The parity gate passed: the AE-64 control reproduced its
on-record R@10 \num{0.0957} $\in [0.081, 0.112]$, so the ranking is valid. All
six spaces trained cleanly (Table~\ref{tab:nexttrack-compression};
Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-compression}). \textbf{PCA-128}
(\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_song\_pca128}, artifact \texttt{seq-20260715-030509};
run \runid{run-20260715-042100-a1d9d}) is the single-GRU winner at R@10
\num{0.1146}, a paired-$\Delta$ of $+\num{0.0189}$ $[+0.0042, +0.0335]$ over the
AE-64 control --- a CI \emph{entirely above zero}, the only arm that clears the
control significantly --- and it also tops MRR (\num{0.0489}), artist@10
(\num{0.3326}) and artist\_mrr (\num{0.2644}). PCA-64 is the runner-up
(\num{0.1041}, paired-$\Delta$ straddling zero: a tie). The two 32-dim spaces
(AE-32 \num{0.0545}, PCA-32 \num{0.0412}) sit far below the 64-dim control, both
with paired-$\Delta$ CIs entirely below zero. The training-free content-kNN
probe (a pure cosine retrieval in each space, last column of the report) is flat
at $\sim$0.06 across all six spaces and does \emph{not} track the GRU ranking:
PCA-128's probe R@10 (\num{0.0636}) exactly ties the AE-64 control
(\num{0.0636}), and AE-32 tops the probe (\num{0.0720}) despite ranking
near-worst under the GRU.

(\num{0.173}, MRR \num{0.096}) on both metrics, with overlapping CIs. By the
decision rule this is not a board event: nothing was promoted, the AE-64
champion blend stands, and PCA-128 is carried forward only as the sanctioned
Phase-2 base space.

\emph{Interpretation.} Four things follow. First, \textbf{latent dimensionality
is the dominant lever and the compressor is secondary}: for PCA the trend is
monotone (\num{0.041} $\to$ \num{0.104} $\to$ \num{0.115} across dim 32/64/128),
and 32 dims starve the space regardless of method. Second, the design's
\textbf{acoustic-preservation hypothesis is refuted}: at generous dim the linear
PCA beats the nonlinear AE (PCA-128 $>$ AE-128, PCA-64 $\ge$ AE-64), and although
AE-32 preserves the numeric/acoustic blocks near-perfectly it ranks near the
bottom --- so acoustic preservation is irrelevant to next-track retrieval. What
helps is a \emph{variance-ordered, decorrelated} target geometry (PCA-128
captures cumulative EVR \num{0.982}) that the GRU finds easier to regress into
than the AE's entangled equal-weight latents; PCA's global-variance ordering is
a feature here, not the liability the hypothesis assumed. Third, the AE's edge
is a purely \emph{low-capacity} phenomenon (AE-32 $>$ PCA-32): the nonlinear
bottleneck packs more usable structure into 32 dims, but the advantage
evaporates and reverses once dim is generous. Fourth, the flat content-kNN probe
that fails to track the GRU ranking is a \emph{flagged divergence}: the
intrinsic retrieval richness of these spaces is similar, so PCA-128's gain comes
from the \emph{learnability} of its target geometry for the trained GRU, not
from a demonstrably richer space in the absolute --- which is exactly why the
decision rule flagged rather than auto-promoted it. The load-bearing caution for
downstream work is the blend result: a clearly stronger GRU leg (\num{0.1146}
vs \num{0.096}) bought \emph{nothing} at the blend, because the Markov leg is
representation-independent and the extra tracks the better GRU recovers evidently
overlap what Markov already supplies. \textbf{Representation gains measured on
the single GRU do not automatically translate to the production blend}, so the
compression axis is genuine progress for the single model but essentially spent
for the blend. \emph{Source:
\texttt{2026-07-15-nexttrack-compression-representation-bakeoff.md}.}


\section{Next-track Phase 1b/2: the dimension ceiling, the model-family sweep, and the R+M+C champion}
\label{sec:nexttrack-phase2}
The compression bake-off (Section~\ref{sec:nexttrack-compression}) found latent
\emph{dimension} to be the dominant lever and left PCA still climbing at 128.
Two follow-on campaigns located the dimension ceiling, then swept the model
family on the chosen spaces --- and, in the process, crowned a new
best-on-record row.

\subsection{The compression dimension ceiling: PCA peaks at 192}
\label{sec:nexttrack-dim}
\emph{Rationale.} With dimension established as the lever, this leg extended the
PCA sweep upward (PCA-192, PCA-256) to locate the real optimum before Phase 2
committed a base space, and added AE-192 to test whether PCA $\ge$ AE holds at
higher dimension. The protocol was identical: all reps fit from the leak-safe
\dsid{spotify\_tracks\_content} source, scored under the \emph{same} frozen
\texttt{gru-infonce-h256} readout, each new sequence artifact passing the exact
parity gate; the reference is PCA-128 (\runid{run-20260715-042100-a1d9d}), the
Phase-1 winner, and the paired per-session $\Delta$ is measured against it.

\emph{Results.} The PCA R@10-versus-dimension curve \textbf{peaks at
$\sim$192} (R@10 \num{0.1230}) and \emph{turns over} by 256 (\num{0.1167}); it
was not still climbing. The full trend is
$32\!\to\!\num{0.041}$, $64\!\to\!\num{0.104}$, $128\!\to\!\num{0.115}$,
$\mathbf{192\!\to\!\num{0.123}}$, $256\!\to\!\num{0.117}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-dim}). PCA-192 is the point-optimum, but its paired
$\Delta$ versus PCA-128 is $+\num{0.0084}$ $[-0.0007, +0.0189]$ --- it misses
the ``CI entirely above zero'' bar by \num{0.0007}, a whisker --- so by the
mechanical rule \textbf{PCA-128 stands as the Phase-2 base}, with PCA-192
flagged as the confirm-and-promote candidate. PCA $\ge$ AE holds strongly and
the gap is widest at 192 (PCA-192 \num{0.123} versus AE-192 \num{0.098}); the AE
plateaus flat at $\sim$0.09--0.10 from dim 64 upward.

\emph{Interpretation.} Extra variance past $\sim$192 (PCA-256 captures
cumulative EVR \num{0.997}) is low-signal / noise dimensions the GRU cannot
exploit and that slightly dilute the target geometry. The AE's flat plateau
reconfirms the bake-off's refuted hypothesis at higher dimension: its
equal-weight reconstruction objective keeps spending capacity on the tiny
numeric/acoustic blocks (reconstruction $R^2 \approx 0.999$ at 192) rather than
the high-variance text/catalog structure the GRU needs. As before, the
training-free content-kNN probe stays flat ($\sim$0.064--0.070) and does not
reproduce the GRU's peak, so the dimension optimum is about target
\emph{learnability}, not intrinsic retrieval richness.
\emph{Source: \texttt{2026-07-15b-nexttrack-compression-dim-extension.md}.}

\subsection{The model-family sweep and the new champion, R+M+C on PCA-192}
\label{sec:nexttrack-modelsweep}
\emph{Rationale.} With the compression axis settled (PCA-128 base, PCA-192
candidate), this campaign swept the model \emph{family} on the chosen spaces:
does a different recurrent cell (LSTM), a non-recurrent pooled MLP (ANN), or a
\emph{learned} XGB stacker beat the fixed GRU$\times$Markov z-blend --- and does
adding the content-kNN third leg (R+M+C) on the \emph{rich} PCA space finally
beat the champion? A frozen held config ran across all arms; only the model
family and the representation varied, and the server was serialized
(\texttt{-{}-max-runs 1}) to avoid the thread-oversubscription trap.

\emph{Results.} A \textbf{new best-on-record row}: the \textbf{R+M+C z-blend on
PCA-192} (GRU $\times$ train-only Markov $\times$ content-kNN, equal thirds)
reaches R@10 \num{0.1859} $[0.166, 0.207]$, MRR \num{0.1025}, a paired $\Delta$
over the prior champion of $+\num{0.0133}$ $[+0.0007, +0.0259]$ --- entirely
above zero, with no MRR regression (Table~\ref{tab:nexttrack-phase2};
Figure~\ref{fig:nexttrack-phase2}). The content leg is genuinely additive on the
rich space, beating same-space R+M by $+\num{0.0154}$ (PCA-128) / $+\num{0.0147}$
(PCA-192), both CIs above zero --- \emph{stronger} than the AE-64 R+M+C that
only tied (Section~\ref{sec:nexttrack-graded}), because the richer, higher-EVR
space makes the content leg pay off. The learned \textbf{XGB stacker badly
underperforms} the fixed z-blend (\num{0.080}--\num{0.087}, losing even to its
own single GRU base leg), reconfirming the fusion campaign; the \textbf{LSTM is
the best single model} (PCA-128 \num{0.1272}, paired $\Delta$ over the GRU
$+\num{0.0126}$, CI above zero) but far below the blends; and the Phase-2 single
base \textbf{stays PCA-128} (GRU-192's edge still grazes zero).

\emph{The crown, and its two honest caveats.} The board-event candidate was
confirmed by reproducing the GRU-192 leg five times (the original plus four
reruns) and recomputing R+M+C each time; the paired $\Delta$ over the prior
champion was $+\num{0.0133}$ $[+0.0007, +0.0259]$ \emph{every} rerun, with the
MRR lead intact, so R+M+C on PCA-192 was recorded as the new champion row. Two
caveats travel with the crown and must not be omitted. First, \textbf{the
reproduction spread was exactly zero} because \texttt{seq-nexttrack} training is
deterministic on this CPU --- all five GRU-192 checkpoints were byte-identical
--- so the confirm verified \emph{determinism, not seed-robustness}; the only
uncertainty quantified is the single-split bootstrap. Second, \textbf{the paired
$\Delta$ lower bound is $+\num{0.0007}$}: the crown clears zero by a whisker on a
single split. The crown is real by the pre-agreed criterion, but the margin over
the prior champion is thin, and hardening it needs a second leak-free test split
(or seed-varied legs once the blend is generalized to three legs).

\emph{Interpretation.} The compression-plus-model-family thread is otherwise
closed: PCA-128 is the single-model base, the LSTM is the best single model, the
learned stacker is dead as a combiner, and the fixed z-blend is the combiner of
record. The load-bearing result is that \emph{content is the one leg that adds
genuinely new ranking signal, and it pays off more on the higher-EVR PCA
space} --- the exception to the standing rule that single-model / representation
gains do not carry to the blend (the R+M pair on the rich PCA spaces only ties
the AE-64 champion). The productization gap remains: the champion is train-only
and off-server, so the prior champion R+M on AE-64 stays the deployable anchor
until the three-leg blend is registered with a \texttt{predict} subcommand.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Thirteen campaigns (Jun--Jul 2026) on the pivoted rotation target converge
on a single story, with one late-breaking caveat (point 12). (1) Across two feature campaigns and three model-family scans (two
corpora), every dense modality and every dense distance/margin/dense-net family
loses to categorical identity one-hots read by gradient-boosted trees --- the
song-AE latent dilutes (Section~\ref{sec:ae-blend}), kernel SVM is
worse-than-mean (Section~\ref{sec:svm}), and on the new full-64-d corpus the two
tree families lead while the dense pyramid-MLP is last
(Section~\ref{sec:bakeoff-p64}). (2) The choice of metric is load-bearing on a
binary target: MAE is degenerate (a two-level $\epsilon$-tube predictor posts a
deceptively low MAE while being worse than the mean), and a grid-by-MAE blend
even mis-selects such a predictor; the honest classification metrics (AUC,
log-loss, Brier) restore a fair comparison (Section~\ref{sec:auc}). (3) The
campaigns delivered two measured noise bands --- $2\sigma=0.0086$ MAE and
$2\sigma=0.0126$ AUC --- and the project's first registered, promotable rotation
champion, \texttt{xgboost-classifier-rotation} at AUC 0.7429 on DS-META, plus a
saved new-corpus xgboost definition at AUC 0.7204. (4) The dense neural net was
diagnosed in full (Section~\ref{sec:anns}): an architecture scan refuted the
budget hypothesis (every geometry self-stops at 73--100 iterations and the
converged net reproduces the under-trained AUC to all digits), so the binding
constraint is the raw one-hot \emph{representation}, not optimization --- and the
net's worse-than-random log-loss beside a genuine AUC marks it as confidently
miscalibrated, not merely weak. (5) That diagnosis was then tested directly: the
Rust/\texttt{burn} \texttt{burn-deep-classifier}, which feeds the identity
one-hots \emph{raw}, repaired the calibration completely (log-loss
$2.0\to\sim 0.62$, into the tree regime) and lifted AUC $\sim$2 bands to
\num{0.7038} (Section~\ref{sec:anns-burn}) --- confirming the pathology was a
representation artifact, not an intrinsic property of nets. But the gap to the
trees only \emph{narrowed} (from $\sim$3.3 bands to $\sim$1), it did not close:
the net still lands below \texttt{catboost} and \texttt{xgboost}, the plain
\texttt{mlp} beat both the embedding and wide-deep topologies (entity embeddings
are starved at the corpus's low identity cardinality), and nothing cleared the
$\sim$0.71 save bar. (6) That standing lever was then pulled. A paired re-run on a
\emph{high-vocab} matrix --- identical to the 60/40 dataset but with the artist
cap raised $60\to300$ and genre $40\to120$ --- \emph{flipped} the topology
ranking (Section~\ref{sec:anns-highvocab}): \texttt{embeddings}, starved and
last on the 60/40 matrix, now \emph{beats} \texttt{mlp} (a $+0.0130$ swing that
reverses the prior result and confirms the starvation hypothesis directionally),
and the saved net reaches the in-dataset tree tier at AUC \num{0.7192} (within
$\sim$1 band of \texttt{catboost}) --- the first ANN to clear the rotation save
bar, saved as \texttt{burn-deep-embeddings-highvocab}. The convergent finding
still stands at the top (\texttt{xgboost} leads every matrix, and higher
resolution lifts \emph{every} family, not just the embedding), but the
ANN-pitfalls thread is now closed: a representation-aware net that consumes raw
one-hots is calibrated in the tree regime, and given enough identity resolution
its embedding topology reaches the tree tier --- identity \emph{resolution} was
the lever all along.
(7) A final probe then tested the inverse of the lever --- whether a learned representation \emph{derived from} the tree, rather than fed alongside it, could beat the bare tree --- and refuted it: the Facebook GBDT$\to$LR trick generalized to GBDT$\to$MLP, with a fresh head trained on the frozen champion's one-hot leaf indices, caps \num{1.8}--\num{2.4} bands below the bare xgboost champion (best head AUC \num{0.6978}) and is badly mis-calibrated on every head, because the leaf one-hots are a strictly lossier view of a tree whose additive leaf-value scoring already weights the leaves optimally (Section~\ref{sec:leaf-head}). The dense head cannot even recover the tree that generated its features --- the seventh convergent confirmation that identity resolution, not new heads or representations layered on the trees, is the durable lever.
(8) Finally, the standing identity-resolution lever was mapped to its edge: a vocab-cap scan on the bare GBDT --- frozen champion config, only \texttt{vocab\_top\_n} moved --- shows the lever is \emph{largely spent} (Section~\ref{sec:vocab-cap}). The first cardinality jump ($60/40\to300/120$) carries the gain; past 300/120 every step is sub-band, the curve plateaus inside the noise envelope, xgboost reaches only \num{0.7349} at 1000/300 ($+0.005$, $\sim$0.4 band over the anchor, so no save fired), and \texttt{catboost} tracks it --- two tree families agreeing the marginal high-cardinality columns carry little independent signal. Not saturated and not reversed, but diminishing: raw cap height is no longer the durable lever, and future identity work should target resolution \emph{quality} (follower/popularity interactions, genre hierarchy) rather than bigger caps --- with the full-vocabulary point (6633 cols) still unmeasured against a host memory ceiling.

(9) A tenth campaign characterized a \emph{second} representation-aware net and reached the same ceiling: the two-phase \texttt{burn-ae-classifier}, which pretrains an autoencoder over the assembled feature vector and transplants the encoder into a classifier, is flat across 13 capacity/head configs (a \num{0.0054}-wide strip inside the band), ties the plain \texttt{burn-deep} \texttt{mlp}, and stays $\sim$1.8 bands below the tree champion; freezing the MSE-pretrained encoder under a deep head is a documented collapse mode (Section~\ref{sec:anns-ae}). The unsupervised reconstruction latent is task-agnostic --- a third confirmation that a learned representation downstream of the identity one-hots buys no ranking power the trees lack.
(10) Two interpretability campaigns opened the nets with a per-model sparse autoencoder (Sections~\ref{sec:anns-sae}, \ref{sec:anns-capacity}): at equal AUC, a tapering pyramid \emph{consolidates} its interpretable concepts into a compressed bottleneck while a uniform net keeps a broader set, and at high vocab an AUC-tied embeddings/mlp pair captures the \emph{same} $\sim$0.14-decodable signal with different concept allocation --- ``same signal, different layout,'' invisible to the leaderboard.
(11) A capacity scan on the high-vocab matrix confirmed the dense net is at its ceiling there: neither widening nor deepening beats the $[128,64]$ control by a band, a third embedding layer \emph{hurts} ($-\num{0.0152}$, dilution not starvation), and the best \texttt{mlp} ties the best \texttt{embeddings} --- capacity, not the embeddings mechanism, was the lever, and it is spent (Section~\ref{sec:anns-capacity}).
(12) Finally, the honest forward question was asked --- and it reframes the whole leaderboard. A first-class chronological (train-earlier / test-later) split drops the champion from AUC \num{0.735} to \num{0.619}, a $\sim$0.11 collapse that is \emph{universal} across xgboost, catboost and logistic and is genuine taste non-stationarity, not right-censoring; a walk-forward read lands at \num{0.59}--\num{0.67} every year, never near the random baseline (Section~\ref{sec:drift}). Every on-record AUC is therefore an upper bound on same-era ranking; real forward ``will I replay a song I discover next'' skill is $\sim$0.62--0.63. The mechanism is identity churn --- unseen future artists fall into \texttt{\_\_other\_\_} and the model must lean on the weaker $\sim$0.60 intrinsic floor --- which is precisely why higher vocab caps and bigger nets, both same-era memorization levers, do not transfer forward. This does not overturn any random-split number; it clarifies what they measure and makes richer generalizable intrinsic signal the only remaining lever for forward performance.

(13) A parallel line of work --- \emph{next-track} sequence recommendation, whose task and metrics are unrelated to the rotation boards above (Sections~\ref{sec:nexttrack}--\ref{sec:nexttrack-phase2}) --- was consolidated across seven campaigns. A session GRU alone only \emph{ties} the first-order Markov co-listening bar and loses on MRR, and neither loss-tuning nor any topology (depth/width/residual/direction) beats it. But a \emph{rank-fusion} R+M z-blend (GRU $\times$ Markov) beats the bar decisively ($+61\%$ R@10, three-seed confirmed) because the two legs rank \emph{different} correct tracks; a learned XGB stacker underperforms the fixed z-blend and adding redundant neural legs does not help --- the win is signal diversity, not model count. The one genuinely additive new signal is \emph{content}: an item-representation sweep found latent \emph{dimension} (not the compressor) is the lever and peaks at PCA-192, and on that rich space a three-leg R+M+C z-blend is the current best-on-record row at R@10 \num{0.1859}, MRR \num{0.1025} (paired $\Delta$ over the prior champion $+\num{0.0133}$, CI$>$0). Two caveats bound the crown: the reproduction confirm had zero spread because \texttt{seq-nexttrack} is deterministic on this CPU (so it verified determinism, not seed-robustness), and the paired-$\Delta$ lower bound clears zero by only $+\num{0.0007}$ on a single split. Every next-track ranking predictor is train-only and off-server, so the crown is a best-on-record \emph{row}, not a promoted model, and the prior champion R+M on AE-64 remains the productization anchor.
