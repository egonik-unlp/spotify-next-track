How it works

You seed a mood; a sequential neural net walks your own library through it. Below is the actual machinery — the model, the space it reasons in, the generation loop, and how a song you don't own gets pulled into that space.

1 · The model — an anti-eager next-track GRU

The engine is seq-nexttrack, a single-layer GRU from the lab's next-track experiments. It doesn't classify over a track vocabulary; it regresses the next track's embedding. Given the sequence of latents played so far it emits a predicted next-latent, trained with a cosine / in-batch InfoNCE objective against the true next track.

Left alone, a next-track model trained on real listening is eager: people play albums front-to-back, so the cheapest way to be "right" is to predict the rest of the current album. We train against that with an anti-eager penalty (eager_beta = 0.1): a term of the form β · relu(cos(pred, x_current) − margin) that punishes the prediction for being a near-copy of the track currently playing. The result is a model that stays in the mood without parroting the record — a GRU (short memory) beats an LSTM here precisely because the goal is coherent drift, not long-range album structure.

2 · The space — a 192-d content latent

Both the GRU's input and its retrieval target live in one space: PCA-192 over a rich per-track feature vector — a 384-d multilingual text embedding of the track's metadata (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2) concatenated with normalized numeric, acoustic, and one-hot categorical features, projected to 192 principal components (~99% variance retained). Every track in the catalog is one unit-norm point here; in the browser they're shipped as int16-quantized latents (latents.i16) and dequantized on load.

3 · The generation loop

Playback is autoregressive:

seq ← seed latents
loop:
  p    ← GRU(seq)                       # predicted next-latent, L2-normalized
  cand ← argmax over UNPLAYED library tracks of  cos(latent, p) − β·[same artist]
  seq  ← seq ⧺ latent(cand)             # feed the choice back in
  emit cand

Two constraints shape the walk. Retrieval is restricted to your library (the discovery pool is seed-only), so the journey is always songs you own. And a hard per-artist cap bounds how many tracks one artist may take — the "shape" control. This used to be a soft penalty subtracted from the cosine score, but measurement showed that knob was effectively binary: at zero you got artist blocks (runs of up to 13 tracks), and at any setting above 0.25 every 30-step walk came back with 30 different artists. There was no middle. A cap reaches what the slider could not — Explore (1 per artist) crosses your library, Continue (3) follows the artist pairings you actually make — and, unlike a penalty, it cannot be outscored by a confident model. Because the chosen latent is fed back in, the context grows and the mood can evolve smoothly rather than snapping around a fixed seed centroid.

4 · Cold-start — seeds you don't own

Seed tracks already in the catalog map straight to their baked latent. A seed that isn't — an arbitrary track in a pasted playlist — has to be placed into the same PCA-192 space with no training example. We can't run the original MiniLM featurizer in a Worker, so we learn a bridge:

  • The Worker fetches the track's Spotify metadata + ReccoBeats acoustic features and builds the same contentDoc text used at training time.
  • Workers AI embeds that doc with @cf/baai/bge-m3 (1024-d).
  • A small MLP — [bge-m3(1024) ⊕ numeric ⊕ acoustic ⊕ missing-flags ⊕ genre-multihot ⊕ album-onehot] → 256 → 192, then L2-normalize — maps that to a PCA-192 latent. It's baked offline (a cosine-loss fit over the corpus) and shipped as projector.bin, run in a Rust→WASM core inside the Worker.

The projector reconstructs a track's true PCA-192 latent at ~0.995 cosine (self-recall@1 ≈ 0.999 on a held sample), so a cold-started seed lands essentially where it belongs, and the GRU can walk from it.

5 · Runtime & architecture

The GRU is exported to ONNX and runs entirely in your browser via onnxruntime-web (WASM) — retrieval is a plain matrix–vector product over the in-memory latents, so generation is instant and offline after load. The backend is a Cloudflare Worker (TypeScript) that serves the static app and does the two things a page can't: expand a Spotify playlist/album/track link (client-credentials) and cold-start unknown seeds (Workers AI + the Rust projector). Seeding itself is a library navigator: sign in and browse your own playlists, saved albums, liked songs, top/recent tracks and Spotify-wide search, dropping whatever you like into a queue. On launch the queue is expanded (playlists and albums become their tracks — optionally interspersed round-robin rather than block after block), deduped, capped, and the whole thing becomes the GRU's seed sequence; anything the model has never seen is cold-started on the way in. "Save to Spotify" is a browser-side PKCE flow that creates a real playlist from the first N stops. Playback is 30-second previews via Spotify's iframe API.

6 · A known bias

Because retrieval favours the nearest neighbour and the anti-artist term rewards dense clusters of distinct artists, the walk has a mild pull toward sonically homogeneous regions (steady-tempo electronic / house-family sits in one such cluster) — measured at roughly 1.5–1.6× over-representation versus a library's own genre mix. It is not a universal attractor: seeds in rock, metal, classical, jazz, ambient, etc. stay put. Turning the variety slider up counters the lingering.
